# Independent paper originality review — cand-d v5, r1

Date: 2026-11-20. Scope: G7/G8 only, exploratory, not the official gate run.

**Finding: one high cell, neither fictional nor a whole-robot match.** The Beckhoff arm-module patent (Y12, D2) shows a centering pin/receptacle, complementary Hirth face gearings and a fastening ring drawing the faces together. In my judgement this is the same alignment, preload and torque-transfer architecture at the interface. It does not share the lock actuator, silhouette, body joint, assembly behaviour or visual design. Q61 does not require five dimensions to be different, nor does a single non-F1 high fail G7. The matrix has 91 rows (455 cells), including 54 moderate cells. No high is against F1/F2.

### Object and independence

I reviewed the exact declaration bytes at 2ac0a54, not a drawing inferred from v4. Its SHA-256 matches the handoff and dossier. I did not design or edit cand-d. Inputs: AGENTS.md; the cloud handoff; Q38, Q61, Q62, Q64–65, Q85 and Q93; the registered Gate 1 v1 criteria, protocol §9, SEL-005/006/009; the Q61 corpus; cand-b v1 for the Family B cross-check; v5 declaration and dossier; v4 declaration and originality r1 for prior leads and change identification only. The generation, joint and coupler calculations were run without rewriting inputs. Their outputs locate features, not evidence of physical qualification.

- **D1 geometry:** asymmetric long PA12 head (33.8 mm), short tail (8.8 mm), 25.4 × 24 mm section; visible hinge gap, tail pilot/stud and flanged head mouth. Not two equal halves. Actual mating-plane pitch is 36.4 − (−11.6) = **48.0 mm**, not the 47.2 mm shorthand in the previous review.
- **D2 coupling:** one allowed roll, offset axis (y,z) = (3.3,1.0) mm. Opposed lugs enter slots; a geared sleeve rotates, draws at 20° then seats at 3°. Axial force is retained at the cam flanks, not by magnetic attraction or a spring; two mating rings, each with 36 radial face teeth, carry twist/shear. Release reverses the motor. These are paper descriptions, not a G6 verdict.
- **D3/D4 movement:** one vertical pin between segments; small spur-sector rotation (working ±15°). A second motor turns the sleeve, **not** another body joint. Initially engaged, head-to-tail, no designed solo approach, no claimed autonomous docking gait, looping, branching or lattice metamorphosis. External presentation is required. Distinguish geometric ability to form a chain from actually moving it.
- **D5 appearance:** closed shell segments, pronounced asymmetry, stud, circular serrated face and mounting lobes, rather than a styled fictional character. PA12, 304 and the coated head teeth describe materials; they do not specify a shell colour, trim, paint, lighting or final render. D5 is a judgement of the declared cues only; do not treat missing visuals as final visual clearance.

**What changed vs v4 (Q93).** The tighter, longer pilot and PTFE film on the head tooth flanks refine alignment. The sleeve is C95400 aluminium bronze, closing uses 1.5 V average, release has a cell-voltage condition, and the revision adds motor clip/bond, two sense resistors and clearance at the hinge. The pilot and coated teeth matter to D2 and possibly final D5. None turns the lock into an SMA latch, changes the Hirth torque path to face friction, or creates a new external joint. I used neither the author's self-check nor the previous verdict as evidence, and did not adopt the v4 reviewer's practice of capping an unread close call at low.

### Close-call reading notes

The dimensions beside each name identify the actual primary evidence for **every moderate/high comparison**. Where a row covers a lineage, primary evidence is specific to the mechanism named, not blanket evidence for all generations.

- **C1/C2/C3, D1/D3:** Murata et al., *M-TRAN*, “M-TRAN Module” and Figs. 1–3; Kamimura et al., *Automatic Locomotion Design…*, system description and Figs. 1–2. I read the semicylinder/link, two independent motors and magnetic-surface passages of the authors’ papers via indexed PDF text. Kurokawa et al., author slides “M-TRAN module (lattice-oriented design)” and “New M-TRAN Hardware”, show two rounded blocks, parallel rotary axes, two 180° rotations and the changed connector. The exact prior colour or magnet arrangement is **not** assumed for III.
- **C4/C6/C17, D2:** ATRON team’s drive description: three hooks, a screw drive not backdrivable when extended. Roombots: the [EPFL group’s connector paper](https://vigir.missouri.edu/~gdesouza/Research/Conference_CDs/IEEE_ICRA_2008/Modular%20Robotics%20Design/An%20active%20connection%20mechanism%20for%20modular%20self-reconfigurab.pdf) specifies fourfold symmetry, fully retractable latches and “no-energy consumption in the locked state”; distinguish this ACM design from later hardware on the project page. HiGen paper: shrouded hooks, motor/gearbox friction, connection maintained after power removal, quarter-turn orientation intervals. None draws two axial lugs against a serrated face.
- **C12, D1–D4; C13, D1/D3; C14, D3/D4; C20, D4:** PolyBot team: rectangular-prism 1-DOF segment and G2 pin/latch-plate/SMA assembly, with chain, loop and node structures. SuperBot paper §III and Fig. 2: two linked equal cubes, rotating central part, yaw/pitch/roll. CKBot laboratory page (“CKbot” and “U-Bar and L7”) identifies it as a chain-style, manually reconfigurable robot and distinguishes two single-axis module builds. Casal & Yim Figs. 2–3 explicitly depict PolyPod closed-chain reconfiguration; Yim’s [Stanford dissertation](http://i.stanford.edu/pub/cstr/reports/cs/tr/95/1536/CS-TR-95-1536.pdf) supplies segment/node context. PolyPod and PolyBot are related, not the same module.
- **Q64-C21, D1–D4:** USC/ISI second CONRO prototype, “Passive Connector”, “Motor Housing” and “Active Connector”: three pinned faces on a cube, two servo axes, spring-returned latch retracted by SMA. A series of such modules can grow into other topologies; see also Casal & Yim’s closed-chain class discussion. This is not a claim that every CONRO iteration uses the same latch.
- **Y1–Y6, D2:** Davey et al. describe manually twisting faces then engaging a compliant locking dowel. Zhang et al., §2/Fig. 4, describe insertion, rotation and spring-back between teeth. Mrázek & Barnat describe a motor-rotated clip translating in a helical slot, with hooks overlapping. The WPI authors’ poster explicitly labels the bayonet/quarter-turn candidate; evidence extends to that concept only, not its preload or realized strength. Kernbach/Liedke et al., CoBoLD Fig. 2, describe four conical spring-loaded bolts and hooks held by a worm. Dettmann et al., RIMRES EMI “Mechanical Structure and Latch Mechanism”, Fig. 4, describe two spindle-driven braces enclosing a conical passive pole; I obtained the [DFKI mirror](https://www-live.dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/5307_110204_Heterogeneous_Modules_with_a_Homogeneous_Electromechanical_Interface_in_Multi-Module_Systems_for_Space_Exploration_ICRA_Dettmann.pdf) after the regular host blocked raw transfers.
- **Y10/Y11, D2:** MODUR Fig. 2(c) and adjacent text: screw pushes a nut-bearing hook toward its counterpart before rotation locks the hooks. Maskur et al. Fig. 3 describes a servo cam moving a lock-pin linearly. These are rotary-to-linear locking mechanisms, not matching toothed tension interfaces.
- **Y12, D2 (high) and D4 (moderate):** Beckhoff’s detailed description explicitly says the internal thread is screwed onto 301, “pressing the second connection device 40 onto the first connection device 30”; face gearings 302/402 then engage “in a torque-locking manner”; the pin/receptacle also encode mating. The product page confirms the thread and Hirth concept and the variety of passive link geometries. **What is kept:** self-centering teeth drawn tight with a rotating member; do not present that arrangement as new. **What differs:** stud lugs follow slots inside a motorized sleeve; head film and shallow holding ramp are not a different principle for carrying twist. The independent small hinge, one-roll homogeneous chain, lack of wired/pneumatic interface and asymmetric shells keep D1/D3/D4/D5 below high. No patent scope or infringement opinion.
- **Y13, D2/D3/D4; Y14, D2:** Xerox detailed description Figs. 1–3, 6–10: indexed tooth plates; per-module gear/actuator at a pivot; end-to-end snake versus other configurations. This reinforces C12, not another independent lineage. Belke, EPFL thesis 7300, ch. 4 Figs. 4.1–4.4 and torque-threshold discussion (corresponding article DOI [10.1109/TMECH.2019.2907802](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMECH.2019.2907802)): front teeth resemble a Hirth pair but the planar tooth faces differ; axial slider and overload disengagement matter more than tooth appearance. I read the author’s thesis, not the access-blocked IEEE PDF.
- **Y15/Y16/Y18/Y20/Y21, D2:** PetLock §3.2 analyses the self-locking condition of pins and pyramid. Reach describes the annular protrusions on the mating members and a threaded lock nut. KIST’s 9,853,386 B1 and [10,160,121 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10160121B2/en) both describe ring threads and wing threads around terminal substrates. Ben-Tzvi describes clamping a hexagonal shaft neck so it cannot turn inside the clamp while the attached bodies can articulate; unlike a rigid Hirth dock. Khoshnevis describes a rotating hollow cylinder with internal spiral channel guiding a claw. These are patent embodiments read in the specifications, not evidence every claimed version was built.
- **Y24a, D3/D4; Y24c, D3; InnoSnake, D3:** ChainFORM design section and hinge/bracket/servo figures identify a linear actuated interface with position, LEDs and sensing. TAMS’s PMR team describes the magnetic interface, RC servos and automatic pitch/yaw topology recognition. Kowatec lists sixteen servos and sidewinding/zig-zag/other gaits for its assembled kit. Kin’s sector in each unit is similar as repeated actuation, not a claim of equivalent locomotion.

US 10,482,786 is unrelated to that DOI: its detailed description shows independent driven hubs mounted in a D-shaped housing.
- **Additional mechanisms, D2:** Davey/Kwok/Yim, original SMORES, Fig. 4 and key discussion: magnets hold in tension, keys resist torsion/shear. Shen et al., SINGO: one circular gear with spiral tracks moves four jaws along rails; connects without continuous power.
