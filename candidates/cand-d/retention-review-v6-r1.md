**Reverse-torque cross-check.** For axial lead q, contact radius r and friction u, lowering gives tangential/axial force (u−q/r)/(1+u q/r); raising changes both signs. For the single loaded tip, include the moment rA of its axial force A as a journal couple across 7.44, not just the tangential force at the lug. With the interrupted journal radii, dry front thrust at 4.4 and u up to 0.23, the maximum lowering arm is 3.4451. The unilateral dog itself consumes 0.2031 of available torque. Thus (3.4451A+0.25)/(1−0.2031) gives the table values. Using the nominal unloaded spring instead, splitting A in half, or using 147.10 at locked rotor as a moving rating answers a different question.

## The three alignment means and the transition between states

1. **Presented pilot and chamfer** (head flange, cartridge, tail stud). An external quasistatic push brings the Ø3.986..3.990 pilot into the Ø4.000..4.008 guide. The 0.30 nose and 0.40 entrance chamfers plus minimum radial clearance give 0.705 geometric nose allowance; only 0.376 of straight pilot leads at the lug/crest meeting. The slots interrupt the bore: at 2.600 insertion the two split stations span 4.375 (rock ≈0.321°); at first tooth contact the rear station is full, span 5.007 (≈0.266°). Passive normal forces can centre a *pushed* pilot. They do nothing across a free gap. No continued external levelling, self-propelled approach or measured 3D capture region is justified.
2. **Lug guide entries, rocker and face teeth** (tail stud, head flange, cartridge). The 0.50 V entry reacts roll; at ±1.4° presentation the nominal remaining allowance after tolerance is 0.320. The 1.60 bar/1.70 slots constrain clocking; the ±1.8° rocker travel exceeds the paired geometric need ≈0.899° in the 2.74×1.64 fork. The tooth flanks convert axial draw to clocking/centring, without a sensor, magnetic force or snap. The opposed-depth interpolation applies only while both tips bear. Roll needs 5.71 torque vs 0.65 adverse supplied in the paired model; combined draw available 0.389 less roll 1.298, pitch 1.446 and yaw 3.895 gives **−6.249 N**. Each axis consumes the same draw. Not proof the mechanism always stalls; no demonstrated trajectory through this corner.
3. **Mouth dwell, draw and shallow lock** (cartridge/rocker, head flange, servo, spline, cassette/braces and boost). External presentation at open precedes the 31.5° dwell, which gives 1.49° nominal worst-case bridge before axial rise and **zero** advance; its 1.66 N calculated reaction is not draw. The steep stage can draw an aligned free unit (0.255 N net in the paired model), then the shallow stage offers 0.389 N net. At the limits of the two-tip model, first touch is after transition and full seating spans 88.36..135.66°, inside the stop. Passive guides redirect force; only the head servo and wound spring supply closing work after presentation. On loss of power before shallow engagement, no passive completion or safe retention is shown. The mouth, a single edge, a jam, and an input angle at the close command are not interchangeable with a fully engaged face.

**Stage limits.** Two webs remain in the section model (0.346 circumferential minimum and 0.834 rear stock); not local stress or blend bounds. Input home 14°, close command 259° (actual window 255.5..262.5), reverse command 2°. Spring-rate window 0.0375..0.0481 N·mm/°, estimated seat torque 3.93..7.76, blocked-close ≈12.04 at 250.1° wind. Minimum live coil gap ≈0.0138; 1,940 MPa jam body stress near the static wire screen ≈1,997 MPa, not a repeated-cycle rating. With end play 0.127 and opposite-flank clearance 0.175 debited, open teeth clear by only 0.0274. That is a geometric check **if open is reached**, not a proof of powered extraction of the 6.50 pilot. The 2° input command extends past the calculated open dog position 6.4..9.6°; stop reaction and termination matter. Input-shaft sensing cannot tell full seat, open stop, or obstruction apart by position alone.

## Independent head release, drive reaction and stalls

The charged head unwinds the push-only spring. After lost motion the relieved metal dog drives the sleeve open via the 28T spline, not via the spring or a responsive tail. The keyed ABS case bears on fitted pads in a closed 304 cassette (welded cheeks and tie, sole, trapped ear and two side braces); the structure transmits reaction to the shell. At 19.64 brace spacing a 176.5 N·mm fault implies an order-of-9 N couple force, before local lever/load sharing; gauge tab, pads and fasteners, do not credit glue or arbitrary clamp friction. The M1.7 hub screw retains the driver, not the joining stud. Pull-assisted descent loads the **front** washer; on positive ejection the **rear** shoulder bears on the collar, hollow hardened cross-tube and housing. The Ø~1 soft keeper and Ø2.50 washers prevent dowel escape, not structural shear. The open stop reacts drive torque; it is not an axial support.

## Classification and evidence needed

**Decisive:** G6 requires bounded release; this frozen declaration expressly disclaims it. The independent upper-bound calculations explain why changing the Boolean alone would also be indefensible. **Unsupported rather than contradicted:** guaranteed simultaneous seating; a fixed rocker load split; minimum working torque and brake behaviour over actual voltage/temperature; safe one-sided steep ejection; recoverability after a jam. **Documentation correction:** the coupler script comment gives a 6.70-deep floor; the rear-drive drawing instead specifies x29.80 from x36.40, or **6.60**. I used the shallower floor above. Its 0.035 tip reserve survives that correction on paper.

A further version needs (1) a rated head-alone reversal envelope with actual load-sharing bounds or conservative single-tip loads, incl. mixed friction, both thrust faces, journal side load, tooth disengagement, slot withdrawal and a finite path through the steep region; (2) a tolerance/force trajectory from presented pose to both shallow flanks, with simultaneous orientation loads and elastic/contact limits, not just positive axial stroke; (3) evidence for the finish, spring, collar and case fits under load, and a way to distinguish open, seated and stalled before assigning success. Later capture/velocity sweeps, wear/fatigue, physical release and 3D benchmark tests remain later work, not additional Gate 1 criteria. The finding is specific to v6, not a rejection of friction wedges, this architecture or the 60 mm envelope.
