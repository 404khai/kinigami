## Retention, power loss and alignment

**Fully seated retention.** Tail crown → radial neck and axial stem → tail flange. On the head, tension bears on transverse keepers; compression bears on rear pads. Differential axial reactions at ring radius 4.65 carry pitch/yaw; the short straight cheeks at the three sites carry shear and roll/twist by bearing. These reactions return via guides, stops, cassette/flange, screw ears and captive metal nuts to shell piers/end plate and hinge. This assigns carriers without claiming six simultaneous contacts. There is a positive-backed zero-lift closed dwell in the proposed section: directly backing each seated keeper and rear stop can hold without servo torque. The plate/slide stock, seating of every follower, crown attachment and housing/ear bearing still need local limits at the quoted reactions. The drawing gives no finished minimum/maximum **tangential pole spacing** and insufficient loaded facet/root dimensions to show the six-axis assumptions at every corner. Gross stock does not set an allowable. In particular rear_drive.py charges fork/crosshead stock as 17-4, not a certified hardened 52100 counterface at every pole.

**Q85 and loss of power.** Transverse motion in the closed flat does no axial take-up work. The measured-by-dimensions 0.090–0.210 gap contradicts Q85's driven torque-limited axial take-up; Q99 expressly keeps Q85. Metal guides and the absence of a PA12 preload thread do not remove the gap. For a fully seated joint the dwell is a credible *principle* of passive capture with play, not a proof of all-load rigidity. If power disappears before closing, one lagging blade, a ball on an edge or a partially extended stripper stays a partial/unknown state: no passive completion or safe withdrawal has been shown. Q31 item 6 does not require every intermediate hinge angle to lock.

**Sole alignment means: geometric guide** (head-flange, head-cartridge, tail-stations). Under external quasistatic presentation, a ball pressed into a throat can react against a cheek; three separated cheek pairs constrain transverse translation and roll after play is taken up. Seated rear pads oppose axial overtravel and set pitch/yaw. They redirect contact forces without power, but straight walls cannot draw a free body across a gap, and a transverse flat cannot pull all three balls onto their rear pads. On reset the stripper **retreats** first; presentation with keepers clear and rear pads back is distinct from presenting against the fully extended stripper and assuming the unit follows it in. The latter would need an inward force. No continuing fixture, yaw correction, tail motor, attraction or snap is declared. At 50 g the correction screen adds W axial and 7.41 / 12.73 / 27.85 N·mm roll/pitch/yaw work-producing moment demands to the service components; a reaction bound with those terms is not a force source. Supported for already presented, compatible poles—not a tolerance path that corrects a mispresented neighbor. No capture window is claimed or required here.

**Guide load is not base drag.** The planar calculation amplifies an assumed base resistance that must already include slide-shoulder and ball rubbing plus asymmetric loading; it does not compute that input from the six-contact solution. Nor is 3 N·mm an evidenced maximum for all other drag. For example, pure service bending 50.8W at 50 g with zero net axial force admits ideal axial reactions of magnitudes 3.571, 1.786, 1.786 N and opposing signs. Rear pads then see 3.571 N total compression. If that thrust returns through a sliding barrel dwell during shutter motion, μrΣN at .23 and 5.38 is **4.42 N·mm on the inactive track alone**. This is an adverse load path to bound or exclude, not proof the actual torque is always this large. The guides also have to carry the external loads while the slides move. No load-free change of stage is justified.

**Work check.** At 50 g, 2.452 N across the 1.80 stroke is only 4.41 mJ ideal output work. At constant 49.033 N·mm the maximum mechanical input over 44° is 37.65 mJ. That comparison alone would conceal the guide losses. Independently integrating the stated one-sided Coulomb model, T(q)=3+5W·G(q)/(1−2μe/L−μG(q)/r), including the two 5° blends, gives **97.26 N·mm peak and 65.44 mJ input screen**. The cam supplies neither this torque nor this work at its credited rating. These are adverse sizing values, not a measured efficiency or a general impossibility theorem.

**Fault and state.** The 52.2 N·mm joint-fault substitution is separate from a blocked coupler drive. The 4.8/6 V catalogue stall torques (147/177 N·mm) are not working ratings, guaranteed clutches, or caps on externally applied thrust at zero lead. Shaft angle, load feedback, a timed cutoff, a cell divider and a stock allowance for sensing leaves cannot distinguish three balls behind three backed keepers from a bent crosshead or a jam. No dimensioned overload isolation, local blade/seat indication or recoverable post-stall reverse sequence is shown. A rejection is legitimate; declaring it a lock or release is not.

## Classification and evidence needed for a further version

**Contradictions:** the explicit false release bound vs protocol §8; clearance-only closure vs Q85. **Unsupported:** all-corner passive bearing and tolerance control, alignment outside direct compatible presentation, loss and strength bounds through edge transition, loaded positive stripping and post-jam detection/recovery. **Not errors:** geometric clearance existing without available work, or a conservative sufficient-force estimate exceeding a rating; neither proves a particular specimen will jam. Do not silently strengthen a conditional equilibrium bound into a completed mechanism test.

A further version would need (1) a Q85-consistent finite take-up and power-off load path with dimensioned tangential/axial gaps, actual crown, land, root and guide strengths; (2) a continuous force/travel/torque budget through first and last contact, including corner normals, concurrent guide/dwell drag, asymmetric pads and table loads within guaranteed head power; (3) a defined presentation/reset sequence and physical evidence distinguishing seated/open outputs from obstruction, with bounded response to loss of power. No credit here for an outward-free pickup, independent strippers, spring ejectors or any later design. Capture sweeps, cycle life and five-unit dynamics belong later. This result is specific to frozen v7, not a no-go for the 60 mm envelope.
