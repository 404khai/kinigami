# Cand-d v5 — independent retention and pre-alignment review, r1

2026-11-20 · G6 · exploratory paper judgement only

**Decision: the declared ejection reaction path cannot be assembled.** A collar with a 4.10 mm bore must be pressed onto a 7.00 mm OD tube. This nominal interference, not a tolerance corner or a prediction of wear, defeats the declared release means (CPL-009). The self-locking principle of a fully seated joint is credible. Separately, the seat window and release torque bound do not cover the corners claimed. This is not a finding that a corrected variant or the 60 mm envelope is infeasible.

## 3. Retention and release, by load

**Tension.** In a seated assembly the stud pulls on the two sleeve flanks; the sleeve bears forward through the PTFE thrust ring against the housing flange, then the screws and captive steel blocks load the head shell. On the other unit, the stud flange and two steel screws load the tail end plate. No energized motor, gear friction or face friction is needed to *hold*. At 1.223 N the least modelled preload still exceeds pure pull. Lubricated flank friction of 0.10 still exceeds the inner-end back-drive threshold 0.072. The PTFE film is on the teeth only; applying its 0.05 low end to the cam would invalidate this finding.

**Torsion and shear.** Opposed 45° tooth flanks transmit twist geometrically, without driving the sleeve, provided they are meshed. Pilot/slot lash exceeds tooth phase error. At 7.77 N·mm the ideal axial separating force nearly exhausts the minimum clamp; transverse shear of 0.306 N is modest alone. If a face gaps under combined load, the wedge must take extra axial pull and some teeth must remain overlapped. A 20 N increment divided by the assumed 20 kN/mm loop stiffness gives 0.001 mm, small against a nominal 0.39 mm tooth; this stiffness is provisional and not an angular stiffness or a guaranteed roll error under rocking.

**Bending and combined loading.** The opposing Hirth rim takes compression; beyond tipping, the Ø~4 pilot in the housing bore provides an out-of-plane couple, and the opposing radial lugs and the surviving rim provide the in-plane couple. These are passive carriers; the tabletop joint's ability to *hold a bend angle* unpowered is a different requirement and is not claimed. At 1.764 N preload either declared bending moment alone exceeds initial tipping, so the typical seated-face margins do not extend to the lower-bound motor. Simultaneous loads leave only ~0.42 N·mm tipping reserve at typical minimum. At lower preload, force paths still exist **if already locked**; angular backlash, tilt and actual contact length matter, not just the resulting nominal Hertz stress. The 52.2 N·mm fault likewise needs those secondary carriers. The nominal hinge has a Ø2 pin, 1.5-long bosses and separated ears/plates: 14.677/18.3+1.223/2=1.41 N per ear, about 0.47 MPa projected pin bearing. The notch restores continuity to the legs; see §5 for traceability.

**Bounded independent release — fail.** The head alone reverses its motor, pinion and sleeve; the outer slot flank pushes the stud out 1.4 mm. The open stop carries torque, not the rearward axial thrust. The only declared support for that thrust is the collar. In `scripts/gen_declaration.py:165–180,195–196`, the tube volume is calculated at OD 7.00 from x=28.4 to 35.6 (before slot cuts); the rear 0.4 is included explicitly. The collar occupies x=28.4..28.8, nominal annulus r=2.05..4.9. Its bore would have to grow from 4.10 to 7.00 mm merely to reach nominal contact. The tube slots do not remove its entire circumference. If instead the tube ends at the cartridge box boundary x=28.8, there is no axial engagement for a press fit. Neither reading yields the specified joint. No turned-down land, fastening feature, quantified interface or alternative load path is declared. A gear-to-collar bearing pressure under the provisional 0.806 N ejection load says nothing about collar-to-tube retention. This is a contradiction between two declared solid parts and the release sequence, not an objection to press fits in general.

**Independent release torque — unresolved even with a mount.** Allowing release only above 3.75 V and using the head's divider is a legitimate operating condition; the quoted 3.6 V is at the motor, not the unloaded cell. The same motor sets both preload and release torque, but it does *not* scale the externally imposed 3.274 N. If its linear torque constant is a fraction k of typical, the dry margin in the author's model is 34.830k / [1.656(13.499k + 3.274)]: 1.254 at k=1, 1.196 at 0.8, unity near 0.44. These are sensitivities, not measured specimen limits. The claim of cancellation is algebraically false. A joint bound with motor/voltage characteristics at both voltages and fixed external load is needed. The two specified motor rows are not guaranteed stall curves, nor is multiplication of independent best/worst motor guesses a valid substitute. A voltage-divider reading also cannot establish mechanical engagement.

## 4. Pre-alignment, by declared means

1. **Lateral guide — credible in a presented pose.** The 0.3 nose chamfer plus the 0.4 bore chamfer admit about ±0.70 mm versus ±0.60 mm of shell stack. At μ=0.8 a 45° surface converts positive axial push to lateral force at (1−μ)/(1+μ)=0.111, not a self-centering force at zero push. At the lug/crest meeting the cylinder leads the straight bore by 0.25 mm (0.90−0.65); adequate for the ±0.6 slot V entries at small tilt, not the 5 mm engagement used for final yaw. Passive geometry; the declared external 2.40 mm insertion is a boundary condition, not onboard locomotion. Q031 starts engaged; there is no measured capture window.
2. **Roll, pitch, yaw — conditional, not demonstrated at every corner.** Opposed lug V entries, 0.6 at r=6.2, give about ±5.5° roll acceptance. Their straight slots index the teeth inside one 10° pitch. These guides and the 36 face teeth need no powered sensor; tooth ramps align under supplied draw and do not provide energy themselves. Q93's PTFE and increased draw resolve the v4 *lock-stage-only* roll/pitch shortfall: even a 2/π projected force factor as in v4 raises the pitch need only to 0.33 N. But the cam is not necessarily on that stage at first tooth contact. At shallow stack 3.5185 and 1.4° relative roll, an outer 45° flank at r=6.3 can meet at about 3.5185−6.3×0.02443=3.365, before the 3.40 transition (v4 review uses the same roll-residual model). At 20°, lower-bound dry draw is 3.870/3.960=0.977 N, not 1.764. At the declared mean tooth arm it gives 2.90 N·mm versus 4.11 needed to tip about the far foot. Locally changing contact radius, moving the other unit, or first levelling a different axis could help; no trajectory/force balance to the transition is shown. This is an unsupported guarantee, not a proof of inevitable jam.
3. **Axial closing / seating — conditional.** A powered 20° cam can draw one aligned free unit against table friction (1.21 N·mm vs 3.87 available); the 3° cam wedges passively *after* seating. Neither the external initial push nor the 1.5 V motor stall is an unpowered axial actuator. The yaw estimate is atan(0.060/5)=0.688° for a straight, fully supporting bore. Even granting it, v5 counts only ±4.875×0.012=0.0585 of lug-depth skew. A tilted tooth ring contacting at its surviving edge can hold the pitch planes apart at the axis by another 5.865×0.012=0.0704. At shallow stack the shallower lug could be at 3.5185−(5.865+4.875)×0.012=3.3896, short of 3.40. Which tooth contacts and whether yaw relaxes under draw must be established; stacking nominal *seated parallel* geometry with only lug skew is not a proof. The tube's axial slots interrupt its bore in the lug direction, so 5 mm of effective bearing at the assumed diametral clearance also needs checking at actual contacts, not only on a full cylinder. Geometric coaxiality of ±0.010 within radial clearance ≥0.020 addresses parallel assembly, not rocking. No silent pose correction or capture claim credited.

## 7. Classification, disposition and next evidence

- **Contradiction (decisive):** the sleeve ejection thrust has no assemblable rear support at the specified dimensions. Under CPL-009, a motor capable of producing torque is not a substitute for a bounded mechanical release path. Protocol §8 makes this record a G6 fail. No official gate run is asserted.
- **Unsupported:** reachability of the lock stage at adverse combined pose/stack; yaw-bounded seating depth at both lugs; guaranteed release torque under fixed external loads; quoted bond capacity on an unidentified gearhead surface. The passive, fully seated force paths and nominal margins neither settle these questions nor undo the contradiction.
- **Corrections not in themselves blockers:** ATI modulus attribution, stale force/gapping strings, hinge ID omissions and fastener inventory. **Later tests, not reasons for fail:** friction in the as-built material pairs, shock back-drive, wear/coating migration, adhesion and aging, contact length and load sharing, fatigue, tool trials, loaded voltage and repeated coupling/capture sweeps. Q031 chain articulation and originality were not reviewed here.
- **What a further version needs:** declare a dimensioned, assembleable sleeve retainer and support that reacts the ejection load, including axial position, mating diameters, engagement, tolerances and capacity at the weakest fit. Show the coupled path from presented pose through first tooth contact into the lock stage at limiting torque, including ring standoff, actual pilot bearing span, and the shallowest flank contact, or narrow the claimed envelope and identify partial states. Bound release at the same motor's two operating points with fixed external loads retained; identify a compatible motor-case bond or an independently justified reaction path. Reconcile the screw, hinge-ID and source discrepancies. These are evidentiary requirements, not changes to the candidate or the criterion.

**Basis:** the collar discrepancy alone is sufficient for `fail`; the unresolved corners are independent and have not been promoted to observed failure. This record concerns the specified v5 declaration only.
