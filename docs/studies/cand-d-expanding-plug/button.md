# Take 22 — male nose, retracting crowned buttons, blind cup

**Exploratory design-author result: build one port and one shoulder sector before building the coupler.** The real port can be shielded throughout travel, without a diaphragm, vacuum or empty-hole assumption. The key strength problem is no longer the cut port lip: it is the moving shoe's rounded transition and, after parking, the part of the original sphere still visible. Do not freeze the mechanism.

Basis: Q85/Q100 unchanged; 70 g, friction coefficient at most .23, **50–55 N actual button-only axial draw** is the latest requested design window, not a result of this study. No equal thrust or fixed tail attitude. The outer six-contact face, floating support and its separate preload are not proved here. Reproduce with Python 3, no third-party packages: put both scripts together; run `python3 -B crowned_button.py` and `python3 -B button.py`. All dimensions mm. All tolerances and force budgets below are proposed gauges, not production capability.

## An actual locked shoulder

+x is withdrawal of the tail. Three stations at 120°. At each port put the origin at x=0, radius 5.80, with outward facet normal n tilted **30° toward +x from radial** (29.75° minimum). Turn a round cone with slope tan(30°) and radius 7.42 at x=0; cut three flats of apothem 5.80, not a regular hex. Limit front x to +4.40; merge the rear into a forward-facing deck at −7.20. Round exposed convex ridges by .40: geometrically erode the cone/facet/cap intersection by .40 and dilate by .40, not an unmodelled sharp chamfer. Adjacent outward normals and their normalized positive combinations have at least the minimum face axial component. The forward deck junction must also be a forward-facing monotone blend, with no accessible flange underside.

On each facet retain a complete disk annulus at z=0, radii **1.345 to 4.30**, and a cylindrical ring of material radius 1.345 down to z=−3.75; z here is distance along local n. The port is a blind, sleeved passage as seen from outside; relieve its top edge only *inside* radius 1.345 and at/below z=0. No cross-slot emerging beside it. Usable bore radius at least 1.300. A radius-4.34 datum disk survives the nominal .40 external roll: the required circular-cone radius excess is 1.560 versus 1.620 provided, with 2.778 clearance to the adjacent eroded plane and .441 to the eroded cap. The three bore axes are still 4.421 apart at their inner ends. These are stock witnesses, not a drawing of three back drives.

Grind each integral shoe to the convex body

    (ball(O,B−b) intersect ball(O−D v,Rw−b)) offset outward by b,
    D = Rw−B+cut.

B=1.250±.006, Rw=8.00±.05, b=.200±.005, pole cut=.150±.006. Direction v is 55° from n toward −x in the port meridian: nominal contact normal on the tail is 25° toward −x from radial. A metal antirotation key must keep the crown axis within a .5° **3D cone**, not just prevent a 180° turnover. A solid stem of radius 1.03 blends within the swept shoe. Locked O at h=+2.45 nominal, ≤+2.48; parked h=−1.052..−1.032. Gauge ≤.018 transverse centre drift including elastic displacement, ≤.5° guide-axis error, ≤3.532 effective stroke and ≥.026 bore running clearance at the narrowest stack. The prescribed parked cap is intentionally **not flush or fully hidden**.

Make a real p-backed flat, not a tangent drawn on a sphere. Use minimum rolling radius p=4.80 for the accessible tail; nominal core offset t=4.83. In contact, O+(B−cut)v is on both working surfaces. The local core disk of radius .82, perpendicular to v, is centred at C=O+(B−cut+t)v. Offset the disk by t: the facing land is genuinely flat with a large rounded end. Its core disk clears the entire supporting facet halfspace by .350 **after** subtracting t. Join these lands to a continuous circular toroidal shoulder: the main torus core is at x=−1.281, radius 14.076 (shifted .78 radially outward from C). Sweep each core disk radially outward through .78, then take the union of the t-offset bodies; no sharp positive tail ridge is permitted. The radial extension moves its support plane away from the shoe. Opposite station disks are much farther away. The main torus clears the whole round cone by .294, not merely the port-centre section. A plain coaxial ring in this exact position is *relieved*, not the working flat.

One constructive blind-cup closure: continue the main core circle at that radius toward +x to x=9.60, close with a flat core disk there, offset by t. The wall clears the whole nominal shoe sphere by **only .068** at its worst axial alignment; deck-to-rearmost land clearance is .346. Inspect the finished whole cup pose and debit taper, key, land location, float and deflection against these margins. Core junctions may be finished outward into excess material only while preserving the required p-rolling envelope and the working flat. The nominal inner-cup portion fits a conservative outer radius 19.727; this is not a 40 mm complete package. The bare solid torus alone occupies 6.482 cm³, about **50.6 g at an illustrative steel density of 7.8 g/cm³**; it cannot simply be added to a 70 g mass budget. A hollow closed shell/pad is only geometrically equivalent if the nose cannot enter its internal void and proof loads preserve its outer collision envelope. Specify metal load loops and local backing separately.

## Continuous contact certificate

At any point of the relevant tail surface require an *interior tangent ball of exactly p*, or a filled, inaccessible envelope with that property. A larger tangent ball contains one of p. For contact on a smooth button the probe centre is Q+p u, where Q is the contact and u the unit normal on the tail. Therefore a probe intersecting guaranteed guard stock is an impossible contact. This works at arbitrary cup offset, roll and tilt and does not require another seat to bear.

Let c=u·n. On the intact sphere the centre has z=h+(B+p)c and radial distance (B+p)sqrt(1−c²) up to the lateral registry error. For the changed crown or blend, write Q relative to O as αu−γv, α,γ≥0. This follows from the two outward core-sphere normals at their intersection plus the b offset. The complete changed region has u within 34.330° of v; on the crown this cone is much smaller. The concentric ball of radius B−cut remains, so |Q|≤B and Q·u≥B−cut. For 0≤c≤v·n these inequalities give Q·n≤Bc; for c<0 they give Q·n≤0. The transverse component in the azimuth of u is at least (B−cut)sqrt(1−c²)−|c|sqrt(B²−(B−cut)²). These bounds also include a contact at either blend transition; no best azimuth is assumed.

The calculator encloses *every* c and h in intervals, constructs probe-centre (radial,z) rectangles, and upper-bounds distances to the actual annular sheet and tubular ring. If either upper distance is strictly less than p, the entire rectangle is excluded. This is not point sampling. With the dimensions above:

| Configuration | Forbidden normal | Minimum witness overlap, intact / changed | Coulomb-reduced force |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Entire travel | c≤.320 | .1480 / .1270 mm | **+.09318 N/N inward** including .5° guide error |
| Parked | c≤.960 | .0182 / .0464 mm | **+.009618 N/N outward in x** |

The body also retains a sphere of radius .65 centred at O+.594n. This constructive witness fits both eroded core spheres after subtracting the blend radius; its margin to the crown condition exceeds .067. At a longitudinal bore or cut-edge contact, a p-ball entering from outside must cross the top disk inside radius A=1.345. If its centre is above the plane its projection must be inside A and z≤p: a cross-section penetrating the hole cannot cross its surrounding metal, and a sharp rim normal points toward the aperture, not away from it (the opposite rim excludes crossing the axis to the far side). A centre below the plane cannot enter this blind bore: p exceeds its throat, the sleeve is closed to side entry and the back is internal. At a top rim contact from the side check the stock on both sides of the plane; a p-ball centred below the plane reaching the rim cannot fit its cross-section inside the tube or approach the convex top edge from below the stock. A hidden rear access to the bore would invalidate this argument.

Every such centre would overlap the retained sphere by at least **.01821 mm**, even at the park and lateral registration extremes. The actual sharp port lip and any relief below it are consequently *not* Hertz bearing paths in this rigid model. Do not let runout, local denting, debris, burr, adhesion or seal intrusion consume that reserve. The parked cap still bears on the uncut sphere; no modified crown or blend normal reaches c>.960 given the keyed orientation. The stem is inside the union swept by the concentric B−cut ball and behind the parked cap. Along a hypothetical forward sweep past a fixed tail probe, all shoe contacts strictly close the gap; a penetrated earlier shoe cannot become disjoint on forward extension. Thus this swept stem cover does not assume it is always hidden by the original sphere at one height. Keep the entire key, retainer and stem foot out of reach.

Counterexample to replacing the finite model by one local plane: for a *different* B1.25/p5 circular ring at h1.125, φ29°, θ50°, and a regular hex nose of inradius 4.50, the torus penetrates an intact adjacent hex corner by **.757 mm** at x=−3.982, radius 7.745, even though the intended facet-centre contact is tangent. The ring/facet construction above avoids that specific collision.

## Closed, transferring, released: three different loads

**Closed.** Require the actual crown-on-flat normals within 2° of their nominal 3D axes, *and* relative land-to-crown tilt ≤2°. At 55 N actual **sum of tensile components from the buttons**, axial balance alone permits 307.24 N total normal. With separately measured net lateral resultant ≤16 N, no button exceeds **117.64 N**, even if one or both other stations unload. Proof: project every contact on −X+.330 eᵢ for the station of interest. For each actual normal u and projection vector d the adverse coefficient is d·u−μ sqrt(|d|²−(d·u)²). Minimize over its 2° cone. The other stations' minimum coefficient is +.001454, so dropping them is conservative; the right side is at most 55+.330×16 N. A side support or other stop on the cartridge that bypasses the measured button wrench invalidates this bound.

At that single-station ceiling, steel/steel E=210 GPa, ν=.30 gives **1.69 GPa** elastic R8/flat peak, half-width .183. After crown-centre migration at 2° and .040 edge debit, ≥.047 of crown remains; the .82 tail flat retains ≥.185 including .13 lateral misregistration. Without the lateral bound the .251 patch at 307 N is **not certified untruncated** at worst tilt. At 117.64 N a 2.06-diameter solid stem gives a gross guide-mouth bending-plus-compression screen of **502 MPa**, before keyways, stress raisers, guide contact and fatigue. Do not convert typical tensile/compressive proof stress into a Hertz or cycling allowable.

**Backs and transient.** Use positive, independently force-limited acquisition followed by metal backing; achieve Q85 take-up with measured drive travel, not radial slop. Meter actual cartridge draw, hold it mechanically unpowered and maintain the separate outer-face wrench; the proposed shoe does not produce that 50–55 N window by itself. Before freeing the stems, advance the draw carriage, measuring actual individual backing reactions and whether the patch remains on its flat. Do not demand impossibly zero tensile reaction under continuous external pull. Even while the seated-angle bounds hold, summed backing-axis load can reach 243.1 N, or 261.2 including the proposed guide budget; a transverse flat backing shelf sliding dry at .23 can cost **60.1 N** before its drive losses. Once the angles change these are *not* back-withdrawal limits.

Require inward guide resistance ≤.40+.055ΣN per stem, including key, overhang, returns and skew. Getting every hard back out of the inward path matters: with backing force Bk still present, ΣN≤(.40+Bk)/(.09318−.055). Even Bk=2 N permits **62.86 N**; a jammed rigid back has no such finite certificate. A dry two-bearing sleeve screening ceiling is .691N per unit normal, not .055. Use a measured low-drag rolling/flexure-guided carrier or change the branch. A positive return should pull the stem in if free-lead does not finish; it must allow each stem to lead inward ahead of it. Prove reverse action under changed friction and power loss, without an overtravel shoulder that defeats the force cap.

**Free/park.** A freed stem then permits ≤10.48 N summed normal; a ≤1 N residual outward spring permits 36.67 N. On the moving b≥.195 toroidal blend, transverse principal radius ≥B (local radius b+core-circle-radius/sin ψ); against a convex p patch the illustrative elliptic Hertz peaks are **5.65/8.58 GPa**, not the seated 1.69. These are conservative sizing combinations, not proof that a complete mated tail attains either load or that a material survives. For illustration only, keeping this same smooth elastic comparison at 3 GPa requires N≤1.565 N and ≤.0598 N residual guide/actuator force after its proportional term. Patch crossing a curvature discontinuity, peening and wear require testing.

For 70 g, 4W axial compression plus W table resistance, and **only** the proved nose/cup contacts, delivered +x push must exceed **3.433 N all parked**, **12.189 N with one freed but unparked**, or **29.702 N with three**. These exclude static balance, not dynamics or stripper misalignment. Parked fixed cone contact is individually bounded by 11.58 N from axial balance, but the small positive parked button coefficient allows 356.89 N at a button under the same broad bound (9.80 GPa sphere/p comparison). Local pressure can therefore still fail even with the bore lip shielded. Size an actual noncapturing stripper and its drive, witness the tail moving, not just the shoe reaching park. At fixed aligned attitude >11.255 mm relative +x travel clears this coupon by axial projection; with arbitrary rotation about its nominal tail frame a deliberately loose sufficient tail-origin x is >28.85 mm (coupon only, not the whole robot). Neither is a verified drive stroke.

**Stop conditions.** Instrument the weakest free guide and both signs of shelf withdrawal, first with the last button deliberately loaded. Gauge turning of the keyed crown, lip shielding on damaged/hot and cold parts, force on the rounded transition, and parked contact pressure before testing a full coupler. The 4.80 rolling-ball condition is **not transferable to a thinner outer seat, bus contact, screw, housing edge or an accessible back face**. Show those parts physically cannot reach a port, and section the R13.5 outer-seat load loop simultaneously with the cup wall, before claiming whole-Kin release. Mass, table clearance, all guides/backs/ejector, actuator force, blocked recovery and the 60 mm installed envelope remain open.

The smaller plain Ø1.80 button and shallow spherical saddle in the control script are a **separate** preliminary 60 g comparison. At 80 N only .0087 mm conservative patch-edge margin remains under the chosen centre errors; 1° control would require .0062 mm transverse registration. None of its 35 N or 60 g load numbers are credited in the keyed-crown design.
