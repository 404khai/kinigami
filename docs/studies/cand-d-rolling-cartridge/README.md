**Why the old lintel fails.** Set d=√(r²−s²)=2.690609 and measure height h=(z−L) cos28°. An R4.20 terminal ball centred at (17.073703,0,6.979925), h=−d, touches both bottom lips and the actual old inner corner at x=18.20,z=L+.40. The corner-to-centre vector gives nₓ=−.268166, g=−.505404. The entire named tail can be put behind this ball on the isolated old hood: align its local +X with the inward roof normal, using the far stem terminal as the touching ball. The stem generators trail the terminal along the same normal; all other centres trail by at least 3.5, beyond one radius below L. This is not a claim that this pose occurs while the six lands remain occupied.

**Finite-mouth proof.** For any generator ball of radius r outside the chamber let c be its centre and p₀ the closest point of W. The centre cannot get inside W: any entry crosses material or an opening where there is stock at y=±s at the same x,z, nearer than r. No pose bound is needed.

1. If p₀ is metal, it is the only possible point of contact, by uniqueness of convex projection. Its outward normal, including a convex edge normal, has positive +X work on the lower, upper, lateral and front exterior. The minimum is the side plane, g=.001495. A normal in a convex cone spanned by these forward normals preserves their minimum nₓ when normalized. The **external aft face and its edges** are excepted unless buried or checked separately below.
2. Otherwise p₀ is in the lower slot, front notch, or their shared convex corner. Its y equals cᵧ. The xz projection is onto the 2D outer profile: the lateral boundary is strictly outside |y|≤s. Write c=p₀+d₀n₀. The actual metal at (p₀ₓ,±s,p₀_z) forces d₀≥√(r²−(s−|cᵧ|)²)≥d. The xz normal n₀ is lower, forward, or between these, hence n₀ₓ≥sin28°.
3. For metal q with |qᵧ|≥s, the squared distance is at least d₀²+(s−|cᵧ|)², already attained at a lip endpoint above. Thus a contact here **must** share p₀'s xz; its nₓ≥(d/r) sin28°. This covers the two finite rails and their front ends, not infinite-plane extrapolation.
4. For central metal |qᵧ|<s, if p₀ is on the front or bottom/front corner, qₓ≤18.40=p₀ₓ, so nₓ≥d₀n₀ₓ/r. If p₀ is on the lower opening, h(c)=−d₀. A lintel point has h(q)≥2 cos28°>r−d: it cannot touch. The central upper shell is even higher. The only other central metal is aft of the blind slot end, qₓ≤9.649≤p₀ₓ, which again gives the positive nₓ bound. This separates the favourable **blind slot termination** from the potentially adverse **rear of the entire housing**.

For R4.20 that lower-lip bound gives g≥.063538. For smaller rollers, positive lip work needs r>3.758618 and hiding the new lintel needs r>3.827807. R3.85 passes both, but has only .0187 normal millimetres spare at the lintel. All results below use R4.20. A union of balls is essential: a decorative fillet radius at a few edges is not this geometric property.

**Parking survives.** A point inside the chamber on a contacting ball is joined to its exterior centre by a material segment of length ≤r. Where the segment crosses either opening, the two stock endpoints at ±s imply at least d of segment outside: the weighted mean of squared distances to those endpoints equals the squared centre-to-crossing distance plus s²−y². Maximum intrusion is r−d. Parked material starts at h=3.835654 (forward edge of bottom cap at z=12.0); the raised notch reaches only h=1.765895. The worst reserve after intrusion is **.560368**. This proves the same parked cone, R4.2688 root to locked z=14 and short internal bridge remain inaccessible. It does not make Take 44's longer bridge fit.

**Why not draft the slot instead?** An inward sidewall normal with positive work requires growing half-width slope k>.248453. At the upper .40 stock layer the cone section satisfies X²+y²≤(a+uX)², a=.48+.32(7.53675+.40), u=.32 tan28°. Even the least intercept of a straight drafted slot containing this ellipse is a(√(1−u²+k²)−ku)/(1−u²). At the limiting slope its forward half-width reaches 4.5221: larger than both the R4.20 roller and the actual 3.4324 outer half-width. A variable slope everywhere ≥k cannot recover width downstream of the ellipse's supporting point. A narrower undrafted slot with a raised lintel avoids both conflicts.

## What is sufficient to call the cartridge clear?

*Continuous work test, not a pose lock.* For any exposed rear cap F at x=f, let c range over the actual transformed generator centres. Compute the minimum over c of

    max(distance(c,F) − r, c_x − f − λr).

A strictly positive value excludes all adverse contacts with F: either the ball misses F or its centre-to-contact normal has nₓ>λ. Evaluate in the cap's current frame, including the face translation. This is a scalar *observation of the loose solids*, not a command to constrain their pose. It can also be made redundant by genuinely burying a cap in metal, but then that metal's own exposed surfaces must be tested. The minimum is 1-Lipschitz in centre displacement relative to its cap. For continuous monitoring debit measurement error, deformation, and an upper bound on relative centre travel between observations (linear travel plus angular travel times radius). An unknown bound or a nonpositive result is **not certified**, not a detected jam.

With the aligned tail and face co-translating, this signed margin is ≥.499317 across the six rear caps; it only improves for further relative +X tail motion. The full, **unburied** aft hood face gives ≥.869084 along the aligned exit. Co-motion is an example trajectory, not enforced by the seat. For any measured trajectory passing these tests, the named local contacts all do positive +X work, even with several occupied at once. A stationary or commanded pusher alone is not evidence the loose tail moved.

*Terminal test.* The named tail is clear of the fixed cartridge whenever its actual minimum x exceeds 18.40 plus measurement reserve. In alignment that needs **more than 15.10** tail travel; +16.80 yields 1.70. Independently of attitude, eye-centre x>34.95621 also suffices, since every named tail point is within 16.55621 of that centre. For complete separation additionally verify a positive measured gap to the **moving face**, e.g. using disjoint x supports after its retreat, and to the actual enclosing frame. A later detached recontact does not retroactively invalidate this event. No loss of contact or match between actuator travel and tail travel is presumed.

**Boundary.** The fixed slot and front no longer need an external pose bound or shutter. The local noses admit a continuous nominal path and a positive-work monitor for their remaining rear surfaces. The rejected carrier, unmodelled fairing/fork, mounts, table loads, sensor accuracy, force capacity, tolerances and actual free-motion history are not certified. This is not an uncontrolled-release guarantee, an acceptance run, or a change to Q12/Q85/Q100.
