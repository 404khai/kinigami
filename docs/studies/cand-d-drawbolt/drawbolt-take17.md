# Take 17 — direct drawbolt stop; permanently engaged internal shuttle alternative

**Exploratory design study on a82f52c; no candidate change or positive G6 claim.** The direct mating thread fails the last-load transfer regardless of catalogue actuator strength. Keep its measured thrust carrier, not the mating thread. The internal shuttle has a plausible axial section; rolling contact, a separately actuated bias, edge shape, overload, and positive ejector reset have real consequences. The six-crossbar alternative does not fit the same force/travel envelope unchanged. Dimensions below are proposed gauge calls in mm, forces N, torques N·mm. Reproduce numbers with `python3 -B docs/studies/drawbolt-take17.py`.

## A. Direct drawbolt (do not pursue as full mating thread)

**Two orientations.** Use a captive rotating male head screw and a nonrotating floating female tail insert as the less bad orientation: one axial-thrust stack carries both clamp and extraction, replaceable nut can be removed with the tail cassette, and spindle displacement can be read without inference from servo turns. A fixed male on the tail and rotating female in the head avoids the projecting rotating tip, but needs a bored hollow spindle and equivalent female start relief, tail tip protection, and angular/lateral compliance of the fixed stud or rotating assembly. Neither fixes first/last thread loading. No start under tilt credited. Back off, re-present unloaded and check positive draw per revolution instead of using torque to straighten crossed threads. A spring-extended captive male tolerates premature contact; require a separate indication that it extended rather than bottomed, not a credit for greater engagement.

*Example locked section:* Face x36.40, opposite tail face x−15.90 (pitch 52.30). Three metal axial pads at R4.80, azimuths 90/210/330°. Exactly three radial-open antiroll keys with parallel axial flanks and tangential clearance, no V seat or pilot. Keep the same three flanks available through unthread by making straight non-undercut tongues 3.00 long, with lead-in beyond the last load-bearing side. They pass torque and shear, not tension. At only one engaged tongue no free-body torque couple is guaranteed; don't let the central thread substitute a hidden transverse support. No lock inferred when one key has not entered.

Relative to the head face, unloaded male full M3×0.50 flank −0.60..+2.35, nose tip +2.72, shifts +.64.. .72 during final draw. Nut entry +.18, full female +.55..+2.95, blind bottom ≥+3.72, ends gauged ±.03. Minimum common full length 2.34 = 4.68 pitches, min tip clearance .25. Provide physical root undercut and gauge full-form ends independently of chamfer. Roughly 5–7 turns from useful start through draw, not a position measurement. Solid surfaces separate at gap ≥2.60 once spring returns, not after one turn. Tail nut OD5.4, wall at thread crest ~1.2, through steel nut insert seated in 5.3 external cassette; through end internally open for inspection then capped with blind recess. Design 0.20 radial/1° pitch-yaw float without hard contact in service, by gimballed tension mount or crossed flexure frame, provisionally Ø6.5 bay. A loose sliding nut hard-clamped to a flat seat has up to μP transverse friction and is **not** isolated. A spherical seat alone permits angle, not lateral displacement. No source-rated compact floating support or antirotation flexure has been established. For full threads nominal female shear proxy .5πd2 L ~9.83 mm²: ~3 MPa at 30 N and ~38 MPa at 370 N, before nonuniform load/roots. This is not a strength qualification for shortened, crossed or partial threads.

*Actuator:* Spline tip x25.35±.025, case xmax22.58, retained M1.7×3.5 head xmax27.35. Fit hub before axial insertion; not an axial bearing. Example rear [NSK F6‑12M](https://www.nskmicro.co.jp/en_ch_bearing_size_pdf/ISC_P45.pdf) 6×12×4.5 x23.35..27.85, rotating collar to 28.55, forward [F4‑8M](https://www.fitrunbearing.com/product/f4-8m/) 4×8×3.3 to 31.85; separate nonrotating thrust seat to 32.20, [Smalley CMS08-M1](https://www.smalley.com/wave-spring/cms08-m1) x32.20..34.84 in retracted state. Fixed metal reaction plate to x35.30. Rear race is taking active extraction only after the floating rotor returns to its rear stop. Allow the rotor's full +.84 movement in the forward seat, radial journals and axially sliding input coupling. Shroud the rear bearing cage across potential unloaded end float. Races and shell constrain axial load; do not axial-slide a clutch on the servo output.

Wave spring fits Ø8 bore/Ø5 shaft, **30 N at H1.78**; free height 2.82 and 28.85 N/mm are REF. Set initial H2.64±.02, demand .68±.04 forward measured carrier stroke after verified three-face seating; H1.90..2.02. Gauge actual force vs displacement (approx 23–27 N linear estimate; not a tolerance). Mechanical travel bypass at .82±.02 gives minimum H1.78, but then **does not cap force** at the hard backup. Use independent displacement sensor at thrust seat AND three actual face/stand-off flags; motor turns/current lack observability. Redundant stop before flattening. A proposed bidirectional input tolerance-ring clutch around Ø4.45 hub, Ø5.6 sleeve, length ~3, separated from sliding torque dogs to the moving rotor, must be tested at e.g. 18..26 N·mm; 26 gives frictionless 327 N on .50 lead. No product rating for this tiny slip window: [EQYO's compact example starts at 200 N·mm](https://www.eqyo.com/torque-components/slip-clutch). M3 at μ=.23 takes .442 per N to tighten; 28 N + 4 other ~16.4 total. At μ=.05 the thread itself can backdrive; require a mechanically supported bidirectional index/brake or proven minimum thread resistance and anti-vibration behavior, not servo friction or a presumed self-lock. No such brake is qualified here.

*Pusher and last turn:* [Lesjöfors compression spring catalogue, p8 no.1163](https://www.lesjofors.com/siteassets/corporate-page-blocks-media-and-files/products/products-categories/stock-springs/stock_spring-catalog_2025_stock-spring-catalog.pdf): wire .30, mean Ø2.42, L0=10, minimum loaded length 4.6, Fn=3.1, k=.57. Three alternating Ø2.95 bores with thin captive plungers in the external 5.30 cassette at R4.65, H4.75 locked to H7.85 after 3.10 face motion. Reference total 8.98→3.68, before plunger loss, tolerances and asymmetric/one-pad contact. Confirm end caps, nut flexure, released plunger retention and tail bolt paths do not intersect. At 50 g with Y/Z moment magnitude from pitch×W plus 14.5 yaw, centred preload for three unilateral pads not to lift is ≥14.24 **net of pusher** (26.19 for 52.2 yaw fault), before extra moment from unequal pushers. Preload is not automatically sufficient. Do not carry over v7's reciprocal normal bound. Pressing only at one offset pusher adds M, not N/3 on each pad.

**Decisive stop:** Once faces are separated and all nonthread members are straight axial-exit guides, a tensile external load still crosses the thread. Last partial flank area tends to zero at finite load. The pusher adds tension, not relief. Compression may also exceed a weak single pusher or jam a guide. Rotating the other gender, an angle counter, increasing full engagement or a chamfer does not supply a sign-changing alternate load path. A positive temporary bidirectional transfer latch would be a separate mechanism with its own release proof. Stop; do not call this bounded release.

**Prior art rather than originality credit:** [Tyco US6435891B1, abstract, claims and Fig 1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6435891B1/en): laterally floating blind-mate connector, central alignment post and through-bolt. [US6406210B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6406210B1/en): captive jackscrew with mechanical extraction load path. [Boeing US4802861A](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4802861A/en): roller/standpipe angular alignment with axial jackscrew. [Glenair AN0021 rev B pp3–5](https://www.glenair.com/test-reports-and-technical-information/pdf/application-note/an0021.pdf): mating hardware and level engagement to avoid walking/cross-loading; bench guidance, not a Kin rating. Neither a central screw, float, positive extraction nor a connector with indexing is novel in isolation. The separated three-face/three-shear architecture still needs independent comparison.

**Ejector return is not automatic.** A straight push shoulder plus lost motion provides only a positive *outward* stroke. For direct symmetric drive, positively transfer each rod from a fixed shoulder to a bilateral shuttle notch before driving it, and transfer it back on the reverse *before* any keeper returns. No spring detent is proof of transfer. [DME two-stage ejector](https://dme.net/wp-content/uploads/ProductSheets/2-Stage-Ejectors.pdf) is a macroscopic locked-rod example (small TL20 is 20 mm on its own, not a part that fits here). Packaging an equivalent ≤1.5-wide transfer with one loaded rod, locked-clear proof and no double capture **remains open**. With direct shoulders, one 8 N pad and .30 eccentricity would cost roughly 8×(1+.092)×(1+2×.23×4.95/5.2)≈12.6 axial before transfer friction; this is not an achieved push rating.

**Genuinely positive fallback**, avoiding a switchable coupling: each output rod driven by its own bidirectional bellcrank slot, not by a common opposite follower. Fixed radial pivot x34.45 (translate the crank and drive housing together to suit the selected face shift); pivot local t=−2.55, R4.65. Output arm 2.80 toward +t, input arm .85 toward −x; ±30° about a non-singular middle. Output cross-slot absorbs t drift .375. Capture a hardened input roller in a profiled (x,t) track on the shuttle. For angle θ, track centre relative to shuttle = (34.45−.85 cosθ−q, −2.55+.85 sinθ). Dwell θ=−30° during keeper motion; linear/blended sinθ on eject; then dwell +30°. It retraces on reversal. With 2.80 screw stroke, .10 roller/cam drag, .23 sliding shuttle and unilateral 8 N on the pad, screen costs **38.0 N before hinge drag**. With 1.40 input (2:1), **59.5 N**. Positive-reset rocker is more concrete than a miniature transfer latch but misses 35; no free lever gain. Shorten the rod or move its pivot 1.50 backward for early head and resweep around the front bearing. If using the longer head/shorter stroke, use the +2.70/6.20 section, not both allowances twice.

### Torque, power-off and fault discrimination

At 17 N aggregate base radial resistance, slope 1.65/3.80, use G=(k+μc)/(1−μc k). Radial drive reaction R=17/[1−μg(G+2e/L)], e=4.30,L=4.40. Axial cam component = GR. The script charges shuttle eccentricity 5.60, guide span5.20, side-force overhang2.80, roll-offset2.40 on ≥8.0 couple, and feedback of screw torque onto the guides. These are requirements on the finished guides, not assumed equal reactions. Sliding (.23 all) = 78.39 N with fine screw or 79.78 with 2 mm lead. **μc=μg=.10**, μsh=.23 gives **34.24 N / 27.54 N·mm including 5 N·mm extras** with lead2. At .12/.12 this becomes 37.86 N; a seized cam at .23 with rolling gibs gives 41.57 N. If shuttle also runs at .10, 21.85 N. A plain .70 journal in 1.70 roller already costs .23×.70/1.70=.0947 before rolling hysteresis; .10 is a tight **acceptance target**, not a material constant. Side contacts, debris and slow reversal are particularly important. Gauge breakaway and camera-check rollers rotate at worst load; do not accept these as unconditional no-jam bounds. Losing an outer roller must not let a blade wedge.

Opposed axial leaf pairs: even two folded-pair stages splitting 1.65 ideally, L10.5, b1.15, t.16 spring steel, nominal bending at E200 GPa ≈720 MPa; pinned Euler load per leaf ≈7 N, below a one-sided 15 N axial seat. Single stage's ~.6δ²/L=.156 parasitic x is already larger than the .09 minimum axial gap; do not assume cancellation under unequal loading. Leaf-plus-rolling thrust shoe might work but doesn't eliminate the loaded shoe. Prefer rollers; require actual axial capture until the loaded contact rolls off.

Static screw comparison at μthread=.23: M2.5×.35 has .362 raise/.243 lower torque per force; a 34 N·mm frictionless jam develops **610 N**, requiring **148 N·mm** dry thread-only to loosen. Reject fine lead as a symmetric recovery argument. Four-start lead2.00 has .658 raise/−.027 lower; same limit produces 106.8 N and thread itself is still backdrivable at .23. That does not unjam a seized pickup or protect the structure near a slope approaching zero. Specify verified two-direction mechanical torque limiter, coast/energy bounds and actual output flags. Conditional target e.g. 30..34 N·mm slip band leaves little reserve for dry catch/release and is **not an identified part**. Near-zero cam lead may turn a small torque into a large jam load. In the closed state both keeper and bias backup must react into housing on real zero-motion lands with sufficient physical dwell/end lock for power-off; motor holding is not a substitute. Independent direct blade/lock and biased-pad home + output-travel/separation sensors required. Fit 3.3 V gated optics on verified sightlines from +y flex; mode 2 turns cannot report shuttle, post-transfer pad or release. Use original 4.8..5.2 V rail under .88 A screened peak. Re-establish actuator temperature at prolonged turns and jam, not just peak power.

## C. Six distinct crowned bars: candidate section and stop condition

Three axial keepers at 90/210/330 with **barrel axes tangential**; three independently located shear cheeks at 30/150/270 with **barrel axes radial**. Same R4.65, Ø2.10 max body, barrel length 2.90 and crown R20 down barrel length. Only ±x poles constrained on the axial bars, only ±t poles on the shear bars. Machine radial underside neck on the axial bars to an inboard axial stem; support the radial bars from the inner end via a coaxial reduced tang into an inboard tail stem. Neither clamp/weld across the working crown. Retain a pull-through section at the roots and replaceable button/flat. All six anchored to a scalloped tail plate; check the 270° station and chassis floor. No V seats or extra central stop.

1. Axial bars occupy n3.60..5.70, |t|≤1.45. Fork inner sides ≥1.75 for the **whole sweep**, not just 1.60, leaving .30 nominal before tilt and manufacture. Widen toe face |t| to ≥1.05 now that axial stations have no shear cheeks. Along a crown, R20 and tilt .5° shift line of contact .175; plus .18 placement +.275 patch semiwidth gives .630 occupied each side before end roll. A 1.45 half-bar with .25 end chamfer leaves ~.57 additional reserve; >~2° tilt can consume it. Keep current load sign/eccentricity bounds separate from these provisional values. An R.60 keeper edge on the crowned middle is better than R.10 against a sphere; it is not a material qualification, especially at a barrel end.
2. Shear bars range n3.20..6.10, |t|≤1.05. Opposed cheek plates |t|1.12..1.52; limit radial footprint to n3.65..5.65. At 60° between stations, closest projection of adjacent cheek on the axial station's tangential axis is 3.65sin60−1.52cos60=2.401, vs old rail at 2.12; .281 left before tolerance. The bar at its inner end has only .126 by the same calculation, before any tilt, and widening the fork reduces it. An old inboard axial-guide support at n1.35, t2.12 runs through the adjacent radial stem: a stem at n2.55 in the adjacent frame maps to n1.275,t2.208! **Move inboard guide starts to ≥2.05** and neck root inward/slimmer, or move supports behind the moving stems and gauge their entire stroke. 3. To put the closed axial pole on a broad flat rather than a 0.60 radius, use lip n3.84 and start of flat at 4.44 (nominal .21 reserve to pole). Still require n≥5.96 open: **2.12 throw**, not 1.65. The 7.60 stack cannot simultaneously claim that longer path, 34 N radial screen and old swept volumes. An all-rolling shuttle or a longer section could change the trade, but it needs actual rail locations, end clearance, bearing and drift data. Do not freeze this as a pass.

**Next discriminators:** build a single section coupon with measured force/position vs cam direction, clean/dirty/one-roller-seized cases, compare spherical edge to a finite crowned bar **at the ends**, measure reversal with worst preload, and run 3D tolerance/tilt sweeps including every moving nose and neighbouring stem. ## D. Three crossed crowns instead of six bars

Prefer this branch for a coupon: it removes three stems, but does **not** qualify retention. Use the owner's 12° meridian continuation, not the initial positive-part construction: the latter admits an entire straight generator at r=0,t=b extending to x=+1.018; forward tilt can take shear to its end. Let b=1.045, H=20, B(u)=b−u²/(H+√(H²−u²)). In station coordinates require h(x,r)≤B(t), h(−x,t)≤B(r), where h(x,y)=hypot(x,y) for x≥−sin12° hypot(x,y), else −sin12° x+cos12° |y|. This is a convex intersection. Front working meridian x/r has its long crown in t; rear shear-exit meridian −x/t in r. At midplane the diagonal corner is at r=t=1.019 (distance **1.441**, not the sphere's 1.045). The script bounds inactive-surface slack >.142 on |u|≤.70 over the ±102° meridian at H20, including grid error. This is a geometric margin, not a contact stress.

**Patch and pose.** Use the owner's 43.1 N, 3.38 GPa, .275 mm patch semiaxis as a comparison for a central steel patch at R.60 versus 12.15 GPa at the old R.10/sphere edge, not a material allowable. Worst weak-axis shift is H sinβ; with .040 edge/form allowance, H sinβ+.275+.040≤.700 requires **|β|≤1.103°**. Pose, profile tangent error, cheek alignment and local elastic slope spend the *same* budget. For example .5° fixture tilt plus .5° loaded slope leaves only .036 mm unspent strip width. Hertz ellipse short semiaxis from the same assumed load and pressure is ~.022 (p0=3F/2πab); also keep the meridional footprint + form guard within the 12° termination, not just its centre. This check alone does not bound shoulder contact pressure, frictional traction, impact or load history.

**Counterexample to assuming alignment from clearance.** Rotate about the chord through two held station centres by 1.5°. The third centre travels 1.5R sinφ=.183 along x, lateral centre motion <.003. The pair's weak-axis projections of the axial plane normal are √3/2 sinφ (β=1.299°); patch plus guard reaches .768. The largest increase in an axial half-extent near a pole is ≤H(1−cosφ)<.007; .183 + twice this remains below a .210 axial pocket. Translation can put a held pole on a front blade. The third site is even less restrictive once its blade leads clear. This is a possible pose in the clearance-only model, not a predicted force equilibrium. Similar proof is needed after the last keeper leaves, when tilt is not captured, even if manufactured faces are excellent. The v7 wrench proof for true spherical poles (normal through centre, zero normal lever) is not transferable to a crown. At β=.5° the weak-axis line of action can already miss the centre by ~.165 mm; do not conflate this with centre location tolerance.
