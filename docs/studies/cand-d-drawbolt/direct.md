# Take 16 — Captive draw screw mating into a passive nut
**Exploratory; do not freeze this topology.** It can generate useful preload within rated motor torque on complete good flanks. Its axial thread strength cannot stay finite down to vanishing last-flank area. No transverse roller, accurate torque measurement or ejector supplies the missing tensile carrier. No revision decision, candidate changes, independent review or gate run here.

## Complete-thread window
Three axial pads R4.65, 120° apart, and three hard tangential roller coordinates at the same radius staggered 60°. Opposite sides of each tangential pair do not both touch. No full round pilot in the working load loop. Three axial pads carry compression and two bending moments; three tangential coordinates carry shear and twist geometrically. The central bolt pulls. It is a seventh reaction, so bound it **independently**, not by assuming even loading of all six pads. Positive tangential clearance alone has v7's Q85 take-up deficit; any bias must be separately bounded.

[Manufacturer, Smalley CMS08-M1](https://www.smalley.com/wave-spring/cms08-m1): 30 N at 1.78 mm, free height 2.82, spring rate **reference** 28.85 N/mm, Ø8 bore/Ø5 shaft. One possible bench window is 1.70–1.88 mm **actual** stack height (nominal linear estimate 32.31–27.12 N). Require 25–34 N END-TO-END axial force after carrier losses over this full interval, both directions, load cycles, temperature and installed alignment. No source-guaranteed tolerance, life, nonbottoming or measurement accuracy is asserted. Use nonrotating spring-loaded thrust cartridge with independent displacement readout. A travel-triggered drive disconnect/end-rotation stop must prevent further advance before the spring bottoms: an axial shoulder or shunt parallel to a compressed washer lets tension grow without changing the reading. The band must cover service separation, obstruction, mixed friction and kinetic overrun, not only assembly. No current→force inference.

Single-start M2.5×0.45 and M3×0.5; assumed pressure-flank radii .92–1.25 or 1.12–1.50 mm, helix as cut, ±1° local normal uncertainty, μthread=.10–.23. On an undamaged **single pressure-flank sign** the minimum normalized axial resultant after arbitrary Coulomb friction is .727 N/N; define l=p/(2πr_min), n_x=(1+tan²30°+l²)^−½. Debit normal angle and μ times the largest axial tangent projection. The flank normals pass through a distributed helix, not the bolt axis. Circumferential normal projection ≤l cos30° plus angle error; use full friction disk and r_max. One sector can carry everything:

| At measured 34 N bolt load | M2.5 | M3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total thread normal ≤ | 46.78 N | 46.75 N |
| Torque, either direction ≤ | 18.42 N mm | 21.67 N mm |
| Lateral force of one sector ≤ | 35.13 N | 35.08 N |

Even the smaller screw leaves 30.61 N mm of 49.03 rated for ALL bearings, drive and carrier. A plain thrust face μ=.20 at r4.4 alone takes 29.92 N mm. With one-sector 35.13 N radial, 4 mm cantilever, 6 mm support spacing, journal μ=.23 at r2, and P·1.25 moment, journal friction alone can screen at 44.22 N mm; 18.42+44.22 already exceeds rating. Not a measured loss. Measure specified rolling thrust/radial stack BOTH ways under worst lateral/eccentric loads. A full-flank screw in a constrained single helical motion resists purely axial backdrive for μ_min=.10; conservative frictional work per radian ≥μ_min r_min P/sqrt(1+μ_min²) vs pP/(2π). M2.5's surplus at 25 N is only ~0.50 N mm. It is not a certificate against arbitrary imposed torque, vibratory ratcheting, oil outside the band, nut co-rotation or a botched start.

## Wrench envelope, nut carrier, and guide conditions
Take the wrench at the face ring, as in v7. W=mg; ±4W pull/push, W shear, 25.4W twist, 50.8W bend, 14.5 N mm other bend or 52.2 N mm fault instead. P>0 pulls on the pad triangle. Ignoring friction, least pad using a moment envelope ≥(P−4W)/3−2(M+M_f+eP)/(3R). A torque of 18.42 N mm can reach the passive tail, appropriate both signs: ONE antirotation tab at ≥3.0 mm can carry 6.14 N. Do not presume opposing tabs share. Floating radial support target ≤1.50 N over ±.18 mm (no unstated preload, e.g. ≤8 N/mm), angular support ≤50 N mm/rad over ±2°. Include tab force in S≤7.64 N, transverse resultant arm ≤.25 mm, e≤.12 and residual pitch moment ≤50·rad(2°)+.25S. The larger carriage can articulate ±2° without certifying the *relative thread axes* track one another that accurately. Flexure must not meet an unmodeled hard stop; metal torque tabs stay engaged at their finite radius for the entire draw and unwind. If the co-axial support is not established use S≤35.13 N and bending allowance 1.37P+.50S, not the optimistic float case.

The source uses the normalized six-column inverse's geometry, then a force/moment interval LP (outer Coulomb cones, ±.18 mm positions, ±.5° normals, poles up to r1.06) to bound all admissible **hard** pad/roller normals, not just an equilibrium solution. Rollers receive sliding μ=.23 even if seized: no invented low coefficient. Bolt measured force varies independently over 25–34 N; its full torque and side-wrench boxes participate. Each axial site may lift, one tangent polarity at most, missing contacts can be zero. Outputs upper bounds in N:

## Thread start, exit and precise stop
[M2.5 6H/6g limits](https://www.machiningdoctor.com/threadinfo/?tid=1008): minimum pitch-diameter difference ~.020 mm, radial .010. Ideal axial thread backlash ~.020 tan30°=.01155 mm. A .25° relative tilt sweeps .01091 mm over Ø2.5, nearly the whole tolerance before lead/form errors, eccentricity and elastic deflection. A custom clearance band could help; it must be gauged under load and still leave full working flank area. A both-flank bind, crest collision, chamfer or cross-thread has no single-sign bound above. [Glenair application note](https://www.glenair.com/test-reports-and-technical-information/pdf/application-note/an0021.pdf) discusses cross-loading/alignment in another product, not a Kin rating.

Pilot and torque keys must enter first, motor/drag limited in both directions. A dog point through a 2.5 mm nut into another 1.2 mm of guiding land needs >3.7 mm projection plus lead-in; check tail PCB, coax alignment, short fit and subsequent relief (a loaded round pilot plus three keys is redundant). Two .010 mm radial-play lands separated 2 mm allow ~.57° tilt, insufficient for the tight standard thread absent another alignment mechanism. Check actual thread-axis alignment/endplay and match screw rotation to local relative axial advance .45 mm/turn. Failed advance ⇒ jam/abort, not correction by increasing torque. Verify real nut/screw gap, carrier and key states on reboot. Coupon test starts with radial/angle errors and debris; force-limited misstart can still nick a first crest. Reject damaged units; do not claim reversible separation of galling from stall torque.

At full service a potential target is ≥2.5 mm **effective** full-form engagement; exclude entrance, runout and counterbore, maintain clearance from bottom. With two .45 mm incomplete allowances, budget ≥3.40 mm relative axial withdrawal (~7.56 ideal output turns) before measured separation. Torque keys must still carry both signs with full lever through final flank contact; minimum active roller/cheek projection must exceed that travel plus tilt/backlash, roller-end and loaded edge debits, not just one nominal pitch. This requires a package drawing and can increase axial drag. Relative body advance is not screw angle if a nut carriage translates or torsionally winds up. A separate positive pusher must clear the last pilot/roller under compression; even ideal frictionless guides need >2.45 N at 50 g under 4W+W table drag. Pressure on a cocked finite edge requires the actual tangent and moment arms. [NASA PRC-9007 §6](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/prc-9007-current.pdf) treats conventional complete engagement, not a rating of a fraction of a last turn.

**Counterexample:** in pure axial tension the three axial face pads and ejector can only push; the transverse rollers cannot guarantee axial traction. Even after all preload is backed off the last male/female thread must carry ≥1.804 N at 46 g or ≥1.961 N at 50 g until it disengages. If f is the remaining angular fraction of the last loaded flank, an optimistic usable surface area is at most f·π(rmax²−rmin²)√(1+tan²30°+l²)×1.02 for this intact helical profile; finite material pressure ⇒ axial capacity→0 as f→0. Even allowing illustrative 1 GPa over the entire flank, f must exceed .000720 (~.259°) at 50 g. Real truncated crests, bending and runout are worse; pressure is not a capacity rating. A rounded/chamfered drop-off changes this load path and must be modeled as a separate finite loaded edge, not counted as an intact thread. A pilot cannot carry tension, and an ejector adds to tension if commanded before clearing. Dynamic release/rupture under external load is not a quasistatic, reusable, damage-free guarantee.

**Decision:** M2.5 complete-flank torque alone is not the stop; lack of a second tensile path is. A fully engaged internal thread driving separate, positive geometric keepers avoids the last-thread defect. An independent transfer shoe could also take service tension while the drawbolt clears, but must itself open under load; no simplification is proved. No package/criteria change or candidate selection.
