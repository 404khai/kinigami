# Take 55 — Side-discharge stud: the rear of the track can hold the shoulder

**exploratory · mm, N, N mm · no candidate or gate change.** A sphere, two inner lips, two outer rear corners and a screw-driven piston admit a static jam before discharge. The piston fills the slit as well as the bore at park. It does not jam by missing the neck or running out of stroke. A more powerful screw cannot establish release for this section. The *installed* zero-roll Take 56 assembly instead hits a face index before reaching the two named local witnesses; that contact is a different load path, not a proof that the package jams or releases.

Run `python3 track.py` or `python3 track.py --json` (standard library). The JSON includes the four 3D contact points, forces, residuals, clearances, pressure screen and nearby sampled poses. No simulation engine, fixed tail, artificial yaw lock or neighbouring actuator.

### Last flank and machining/pressure screen

For a ball moving toward +Y past the rounded mouth, let d=cy−3.8≥0. As long as both inner end corners contact, |nx|=sqrt((R+f)²−h²−d²)/(R+f), ny=d/(R+f). To hang a fixed positive X load on the frictionless lips alone would require summed normal Fx/|nx|. At 99% and 99.9% of the 3.204294 mm limiting d this costs 8.185 and 25.826 N per N; there is **no uniform last-flank axial rating**. This does *not* predict an infinite jam at the exit: above d=.795 mm (approximately) the full μ=.22 friction disks on those two contacts alone point toward +Y in projection. They help discharge; another opposing support or a −Y wrench would be needed to remain there. Aligned in continuous nose contact, c_y=p+R and at p=5.60 the entire aligned ball is ≥1.60 beyond the fixed mouth. A tilted neck or a housing wrapping the exterior is not an aligned ball; commanded park cannot prove release.

At a provisional **40 N aligned X draw**, only the two *straight* inner rounds are taken to carry the opposing axial load. The lower axial projection per unit normal is cos30°−.22 sin30°; hence any one contact ≤52.909 N without assuming equal sharing. Axis-only gross neck stress 5.66 MPa (Ø3), before fillet, bending, fatigue or manufacturing damage. The conservative remaining XZ core metal area is 40.65 mm², **not** load area of a closed ring: the C is split. Lip spreading, root bending, one-sided torsion, rib/fastener and carriage transfer into the indexes still need real section analysis. With provisional steel/steel reduced modulus 110 GPa, the R3.50 sphere vs straight R.20 rolled lip has relative principal radii (1/3.5+1/.2)⁻¹ and 3.5: one-site elastic Hertz semi-axes .02344×.15481, peak **6.96 GPa**. For the jam certificate at illustrative 20 N drive, each outer R.20 sphere/flat takes 13.855 N, patch .02663 and peak **9.33 GPa**. The contact is inside the shoulder rim but not necessarily elastic; measure hardness, yield, residual dent, friction and wear on actual tool-finished sections. No strength, life, actuator or procurement rating follows. The limiting piece is contact, not nominal tensile area.

**Integration decision:** keep the X take-up separate and reject “more Y screw torque” for this shoulder/track pair. To reuse the idea, physically prevent simultaneous support on both rear outer corners and both inner lips (e.g. relocate rear rails/shoulder), or add a controlled independent escape coordinate. Prove it using swept solids including the six face indexes and the *real* guide/housing; do not prescribe a maximum measured yaw and call it retention.
