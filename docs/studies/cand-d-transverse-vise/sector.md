## Material, not an infinite force plane

At home set O=(15,3.50,0), D=(.8,−.6,0), W=(.6,.8,0); position O+uD+wW+zZ. Rotate with the toe about the z axis through O−RW. R13 pivot is (7.20,−6.90); R12 pivot (7.80,−6.10). The full-size nominal convex stock is

    u ≥ −3.05;  .6u−.8w ≤ 0;  .8u+.6w ≤ 2.85;
    .62u+w ≤ 3.20;  .62u±z ≤ 3.00.

Its closed xy vertices and paired z limits are (11.1875,3.50,±4.891), (15.6146,9.4028,±4.891), (17.85,3.678505,±1.652804), (17.85,3.50,±1.5864). **No crossbar, arms, shaft, bearing or neighbouring motor is included.** Use the intersection of inward-offset planes dilated back by a ball: radius .15 for original, .08 for the 89° branch. Thus all corners are rounded inward, and the actual back plane is retained. The compact alternative changes the rear cut to u≥−2.40, upper flank to .50u+w≤2.7727051, deletes the x nose plane and rounds by .15. Its six vertices pair z about (12,3.50,±4.488), (15.463623,8.118164,±4.488) and (17.772705,3.50,±1.624738). A ball tangent above *every* point of the Ø5 horizontal circular land fits every stock half-space on each branch. This is finite coverage, not uniform plate thickness.

At a material point (u,w,z) the opening velocity per radian in the rotating frame is (−R−w,u,0). For each outward unit normal m compare m to the unit **opposite** velocity. With angle a the worst friction-disk projection per N is cos(a+1°)−.23 sin(a+1°). The 1° debit is a total directional mismatch, not a free angle per face, stop, friction cone and hinge. An affine velocity enters a convex circular cone: tests on all raw vertices bound points throughout the solid, including all radii on rounded faces. Eligible blends are unit vectors in the cone of their adjacent normals. The normal on the **rear** is −D and is not eligible by this argument. At the parked angle repeat the projection using the actual tail force along unit v=(1,2.05,0)/|v|, **not** the torque about the shaft. A latched actuator need not move for this virtual translation.

## What a terminal landing can and cannot shield

Place a real fixed plate behind the toe's parked rear plane, parallel to it, facing +E=rot(Q)D. Nominal front face is u=−back−.09 in **terminal** axes; plate is .71 thick. Extend .70 in both directions past the entire w,z footprint of the original rear face. Projecting earlier toe positions onto E gives u cos(δ)+(R+w) sin(δ) where δ=Q−q. Its minimum is an endpoint, so the seat need not occupy the preceding toe sweep. This is not true for a fixed plug directly behind an intermediate rear face. For R13/89° the front plate spans opposite corners (−2.6315,−3.2324,−5.591) and (−9.5613,2.1565,5.591).

Consider a **real tail** that admits an interior tangent ball of radius p=.45 at the contact point. This holds by construction for the rounded bosses, web, cropped butt, properly rounded arms and the modelled integral crown, not automatically for a retainer aperture, wire, unrounded rail tip or housing seam. A toe fillet involving the rear is centred ρ ahead of that plane. If normal projection m·E≤a, the tangent ball's centre lies at most ρ+(ρ+p)a ahead of the plane. For actual local seam gap g it intersects the flat landing by at least p−ρ−(ρ+p)a−g. Its centre projects inside the .70 fringe, and plate thickness exceeds p; a tail cannot contact such a toe normal without interpenetration. The rear flat itself is also covered. This addresses rounded seams, not just the flush centre.

* **R13/89°:** ρ=.08, g≤.100 across the whole loaded seam, a=.49. Minimum interference .0103, remaining normal work ≥.0630 N/N along unit v including 11.867° direction difference and 1° gauge error.
* **R12/101°:** ρ=.15, g≤.110, a=.28. Minimum interference .0220, remaining work ≥.0389 N/N with the .1335° direction difference included.

Use a flared plate, not an exposed square side: the footprint expands .65 (R13) or .45 (R12) per unit depth behind its front. Rounded front and side normals then still deliver ≥.1286 or .1806 N/N along unit v. **The back of the plate and its final rim remain adverse** (rear nominal work −.979 / −1.000 N/N even without friction). It must terminate in a verified inaccessible support, or the approaching body must be measured clear. A separate free-standing sliver at the stop is not a closed shroud. A finite butt can in principle touch the back by orienting its rear pole along +E; every other paddle point is behind that pole. This two-body construction is not an assertion that the whole installed neighbouring unit fits in that attitude.

For an independent terminal exclusion, the paddle incl arms through local +12 lies inside site radius 14.871 in *every* attitude. At R13/89° the entire core plus conservatively flared seat has xmax=−2.267; **measured site x>12.804** excludes both with .20 debit, with no yaw, roll or height condition. R12/101° needs x>13.524. Do not transfer either inequality to the drive bar, other links, feet or the portion of a Kin past the counted arms.

## Mid-sweep: bounded route, not an arbitrary contact theorem

If the *measured* pre-push site remains x≥14.95, y≤+.25, paddle tilt ≤3° and yaw within ±3° of seated clock, the rear sweep clears the eligible paddle. No restriction from a commanded motor angle substitutes for these gauges. For normals with m·D>.53 the angle between D and the local opposite velocity gives a positive torque even on a rear fillet (worst speed-normalised coefficient .0387 for R13). The remaining adverse rear zone is inside the slab −back≤u≤−back+1.53ρ. Test the whole clipped polyhedron. For each of 808 angular intervals debit the maximum chord to its midpoint. Low stock is below the rear or entirely in front of it. The R3 loose ball has a separate centre-height/wander bound (.96/.38). For the fixed crown use the R7.55 ball about (0,−4.50,0) cut to its upper 19° cap then dilated by .45, with a central R1.95 stem to y=2.68 likewise rounded. This preserves a true R8 surface to r=2.605. Separate its whole cap and stem with a real axis in xy at each interval; extrema over inclination ±3° include stationary directions, not just nominal tilt. Table margins include .10 stock after these sweeps. A tight monitored trajectory must actually persist through load relief; this is not a trapping or sensing proof.

**Forward finger test.** Two trial fingers x14.6..15.2, |z|6.10..7.10 from the lower cheek to y12 miss the home web by .483, forward bosses by .300, and the complete central toe by z≥1.209. A finger point penetrates a forward lower boss in Take 31's aligned displaced-shoulder witness by 1.205. They are not an arbitrary guard: at q=36.8699° the R13 rear has x=4.15. Turn the paddle 180° about +Y and put its site at (−6.30,9.02,0). The actual R7 butt pole touches the **interior** of the rear face at (4.15,9.50,0). Every enumerated paddle point is at x≤4.15 and y≥5.52. Both fingers miss by 10.45 in x and even a y4.70 blade misses by .82 in y; the normal alone gives **−16.4 N·mm/N** torque. No earlier central toe overlap: before that angle its entire x extent is ≥4.15. This disproves the fingers-alone inference, not reachability from the gauged home branch with the rest of a Kin attached. The fingers themselves would also need non-adverse end/side normals and a loaded support.

A .45 tangent probe on the intermediate central rear lies wholly inside the toe seven degrees later, even allowing for rounding. A local immobile plug cannot occupy that contact volume. An arcuate *enclosing* duct preventing access from the outside, or a second moving bulkhead, would be a different design: the duct must actually close around/between the radial edges and z sides and clear the drive opening. A simple fixed inner wall at the R13 full-size minimum radius cannot retain even .08 running gap after the .20 blade debit (.036 available). No duct is claimed here.

## Package vetoes and next fixture

Take 35 packaging found the original R18 positive-z stub entering the eject nut guard. A full-width beam through the working toe enters the own tail at articulation; a one-sided sector and forward beam are **not** thereby clear. In the R13/89° layout the forward beam overlaps the next GP motor by about 1 mm at q=15° and +15° articulation and hits the local transfer wheel near q=87°. A cleared core with a blocked drive has no demonstrated stroke.

The initial R13/101° proposal at pitch 34.5 fails even without a beam or landing: (u,w,z)=(−2.85,4.70,0) lies .20 inside the core planes. At +15° joint yaw its position in the preceding head is (23.0804,−2.8459,0), with ≥.272 mm surplus to the tests for an R.45 interior ball in the actual smoothed and rounded V cheek. This bound follows from the machined deck, not its cylindrical bounding box. Take extrema over rotation and solid vertices, bound ±15° joint angle by chord between quarter-degree samples, debit .20 plus .06 each smoothing and height uncertainty.
