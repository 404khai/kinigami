# Take 35 — arcuate toe: head spacing can save the landing, not this drive

**Exploratory. No installed survivor.** A compact rotating toe can turn its working faces toward ejection, without a 21.5 mm lift. With R13 and a 101° opening, advancing the head **3 mm**, increasing pitch to **37.5 mm** and leaving the tail where it was saves the landing without trimming the previous cheek. A bent crossbar restores the named unshrouded stocks to Ø59.838. It does not fix either the transfer-wheel collision or the inadequate outer bearing lip. The rear of the curved rack now enters the fixed eject motor. Do not freeze a candidate or count this toward Gate 1.

## Cheek, blade, neighbours and guard

**Cheek test.** Take 27 has a .060-planar-smoothed falling deck with raw upper y≤−1.75−1.2 max(0,r−8.04); the .45-ball opening cannot add material. Thus x+y/1.2≤15+Δ+8.04−1.75/1.2 before smoothing. Add .060 planar smoothing and .060 upward datum, then debit .20 stock and exact finite chord yaw interpolation. Core angular hulls enclose the arc between nodes with a chord-sagitta debit; z-invariant separating planes need no yaw debit. The deck plane can prove separation without deleting material. To **prove collision**, certify a whole .45 ball within the finite backed raw cheek, debiting the 1.20 surface slope and .060 smoothing. This ball survives morphological opening. At R13/q101/p34.5/yaw+15, the original core point (u,w,z)=(−2.85,+4.70,0) lies beyond the .15 inward-fillet allowance and maps into the previous cheek at (23.0804,−2.8459,0), ball-containment spare .2725. The original seat point (−3.50,4.625,0) has spare .9734. Larger radii do not rescue this installation: R15,18,20 each have core witnesses at 101°. Original R12 seat also hits, and the original R12 core hits on its way to 110°/120°. The shortened R12 core below is not the rejected original one.

## Load path and power-off state

The model includes a two-station hardened inner track, screw-accessible frame tie, interior bridge, backbone and pier joined to the lower cheek (metal load return, not pouch or PA12). The 124°/165° shoe centres are 7.074 apart; they are not assumed to share load equally. Outboard face cap at z−16.09..−15.84 is just .25 thick between cell and moving sector. Using the full Ø5 working patch +.10 location allowance at 85 N, µ=.23, gives 1087 N·mm axial opening moment and up to 1642 N·mm transverse in the one-plane screen. Reacting the latter across these stations needs ~232 N at a shoe. A .25-thick, 1.763-wide lip with a 1.6 radial cantilever would see **20.2 GPa** nominal bending in this adverse case. This is a section, not an unallocated bearing wall. The solid crossing alone sees ~393 MPa nominal before its bend/junction, screw holes, misalignment, impact and fatigue. The frame backbone is only 1.35×2.60 and must carry torsion; no rating from those dimensions. Use a captured groove with a thick steel reaction flank or physically separated load supports, then check the fixture in 3D. No credit for a second support not present.

Provisional .35-module single-start worm, wheel pitch R13.475 (77 virtual teeth), worm pitch R1.525, independent thrust shoulders, bevel quill and ~3.5:1 external spur reduction to a Ø6×28.7 motor/gearhead. The table is a sizing case, **not a guaranteed bound**: 85 N, full working-flat extent, .23 contact friction; one transverse moment couple plus direct reaction, no credit for assist on opening; thread µ=.18, 20° pressure angle, plain thrust µ=.10 at R1.3, .85 spur and bevel efficiencies.

Compare [GP6A 221:1, 30 N·mm continuous](https://www.maxongroup.com/medias/sys_master/root/9406823596062/Cataloge-Page-EN-412.pdf), not stall torque. Full shell contact could add moments or cause self-jamming; bearing drag and thrust preload unknown. Worm lead angle **6.55°** is self-locking only if flank µ exceeds **.108** in the ideal static analogy. Oil, wear and vibration can remove that margin. At zero friction holding the 1087 N·mm service moment needs at least **14.12 N·mm at the worm**, before shock/backlash. With no rated positive brake/stop and verified powered release, power-off hold is unproven. A brake could be spring-applied and independently unloaded/released; do not energize against an engaged dog. A 20 N·mm worm limiter would produce up to 1540 N·mm ideally: at a 5.2 mm normal lever, ~296 N even with zero tangential contact. No fixed torque trip alone proves 85 N. Add a metal series load element with calibrated bounded travel, independent force trip before solid height, mechanically retained open/closed position feedback and positive opening shoulders. After loss of power support the chain externally, inhibit movement and verify/contact-unload before rehoming.
