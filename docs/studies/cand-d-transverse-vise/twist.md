## Equilibrium inequality

For the pivot line parallel to head-frame z, let X=x−xp, Y=y−yp, Z=z−zp. Unit CCW angular test speed gives A=(−Y,X,0) everywhere. At an external contact with unit inward force direction n on the moving group, define D=A·n and s=|A|. For normal load N and a full static Coulomb disk |f|≤μN, f·n=0, the minimum signed work per N is exactly

    g = D − μ sqrt(s²−D²).

For a cone of possible normal errors, minimize over the cone too. The script handles the negative extremum of this expression, not just the favourable branch. If the head rolls or pitches, transform the actual floor/pivot twist into a common frame; the expression is unchanged. A real sliding or freely rolling internal contact requires no favourable friction assumption.

Use the union of jaw and tail-side bodies, assigning the same field to every included link *for this test*. Ideal equal/opposite contact forces at the same world point and opposite moment pairs contribute zero. Contacts with the head/base, independent blade, other branch of the chain or floor are **external**. Springs/brakes anchored to the base are external. The bearing transmits force through the pivot without z moment only in the ideal limit. The finite-bearing and mesh reactions below are not erased. An applied free wrench contributes its actual z moment about the pivot. Gravity on each included body contributes ((c−p)×W)·Zhat. Nonzero acceleration requires the actual angular-momentum-rate term; a static contradiction does not prove clearance, direction of the free tail, traversal of a singular state or restart.

**One finite conditional bearing bound.** For two conventional journals centred on this axis, span L, a known transverse force F and off-axis moment M⊥ need a summed radial reaction no greater than |Fxy|+2|M⊥|/L in a determinate two-group layout. This follows by splitting the force equally and adding opposite transverse reactions for the couple. Count preload, misalignment, seals and unexplained bearing self-load in a measured stiction term T0; this is not a bound for an indeterminate or jammed mount. With effective static-loss lengths b, ba for radial and thrust reactions, a conservative loss per N is

    B(r,z,μ) = sqrt(1+μ²) [ b (1+2 hypot(r,z)/L) + ba ].

Here r=hypot(X,Y); use actual contact lever z from the bearing midpoint. The thrust group must bound the sum of axial loads by |Fz| plus its measured preload. Apply the same decomposition to gravity, true external forces/moments and motor mesh. Illustrative only: L=24, b=.12, ba=.16. For an actual positive delivered shaft torque T with a 20° spur mesh, pitch radius 12 and axial offset 14, the radial loss feedback is at most λT with λ=.02306 (do not count the same mesh moment twice). Different gearing, overhang, preload or shaft has different values.

For known external contacts use hᵢ=gᵢ−Bᵢ. If (1−λ)T plus all known signed wrench terms exceeds T0 plus their bearing losses and Σ max(0,−hᵢ)Nᵢ, **static rest is impossible** in this model. Strictly positive hᵢ let an aiding unknown Nᵢ be omitted from this *lower* bound. Conversely in any putative static equilibrium the signed budget bounds Σ hᵢNᵢ for those positive contacts. An unbounded adverse contact makes this sufficient test inconclusive. No shaft load, available torque or brake release has been measured here.

## Floor, blade and terminal handoff

For a level table perpendicular to head +Y, floor h before bearing debits is X−μ|Y|. At (15,−23) and P=(−2,−4) this is **−2** with μ=1. If the floor normal may vary 15° around head Y, a fixed head-coordinate point with X>0 needs X>√3|Y| for robust positive work at μ=1, before bearing drag. The same point gives −11.249 gross in that cone. In world coordinates use the actual signed horizontal lead and actual pivot height with the true axis; don't independently maximize tilt and height when measured geometry correlates them. No foot-forward guarantee follows from the jaw.

Q31 is an **unclamped five-Kin** articulation benchmark, not a permission to detach during that test. For a separate quasistatic free-chain release calculation, world vertical equilibrium of the entire system gives ΣNfloor=Mg plus actual external downward load (and vertical momentum-rate change when nonstatic). On a single planar table friction is horizontal in the world frame. Internal motor and coupler loads cancel. At 5×70 g and 9.80665 m/s², **3.432 N total**, potentially all on one foot, not .687 N per Kin. For known footprint radius r≤60 about the pivot, gross adverse floor moment is bounded by 291.24 N·mm even without knowing the foot or normal orientation; this is a *conditional length*, not a measured whole-chain reach. Use max negative h over the real union of feet and subtract gravity separately at the real distal COMs. With an anchored head, overhead hold-down, unsized boundary wrench, acceleration or extra surfaces the unanchored bound does not apply unmodified.

A stationary independently supported +X blade has g=yp−y−μ|x−xp|, whether powered or merely sitting at a stop. That is not an assumed real trajectory. If the independently commanded blade is advancing, a moving surface can still supply the adverse force; measure/bound it or verify non-contact. Positive s worsens it; negative s could improve it, but only with the real guide reactions, drive/brake loads, stops and available travel included. **Disposition:** the common-twist test removes the need to know internal rear jaw forces for a *specified external contact and load domain*. A bounded floor budget and a positive upper-graph region survive. Missing static side/back/blade loads, bearing limits, actual floor footprint and terminal access prevent an uncontrolled-release claim. Before testing a fixture, gauge those contact sets under load, independent shaft torque/stiction and blade force; retain faults rather than asserting separation from a commanded angle.
