# Take 61 — A finite pivoting cone patch in place of the forward lip

**Exploratory.** Removing the fixed front ligament under each working leaf repairs the specific Take 58 parked catch *nominally*. The complete circular flange fits a true moving conical face up to the tested .48 axial pole, not .55 or .60. Neither a robust flush stop nor all-attitude powered release is established. At a realizable local partial pose a whole flange can exert **closing** torque on a positively opening leaf. This is not a mechanism self-stress at zero external wrench.

Run `python3 leaf.py > result.json` (stdlib only; mm, N, N mm). This is a replacement rotary leaf on the same ring frame, **not** a hidden bridge over a simultaneously present Take 58 shoe. The unmodified Take 62 slide/sleeve package still has exposed ports, and none of this section applies to them. For the variant, the three leaves must be driven positively both ways on the frame; park them against real load, then actuate the independent axial disk, or retract the wall frame relative to the disk. Relative force and displacement at the passive body, not motor position, determine handoff. No spring is credited for closing a port. No revision to Q12/Q85/Q100.

## Real circular flange, finite face and pressure

For n=qx, d=sqrt(1−n²), contact (xp,yp)=(C+Rn,A+Rd), C=−2.25, A=10.75, R=2.5. H is the intersection of the work tangent line through that point (slope −n/d) with the parked meridian. For the aligned **entire** core, rotate each rim generator center back to park. Write a=C−h, b0=A−v, c=cosθ, s=sinθ, p0=h+ac−b0s, y0=v+as+b0c, J=b(p0)−R hypot(1,.28), U=1−cosφ. Nonpenetration of a rim ball with the extrapolated smooth cone follows from

    J−y0 ≥ 0,  J>0,  J²−y0² + 2A[J*.28*s + y0*c − A] U + A² s² (1+.28²) U² ≥ 0.

This comes from the actual radial/tangential coordinates of the rotated circle, not three equal normals. The cone eroded by R is convex, so the filled convex hull of the rim centers also clears. For .355 and .48 the constant term is nonnegative on [0,β] by concavity of the sine/cosine combination; the code brackets the linear coefficient *including between nodes* by its second-derivative bound and a one-sided derivative bound at zero. Positive thickness adds only metal outside the rotated cone. At lock the transverse relative curvature is d/yp − 1/[hypot(1,.28)b(pp)] with pp the parked pole x. ## The parked seam: actual unilateral work bound, not a closed-slot label

For any nominal cone point whose meridian projection p* is in the window region, find actual, same-cone metal. In a side cut step ≤.060 in t to the intact cheek at the **same** p (also works at the forward endpoint). Otherwise keep t and move p onto the nearer covered arc cutoff: abs(∂rho/∂p)>.77 across the .060 radial scarf cut, since abs(w−v)<.40 and abs(∂w/∂p)<.30. Thus abs(Δp)≤.03847, including the small azimuth change at fixed t. The squared-distance increment for the nearest meridian cone point is (1+.28²)Δp² + 2 r_center r_stock (1−cosΔφ). For interior centers on the bore side r_center≤b(p*)≤14.50. The required tangential fill distance Hcov is at most .06594 mm; use .070. At the actual lip p=0 there is no rear cut; same-p material exists within this bound. These are **open** running cuts, not filled slots.

For a nonpenetrating R-backed generator with meridian projection p*≤0, signed interior distance D=(14.50+.28 x_center−r_center)/hypot(1,.28)≥0, nonpenetration of that real point requires D²+Hcov²≥R². Every contacted piece of parked inner wall lies outside the ideal cone. Dot the unit vector from contact to ball center with the ideal cone normal at the **contact azimuth**, not only the center azimuth: the dot is ≥D/R since r_center cosΔφ≤r_center. Therefore n_x≥sin(atan(.28)−asin(Hcov/R))=.242561. At p*>0, r_center≤14.50, nonpenetration of the ring at p=0 implies x_center≥(.28/hypot(1,.28))sqrt(R²−Hcov²): here 14.50−r_center<x_center/.28. Since all pertinent poles have x≤0 this end case is no worse. The same simple axial bound holds for any frontal center x_center≥R*.242561. No extrapolation to accessible outer housings, back of the barrel, razor-thin forks, or a generator approaching from behind the window.

With μ≤.23 at each such pole the worst frictional X force on the **tail** is ≥.0194299 N per N normal. This holds for every such pole of any tilted flange orientation, including loaded cuts. With actual delivered forward disk force P and bounded adverse external, inertial and **all other** axial forces Aext, equilibrium requires ΣN≤(Aext−P)/.0194299; if P>Aext no such equilibrium exists. No assumption on friction direction, sector sharing, neighbor fixation, or wrench moment is needed for this necessary axial condition. A retracting frame gets equal opposite pressure in its desired −X direction; slide drag and the index reaction still require budgets. This does not bound an unmodeled external opposing support or establish the release travel. This proof requires the built profile, not the commanded θ. If the nearest covering surface uniformly retreats by e normal to cone, the conservative argument loses its positive reserve at e≈.001774 mm even at unchanged tangent coverage. That is **not a practical linkage allowance**. Unmodeled edge breaks, step up/down, compliance, backlash, damage and off-angle stop loading invalidate a flush claim. No opening credited power-off.

## The last moving toe can resist opening

The single centered normal assists rotation; a finite **front face** is different. In the park chart choose (p,y,t)=(0,14.224744968,3.10), .060 inside the front face. Place the whole tilted disk core behind an R2.50 terminal generator touching this point, tail axis inclined .38 rad in its local radial meridian. Rotate **both** bodies together .012 rad clockwise around H, not just the probe ball. The flange has that front plane as support at a single generator. For .48 its other stock has x_min=.00489, button min .07636, socket min 4.56535, forks >10.0. The other two .95-backed leaves clear the tilted flange back support plane by ≥5.1799. Their bosses/pins at this small angle have x<0; the other leaf skin fronts have x<.022. The head disk/inserts through x=−5 are clear, not assumed to push. All three angles can be equal. The normal plus admissible opposing friction on the leaf in the park chart is (−1,−.23,0) N per N; the opening moment about t is (y−v)−.23(−h)=**−.14642 Nmm/N** for .48 (−.12900 for .355). All these bounds persist on a small interval near this pose; one can approach the toe from the positive side of its support plane. This is a geometric partial state, not a demonstrated path from fully mated lock or proof of dynamic reachability. It falsifies “every contact helps rotation”; not a zero-external-wrench jam. For unit normal the required external tail wrench about core is (−1.00269,−.21798,0; .46338,−2.13145,11.24630). Scale only with the actual external wrench.
