# Take 69 — Pose-dependent force certificates; a guard that adds a catch

**Exploratory. No change to Q12, Q85 or Q100.** Three nonempty configuration families have free-body work bounds with the Take 66 jaws, rigid rounded tail buttons and the finite forward Take 67 drive. These are not a covering of arbitrary poses. In particular, certification of all actuator angles within one family does not force an attached neighbor to stay in that family. A positive parked stroke, a hole sensor or a pose label is not release.

Run `python3 twist.py > result.json` and `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 cuts.py > cuts.json` (NumPy 2.2.6 / SciPy 1.15.3). Both scripts assert their conditions. Values use mm, N and rad. No random search, friction reduction at the interface, equal jaw load sharing or dynamic solver.

### Work convention

For unit normal n from tail into metal, Coulomb μ≤.23, the minimum force power per normal N for relative virtual velocity V is n·V−μ|n×V|. V is head-contact velocity minus virtual tail velocity. Choose a different tail twist for different configurations; this is an inequality on actual wrenches, **not** a prescribed or sensed motion. All eligible contacts at a given pose use the *same* tail twist. A strictly positive lower bound m implies a summed credit mΣN when bounding adverse opening-input torque by external virtual power. External wrench includes the real attached neighbor and floor; stationary face contacts must also satisfy the sign condition or be carried as an explicit load. Do not multiply a one-jaw preload estimate by three as though load sharing were guaranteed.

### Can the proposed pole forces coexist?

For the old working and adverse boss poles at .99β, their normals fix the two actual generator centers. Across the other stations the required separation exceeds the entire core's **exact diameter 21.15056** by 2.6910 or 4.0576 mm, including both button families. At the same station, if the working contact bears on the smooth convex hull (not a separate button), the boss requires another center 8.0527 mm beyond that hull's support plane; even the most protruding extra button core is at most 3.3962 mm from the hull. This pair cannot coexist in those cases. These tests do not exclude all normals on a finite edge or a different contact combination.

A real simultaneous compression equilibrium is possible at the fully seated working poles: three jaw normals of 1 N each, six 56° V face normals of .5364875 N each on the three midway buttons; both force and moment residuals below 4e−15 in N and Nmm. This symmetric *example*, not an equal-load bound, **assists** leaf opening by 5.15090 Nmm. It is not an adverse release jam. The force bounds above rule out an adverse zero-external-work self-stress in their respective contact sets; no exhaustive search or guarantee for configurations outside them.

## One-sided loads, journals and the missing slider guide

Let S_i be the sum of all contact normals on leaf i, K_i the magnitude of its planar clevis force, κ=sqrt(1+μ²), ρ≤3.49 the maximum exposed-leaf arm. For journal centers at ±t with half-span ≥5.55, load at any t inside that span needs no equal sharing. Axial force at meridional arm ρ also creates a transverse couple. Summing the two positive radial-bearing loads gives

    J1+J2 ≤ κ sqrt(1+(ρ/5.55)²) S_i + K_i + independently bounded preloads.

This is a two-support force/moment calculation, not a normal cap borrowed from the centered pole. The thrust collar can carry up to κ S_i at R.90. In-plane clevis force may be carried entirely by either plate; the screen requires no hidden squeeze beyond measured offsets. Pin friction couples let the actual rod force deviate from the center line by sin(error)≤μpin(.60+.69)/4.66. The calculator debits that error and the phase variation of the virtual moment arm. Stub drag is μj·.52·(J1+J2); P/D pin losses are μpin·K_i[.60(1+|ωlink|)+.69|ωlink|]. Actuator-induced forces feed back through these bearings, not just through S_i. Rod load balance at the leaf includes the holding torque, P journal and two stubs before estimating slide work.

An illustrative **required** measured pin/journal/thrust μ≤.020 (not the jaw μ=.23) gives K_i≤4.885 S_i and both journal radial loads together ≤6.097 S_i, before offset/friction baselines. Pin/stub/thrust loss ≤.21388 S_i Nmm. If a *repaired*, independently supported carriage limits all effective guide drag to .030 K_i, correlated phase bounds give total loss ≤.32910 S_i, leaving ≥.03090 S_i of the .36 near-cell work. Sum S_1+S_2+S_3 without assuming equality. Actual preload, breakaway friction, clevis skew, inertia, cam/roller/worm drag and other actuators add independent terms, not zero. A normalized allowance of .03805 K_i already spends all the minimum per-contact margin. No dry rail is presumed to satisfy it.

**The installed Take 67 drive does not have this load path.** Its ±t way cheeks are at two different *x* stations. They do not provide opposing X reactions separated *radially* to react the couple from slider pin x−.54 to cam follower near x−10. Keeping transverse force F at each of these stations creates about 9.5F Nmm. For illustration, even a new 5 mm radial guide span needs opposite X reactions totaling ~3.8F before other loads. Existing cheek spacing along X is not that radial span. Packaging independently confirmed the missing guide. There is no rated motor/input torque or slider-drag bound for the as-installed mechanism.

**Face compliance and handoff.** The 68 rigid-button/head-saddle alternative assigned virtual *zero* relative velocity to each independent head saddle in a work test with λX; an applicable front-side saddle force on tail with f_x≥.35847 N/N (upper face), .66375 (blind skirt) or .18764 (terminal annulus) contributes with favorable sign. These are the separate Take 68 finite-face bounds, not results for the Take 67 moving tail cartridges. Actual saddle spring travel, differential loads and guide losses still enter their own force balances; no certificate that a sliding or tilted body remains on the seats. For an underlying compliant bolt/kinematic coupling forcing a nonzero virtual seat v_s include F·v_s, spring generalized power and guide work explicitly. No disk spring directly closes a jaw. In the Take 67 tail-slider prescreen keep its spring/guide and exposed cartridge-face reactions separate; none of the .36 credit comes from them.

Before retracting a seated parking bolt the active drive must pick up spring and external load; a bound on opening is not a holding rating. Just after retraction there is no positive stock lock; if power fails between holes, a spring-return pin on a land can backdrive or impact before engagement. Before the next park handoff retain drive torque and check real seating of the bolt *past* the chamfer, and actual held load before removing power. No worm self-lock, commanded completion, load-equalizing leaf or guaranteed next-hole capture assumed. Positive stop/preload limits and failed-handoff response require separate hardware.
