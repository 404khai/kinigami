# One plate, six seat faces (Take 71)

**Exploratory. A common, spring-loaded plate removes the three separate carriages, but a measured draw force cannot establish that every seat is occupied.** One seat can be recessed 0.020 mm and unloaded while the other eight contacts balance the entire draw, with no external wrench or guide friction. This is a statics counterexample to a force-only seating rule, not a demonstration of a jam on release.

## Which motion is actually free?

Same R10.30 three button centers and six 56° faces as Take 68; three .60/.80 jaw normals on the rounded three-lobe flange. Axes X forward, Y and Z transverse. Use true ball surface poles for friction moments. Separate working concepts:

1. A spring applies a measured axial force S to a plate that rocks in pitch/yaw about an actual center xg and cannot roll. No three-slider guide or helpful joint friction. Six body balances, one axial plate balance and two plate moments about that center make a 9 × 9 system.
2. A non-tilting, non-rolling plate floats in Y/Z instead. The same final two equations are transverse force balances, **not moments**. A common cam-driven disk advances the whole bearing stack 0.50 mm after the jaws lock. A separate finite-lead disk ramp, torsional compliance in the disk drive only, and bilateral hard drive dogs for ejection would be needed; the existing welded quill rotor does **not** provide them. Spring return cannot close a jaw. On unlock, drive the disk back under load before opening the leaves; no claim that the tail remains seated or follows the disk.

    python3 -B floating.py
    python3 -B slide_support.py

The normal-only system gives compressive *virtual* reactions at an already-seated interface. The inverse bounds first loss of a reaction over the trial independent external wrench box. For rocked plates the perturbation test uses ±.60° normals, .19 mm positions, .06 mm pivot-center error, two independent axis errors ≤1° and 1 N mm resisting torque each. At 70 g with xg=+2.0 the nominal table bound is 30.429 N, with those errors 42.870 N. The joint center would be in the opposing tail region, not in an available cavity. At xg=−4.31 the latter rises to 72.737 N; at −6.95 the singular-value debit exceeds the rank reserve (not proof every such joint is singular). No packed remote-center transmission. The quill admits only a 0.73 mm deep, R6.92 central pocket behind the disk, not a 7 mm-deep gimbal/spring.

For the translating plate the axis deviations affect only the Y/Z projections; there is no lever-arm debit for the slide axes. With ±2 N resistance on each float coordinate, the 110 g illustrative service/table/fault bounds are respectively 65.919/91.733/83.699 N. This load scales the Take 63 box, not measured on a complete unit. With no independent offsets the nominal geometry's infinitesimal plate travels per mm of worst independent normal gap are 3.455, 3.451 and 3.754 in X,Y,Z; independent 0.020 mm would cost up to 0.0751 mm in Z. This is not a finite-stroke assembly proof. Actual draw by a spring on a rotating sleeve requires a bounded torque-to-force transmission, true contact/stop sensing and independent evidence on opening; a 28 N per-button figure is not the total-disk force.

## A statically supportable missing seat

Recess only the first working face by 0.020 mm normal, retain the centered pose and leave the other seven planes/one face unchanged (eight remaining contacts). At all three buttons/three jaws the sum uses the actual surface poles, including the moments of tangential forces about the balls' centers. For each active normal the script uses a convex combination of 32 forces **inside** the circular Coulomb cone of coefficient .15, below the .23 bound. At 1 N common axial draw, the six seat normals are 0, .17660, .37740, .34490, .39407, .33159 N; the three jaw normals are .46693, .69698, .48046 N. No Y/Z bias, body force or body torque. Summed nine-equation residual <1e−10; scale the rigid equilibrium with the applied force. Complementarity holds (strict gap, zero force, at the missing seat). This does not simulate how the state was reached; finite compliance can change the state, not certify it without a deflection calculation. It invalidates treating the normal-only inverse or disk force readout as an all-contact sensor. Direct per-face contact or a different elastic load path, plus a bounded reseating method, is required.

## Face and bearing stack

Take 72's two-button rocking pose illustrates the bearing duty during release: 20 N axial and zero net transverse force still give **120.59 N mm** pitch. Its center of pressure is offset 6.0295 mm at either bearing level. On a hypothetical uninterrupted R10.9204 round bed, a uniform circular pressure patch of radius 4.70 centered there fits with .191 mm radial reserve, at .288 MPa. That's an admissible pressure field, not a claim that the thin stack supplies it. Linear pressure over the entire circular bed would require tension (no-lift eccentricity limit R/4=2.730 mm). Slots, nonuniform bending and local contact still need a real bearing model.

The common face is a complete R11.66 disk, with fixed concentric rim R12.04–12.42 and plate travel ±.18 on each transverse axis, plus .025 mm transverse allowance. At full draw, rim is at −6.45 and depressed deck at −6.95. Use the six finite capped 56° pyramids of Take 68, clipped by height ≤tan(64°)(11.66−r), plus a central boss of height max(0,min(1.60,(8.40−r)tan(60°))). Single-ball poles are not clearance envelopes for the diagonal capsules. The [release Graft](../../../graft_report_kinigami.md#take-72--release-across-a-floating-face) checks that other stock separately. From the true working pole to the first edge there is ≥.3901 mm in the tangent plane; at 214.5 N a provisional identical-steel (E*=115.4 GPa) smooth Hertz half-space gives radius .1516 mm and peak 4.455 GPa. Only .0484 mm remains after .19 mm location error. Not a hardness, finite-thickness, signed-force or strength rating.

The force LP encloses arbitrary *unequal* frictional reactions in the seated geometry. With true axial S = 96–112 N and additional unknown axial drive drag ≤.7 N + k times summed seat normals, k=.025 gives a single seat/jaw at most 214.49/307.94 N, minimum total axial force 89.09 N, maximum roll moment 945.01 N mm, and projected pressure center within R9.736 at bearing depth −7.58. This k concerns **axial** (disk) force transfer; the float's transverse drag must be budgeted independently. The 36-direction fractional LP bounds the support radius via sec(5°), including adverse contact-location error. At k=.20 this radius bound becomes 11.133 mm. The maxima are separate sizing screens, not simultaneous forces or proof all reactions occur. A real pad must spread its pressure over nonzero area without peeling; a disk contained in the rim is not evidence for two narrow rails or an ideal point-pressure peak. A solid circular R11.20 bed, less .2796 offset, has 10.9204 mm guaranteed nominal extent; its keys, film edges, wear, stiffness and actual friction have not been qualified.

Consider two crossed plate slides, hardened deck .44, two .04 interlayers, middle .34, backing .44 mm. The 1.30 mm overall stack exceeds the .94 available ahead of the advancing post tips after .04 clearance: **a separate uncut backing cannot be added**. Only the backing overlaps; machining it into the post carrier or opening positive sockets could avoid that conflict. The two sliding elements still need positive antiroll keys at separated lateral stations, captive returns, stops and a hard bearing/guide that withstands the above roll moment. At 7.5 mm half-span a 945 N mm pure couple alone needs two opposite 63 N key reactions. No air gap stands for these parts. No demonstrated sleeve take-up, preload transmission, complete stack, self-centering or loaded release; no declaration or Gate result.
