# Take 68 — a solid tail and three compliant V-saddles in the ejector

**Exploratory, not installed.** Separate trial from Take 67's moving tail buttons. Moving the compliance into the head removes the exposed sliding-button port; it does not remove the guide load. The short miniature guide exceeds its manufacturer's smooth-running moment screen in a clean admissible equilibrium, and this installation of the back bracket intersects the disk post. Neither a Gate 1 result nor a general impossibility.

Reproduce from this directory: `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 PYTHONHASHSEED=0 python3 -B saddle.py`. Requires the NumPy/SciPy environment of Take 65. Lengths mm, forces N. The inner wall, three .60 lobes, finite neck, fork and filled R2.50 capsule paths have the dimensions in [Take 66](lobed/README.md). The fixed-face calculator's seat clock differs by 180° about X from the installation, a symmetry of the signed independent wrench box; the two clockings are **not** interchangeable for table and hardware collisions.

### The real moving face

Three separate metal islands, all translating parallel to X in the advancing R12.42 piston. Let u be station radial displacement from r10.30, t tangential, and r global cylindrical radius. At nominal spring compression put the island base at −6.95. Its projected footprint is the convex intersection of |u|≤1.35, |t|≤3.81 and r≤11.88. The positive height above that base is the positive part of the minimum of:

* 1.65 (cap);
* 1.2420 + tan(56°) (|t|−2.0726) (two working flanks; use unrounded values in code);
* tan(56°) times each of 3.81−|t|, 1.35−|u|, 11.88−r (draft the other sides, including the *curved* outer clip).

Machine a .62-deep skirt on this footprint. The matching pocket is the footprint dilated by a .060-radius circle, not independent box cuts at its corners. The inboard spring/guide connection needs a stem bore through the pocket floor, always covered from the front by the island. Back the 1.60-thick piston with three metal posts; the front stays unperforated outside the pockets. At .91 pocket depth the tapered disk still has ≥.1672 radial stock outside the opening; only .0699 axial clearance under the skirt at full inward stroke. These are nominal *material widths*, not allowable lip deflections or a strength rating.

At midstroke the two poles of each integral R2.50 tail button are x−5.7080, t±2.0726. The additional capsule to x0/r5 moves 2.410 away from its working support plane in the axial projection; the whole extra path projects at most 7.65 radial on the nearest jaw axis including backing. Take 66 includes those capsules in its exterior pose. The seated button's rear reaches x−6.81, only .14 ahead of the nominal plate; their relative axial displacement (not saddle travel alone) controls bottoming. The finite stamp reaches cap x−5.30, leaving .3493 × .4742 top stock before the circular trim. A ball-plane patch of tangent radius ≤.3436 fits nominally: include the circular trim using the 1-Lipschitz radial-distance bound as well as the trapezoid's flat edges. This is before site error and finite patch.

The full island moves inward .22/outward .21, plus .02 axial shared build allowance. Require real metal at or above −.24 relative to the moving plate within transverse distance H=.085 of each nominal interior point (a .060 Euclidean gap plus .025 error). At an exposed *vertical/lower skirt or pocket edge*, contact height is ≤+.23. On the connected front side, a generating sphere of radius R≥2.45 must have its center ahead of nearby metal by ≥sqrt(R²−H²); it cannot pass through this blind gap or under the piston. Its contact normal thus has nₓ≥(sqrt(R²−H²)−.24−.23)/R=.8076. Interior pole projection only: the uniform positive axial work after a .6° normal error and μ=.23 is ≥.6637 N/N there. At the upper facets it is ≥.3584: their normals, including convex mixtures at exposed edges, are within 56° of +X before form error. Concave edges do not create a new exposed normal. A positive step larger than budget, a lost pad/retainer, exposed bore through the disk, smaller backing radius, or an exterior fork/strut invalidates this proof.

For centers projected into this interior the terminal sphere rear is at least 1.1085 ahead of lip; **not** the governing rim case. The outer .480-wide continuous annulus is not a moving skirt. Retain Take 66's separate radial aperture budget 2.17, +1.35 minimum stroke endpoint, .03737 terminal lift there, +1.45 adverse for edge direction. Neither computation excludes exterior contacts, table pinning, linkage lost motion, or an unfollowing lobe outside the aperture. Intended assembly: pad/stem from front, spring, back cap and positive retainer from rear, then attach disk before closing the barrel. Tool access and independent strain/displacement pickups not demonstrated.

### A load window, not a sensed seat

For each island, a **separate** spring pushes the V toward the rigid tail; it cannot close any jaw. Holding requires a separate positively backed jaw. The frictionless virtual-work matrix of Take 65 transfers: complement the moving-tail-button coordinate to get the moving-seat coordinate; each spring still supplies positive axial work on opening the two mating bodies. Three spring-balance rows plus six rigid-body equilibrium rows. The finite-face, guide-drag and friction LP is for **actual** contact forces, distinct from these virtual multipliers. Do not require all six real forces to be equal or infer preload from the motor.

At 70 g, 56°, qₓ=.60, ±10% independently bounded nominal spring S=28, ±.6°/±.19 pole errors: required S exceeds 19.3082 / 26.8006 / 26.1587 in service/table/fault respectively (strictly exceed each threshold). For the *same* mass-scaled box and lever arms the sufficient table threshold alone limits this S to below 73.133 g. At 120 g it needs >45.944 N per station. Use the larger of actual rated joint torque and the mass-scaled requirement, and remeasure COM/pitch for a real design. A failed sufficient bound is not proof a physical unit opens. Take 67 is not an installation of these saddles.

Assume installed effective slide drag ≤.5 + k times the sum of that saddle's two actual normal reactions. At k=.05 the worst per-contact numerical outer screens are 101.47 N seat and 227.26 N jaw (individually, not simultaneously). The exploratory R2.50/flat Hertz patch on the former has radius .1181 and peak 3.472 GPa; after .19 displacement only .0355 nominal tangent clearance remains. At k=.23 the uncertainty plus the calculated patch already exceeds the finite face. These are not material allowables. A raw sliding or rolling coefficient is not k: eccentricity raises bearing forces.

Trial unqualified round-wire compression spring OD4.42, pitch diameter 3.70, d=.72, four active/six total turns, nominal length at S=28 of 5.12, trial shear modulus 79.3 GPa. Helical equations give 13.15 N/mm, free 7.25, ideal solid-height margin .58 at inward stop and Wahl-corrected shear ~1014 MPa. Seat/stem and guided spring cannot occupy the same space; locate coil and rod concentrically on an offset inboard connection. No fatigue, end winding, buckling, pin, surface or material rating claimed. The whole ±.22 mechanical range **doesn't** stay in the allowed spring band: allowing ±.7 N calibration error gives only ±.1597 mm acceptable from the indicated midpoint (±.0989 for ±1.5 N). The transpose of the nominal 9×9 matrix maps twist and island displacements to normal gap changes. Its inverse has max absolute saddle row one-norm 7.594; pairwise-difference row one-norm 10.257. Thus even .020 independent normal-gap offsets can cost .152 absolute or .205 pairwise differential in the *linearized* model. A common draw only corrects the common part. Need matched/gauged all-site errors, seating sequence, stop clearance and actual three spring forces independently; no build tolerance certificate.

### Test the guide rather than assume low drag

[Chieftek MR catalog](https://cpc-europe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/miniature_linear_guides.pdf), printed pp. 12–15, 30–31. MRU 3MN SS/ZZ: 4 high × 8 wide × 11.9 long, C₀ 310 N, pitch/yaw M₀ .4 N·m. MRU 3ML SS/ZZ: 4 × 8 × 16.1, 575 N and 1.1 N·m. The manufacturer gives about .1–.3 times the static pitch/yaw moment for smooth running of a single block depending on preload, not a combined service rating. Published average drag and basic static load are **not** installed starting-force bounds.

Orient rail along X on each station, back of island connected by a rigid arm to an inboard block with front at −7.75. Coil on same radial meridian. With zero friction, equilibrium at the 512 independent *table wrench and spring-force box corners* gives a worst tangential force 26.22 N. These are admissible states of that box; they are not measured tabletop loads. Tangential force about the block center produces .246 N·m on the short block, already >.120, the top of the smooth-running recommendation. This is not a static fracture prediction. A longer block gives .301 vs the .110–.330 range, before combined load, friction and mounting deformation. Clearance is not automatic either: a proposed solid longitudinal pad-bracket strap at x[−18.8,−8.8], local r[8.07,8.55], t±1.25 shares an actual R.20 ball with the [Take 64 post](drum-input64.py) at (−13.80,8.35,0). Even putting the rail inboard of the drum ID cannot remove this interference. Reject this installation. A flexure or a differently located positive guide would be a new section with its own tilt, stroke and fatigue bounds.
