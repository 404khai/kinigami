# Take 58 — Three withdrawing sectors on a broad external flange

**Exploratory: conditional radial release of the shoes, not certified axial separation.** With the finite cheeks below, every force from an R2.50-backed tail onto a moving shoe helps its radial withdrawal, including on its circumferential ends. A five-reaction guide still needs an effective drag coefficient **below .02221**; a plain dry slide has no such demonstrated bound. All three shoes can undergo identical travel yet only one loads the last rounded crest. The parked shoe physically backs the window but **does not restore the funnel surface**: a rounded fixed edge can push a local ball backward. Do not transfer the clean flange friction/Hertz numbers to that edge or to the installed Kin.

## Why arbitrary rim loads cannot wedge these moving surfaces inward

Let n point **from the tail into the jaw**, N≥0, so the tail generator center at contact q is q−Rn. On the rounded roof alone, yz/x slopes are convex combinations of the four plane slopes (maximum gradient magnitude 2.20). Thus n_y≥1/hypot(1,2.20). Simply applying that bound to cut ends would be wrong: their face normals are horizontal.

At each active vertical trim with outward horizontal unit u, there is fixed cheek stock at q+.17u. The .11 side clearance plus .06 enters real cheek; the centerline and end calculations leave ≥.0728 mm of radial depth relative to the unrounded cone there, enough for the .04 fixed edge radius. At the top the .04 fillet remains inside the 18.15 liner. The same per-face witness works at a two-face corner. Nonpenetration of the tail ball requires n·(−u)≤.17/(2R). Roof slope at each extremum is outward increasing or zero toward that cut: the opposing axial plane is inactive even across the .12 blend. Therefore a trim normal added to the inward roof normal cannot be concealed by a canceling horizontal roof component along that cut. There are at most two trim components. Writing a unit normal in the convex normal cone gives

    sqrt(1−n_y²) ≤ 2.20 n_y + 2(.17)/(2R).

Hence n_y≥.388023 for **all** reachable moving poles, not just the nominal .934784. This also excludes a naked vertical side. Access to the rear face is impossible from the assembled front: a full solid shoe section spans the port with .11 running gaps throughout travel, the stationary diaphragm holes are smaller than a ball diameter, and the cap is closed. No ball starts inside the guide. For μ_tail≤.23, every force contributes ≥.17604 N/N along its own +radial release direction. An arbitrarily tilted flange, another sector or a fixed cheek can increase N but not reverse that projection. A small pin, dent or exposed back face can. This argument does **not** turn the fixed cheeks themselves into assisting +X contacts.

**Guide inequality, per sector.** Let N be summed tail-on-shoe normals, P positive radial drive, Q=sum of the two transverse bearing resultants and the absolute anti-roll key reaction, D axial guide drag. Each contact force magnitude ≤hN, h=hypot(1,.23). Max transverse pole eccentricity e=hypot(1.56,3.20), front-station overhang H≤18.6−12.97896. Roll key force ≤(ehN+.40P)/2.50. The .40 mm is an assumed bound on net drive torque per drive force, with independent preload/breakaway in the zero-load budget. Rear bearing reaction ≤[(H+e)hN+.12P+3D]/5 from the transverse moment: .12 mm max drive eccentricity, 3 mm max lever of guide drag; front reaction follows by transverse force balance. Guide normal resultants act through the stem centers in transverse section except the stated key (no hidden bearing couple). Combining,

    Q ≤ 7.71677 N + .368 P + 1.20 D,     D ≤ D₀ + μg Q.

Here N,P,Q,D,D₀ are force magnitudes; the coefficients multiply forces, not units. μg is an **effective measured** breakaway coefficient per bearing resultant, including any race/contact-angle multiplier and preload represented by D₀. At μg=.015, net outward force ≥.99438P + .05817N −1.01833D₀, before separately bounded non-guide parasitics. This is independent of unequal or one-sided contact and applies to coordinated positive opening with each jaw's drive properly distributed. At zero input and nonzero N the pressure part is strictly assisting; it does not mean an unloaded jaw moves itself. With no other drag, P>1.025D₀ suffices even as N→0. μg=.022205 is where this proof loses contact assistance; μg=.23 is a **failed bound**, not a predicted jam. These inequalities bound required drive, not arbitrary externally induced contact stress. A locked thrust limiter/compliance can bound contact force only with a known load path; sleeve drag, static friction, guide strength and stop bypass need measurement.

## Last one-sided contact, stress and the parked edge

Use the **actual broad filled disk**, shifted laterally by d toward station 1, axes parallel. All three jaws can have identical s. On a rounded crest with unit normal (a,b,0), b=sqrt(1−a²), the contacting center of the outer rim generator is (x_c,y_c)=(−1.140390,13.131136+s)−2.62(a,b). The flange core disk center is at axial x_c, lateral offset d=y_c−10.75; all its other generators are under this support plane. For either of the two sectors 120° away that *same supporting plane in its own frame* has strictly positive gap 1.5 d b. The conical-wall normal clearance lower bound is (14.50+.28x_c−y_c)/hypot(1,.28)−2.50 for the whole broad rim. At s=.40, a=.10, only station 1 touches: other jaws ≥.26009 mm, guard ≥.56517, seated buttons vs home head cones ≥.47645. At a=.01 the other two still clear by ≥.24189. This family is not a one-third load division.

A local nonpenetrating disturbance path from lock: translate along the working tangent and open together to s=.20, x_c=−2.071060, d=.132003 (linearly from the seated coordinates). At fixed s=.20 follow the circular crest to a=.25; increase s to .40 there. Continue to a=0 and on to a=−.50 with s=.40. On the first segment the contacted plane is tangent, d>0, axial advance exceeds lateral offset; on the crest the circle construction supplies the exact support. Between the listed points d is monotone on each side of a=0, x_c increases, and the guard bound depends on b−.28a (concave, minimum at an interval endpoint for fixed s). Axial advance exceeds d throughout, so retreat of any button towards its corresponding cone in transverse projection cannot exceed its axial advance. The six cone-pair distances to nonpartner buttons are larger; central disk stays at home in this geometric test. This is not powered motion of the neighbour. Outside flange hardware, tilted table contact, alignment, structural compliance and integrated drive are not in the path.
