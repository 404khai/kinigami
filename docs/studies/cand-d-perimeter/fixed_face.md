# Take 63 — take up the axial tolerance without making a spring-closed latch

**Exploratory.** A spring can supply axial preload without closing any jaw. Where it lives changes both the lock calculation and the loads. A nominal catalog curve is not a guarantee of any installed force, and a positive solution at the *seated* pose says nothing about reaching it or releasing it. No declaration, decision, criterion or gate result changed.

### A. Axial compliance on the *head* jaws (Take 62)

The radial jaw carrier is positively driven/locked; each nose can move a small distance along +X against an independent axial restoring force S. It cannot close the radial jaw by itself. For tail twist v (contact velocity vᵢ) and jaw motion dxᵢ, nonpenetration imposes qₓ dxᵢ ≥ q·vᵢ. Work from the spring is −S dxᵢ ≤ −(S/qₓ)q·vᵢ. Seek a solution with strictly positive virtual multipliers on the six unilateral hard-seat normals balancing the external wrench and the three equivalent shoe wrenches. Every nonzero twist then has strictly negative available power or violates a contact constraint, because the six-contact wrench matrix stays nonsingular over the trial tolerance box. Friction inside this *combined system* dissipates, not a separate helpful force on the flange. This proof requires all nine sites initially seated and no engaged axial hard stops. It establishes only local retention against quasistatic escape. It does not bound the *actual* contact normals by interpreting S/qₓ as their force.

Work pole qₓ=.35522, actual normal ±.6°, location ±.19. True independently delivered Sᵢ in [33,39] gives equivalent midpoint 36.120 axial and ±11.254% equivalent normal coefficient. Required midpoint for service/table/fault 24.099/34.098/30.687 (round up). The margin disappears if the same spring forces acquire another ±10% error: table needs 75.04, midpoint only 36.53. [SCHNORR dimensional table, standard materials](https://rgafasteners.com/media/0S8U/Schnorr-Disc-Springs-Standard-Materials-Table.pdf), item 000 550: Ø8/4.2, thickness .20, free .45, nominal 33 at .125 and 39 at .188. Four opposed series elements give total deflection .500–.752 nominal, **not a fourfold force**. Calibrate complete cartridge at both ends, both directions, after seating and cycling; local sensing/rejection, free travel, rubbing, tolerances, backlash and assembly forces remain necessary. At the Take 62 placements the shoulder crosses the shoe and the lower cartridge crosses the tabletop.

**Actual strength is a different calculation.** For each jaw assume an effective axial-guide drag ≤.5+kN, with N that jaw's true contact normal. This must include all moment amplification and guide preload, not just a sliding coefficient from a materials table. With c=cos(arccos(qₓ)+.6°), positive axial transfer gives N ≤ (39+.5)/(c−.23√(1−c²)−k), only while the denominator is positive. Similarly the sum of positive seat axial reactions is bounded by the total axial shoe spring, drag and external axial resistance. The independent nine-contact outer LP tightens this: actual pole locations (for seat shear, **not** the ball centre); expanded friction cones approximated from outside by 24 planes; bounded wrench residual from .19 location error. At k=.05 the numerical worst table bounds are 323.54 on one seat, 496.45 on one jaw. The independent analytic bounds are 676.03/496.45. At k=.15 neither calculation demonstrates a finite bound. These are outward screens, not loads known to occur. A 323.54 N R2.5 sphere on a flat, E*=115.4 GPa, gives Hertz radius .174 and peak 5.11 GPa. It fits the *nominal* finite flat even after .19 displacement, but has no rated indentation or wear allowance. A conventional ball bearing's published static rating is not a strength permit for this custom part.

### B. Put the compliant buttons on the *passive tail* instead

This moves the spring away from the moving jaw: the jaw is rigidly backed; each index **button** translates axially, biased toward the head, against its own two inclined seat faces. Losing power cannot close a jaw; removing the jaws would let the springs push the faces apart. This is a different nine-constraint system. Retain the three independent button translations as DOFs along with the six tail DOFs. Seek six positive seat coefficients and three positive opposing clamp coefficients. In addition to the six wrench rows impose one equation per button: sum of its two seat coefficients times their actual nₓ equals Sᵢ. In the virtual-work proof these equations cancel the three spring power terms. They do *not* fix the real ratio of loads on a pair of faces when there is friction.

The calculation uses radius 10.30, 60° seats, jaw clocked 60° from the button sites. For each possible first zero of a coefficient, maximize nominal S while external wrench, independent spring bands, normal directions and pole coordinates range over their error sets. The 9×9 nominal matrix is nonsingular even after a Frobenius-norm perturbation debit. At nominal zero external wrench all coefficients are strictly positive. A continuous perturbation cannot reach a negative coefficient without a first zero. The LP relaxes the uncertain equations using necessary projections along matrix/inverse rows and their pairwise sums/differences; nonnegative multipliers combine those rows into an upper bound with residual debited by axial-balance sums. Exceeding the largest such S upper bound excludes all first zeros. Infinity means no certificate, **not** an observed instability.
