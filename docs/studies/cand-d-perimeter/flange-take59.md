# Take 59 — peripheral three-sector clamp and solid ejector
**Exploratory. Reject both installations, not the underlying clamp principle.** The second layout retains the full annular cam; no trimming is required merely to enclose the listed stock in a straight Ø58.504 sphere. It has two actual material crossings, and a registered three-pad disk cannot take an arbitrary single-cone load without additional hold-down or guide moment. No Gate, selection, Q12/60 mm change, official result, material rating, fabrication or release claim.

## Drive, lock and load paths
**Clamp.** Three captured radial slides; Ø1.70 pin, R1.05 roller, nominal .075 groove clearance. A 1.74-thick annular plate OD42.10 and separate 140T internal gear (m=.25, pitch R17.50) connect by a positive rear shoulder. Input 20T pinion, independently supported quill and 8-slot Geneva; second layout adds a transverse 18:32 pair and 1:1 bevels. Eight increments rotate the ring 51.4286°. It has 4.5° constant-radius working dwell at each end; half-cosine rise over the middle 42.4286°. Editable ring-local slot edges are normal offsets from the actual follower-centre curve, including radial change; full blank is an **outer** model. Endcaps, tolerances, cutaways and lost-motion stops need drawing. Thin radial walls 1.575 inside/1.025 outside. Max cam slope 3.076 mm/rad. Positive opposed groove flanks retract the jaws; concentric closed arc resists radial load without motor/friction. Geneva blocking disc/scallop backs rotation in either direction at a dwell. No selected proof of arrest if power is lost in the active cut interval. Dwell friction cannot be the lock.

Load path: flange flank → finite shoe → heel and rear T-slide → cam follower → circular cam land → outer radial keeper/frame; axial component → front/rear slide cheeks → jaw mount → fixed frame. Division among shoes is **not assumed**. The three .12-gap keepers permit up to .24 radial ring float in a worst direction before contact, plus .075 roller clearance; backing geometry is not zero lash, calibrated preload, strength or individual load control. No four-series SCHNORR bias stack was installed; this layout claims none of its preload, stroke or packing. Runner stiffness, follower/pin fit, release edges and shoe compliance are unresolved. For a 75 N axial load on one shoe, trial μ=.23 makes N=535 N, radial reaction 544 N possible; at 113 N these rise to 806/819 N. At 544 N a Ø1.70 single-shear pin sees 240 MPa nominal before bending, crown stress and wear. At the same load, two unpried outer rim ligaments each 1.025×1.74 would see 152 MPa nominal average shear; not a material rating or credit for equal ligament load.

**Actual phase matters.** For aligned frictionless contact on the first flank, ΣFr=Fx/.38; finite slope and guide drag make global “max cam slope × full load” only a sensitivity. At ring φ=9.64286°, halfway through second Geneva index, s=.051933. A following neighbour can stay on that flank at ΔX=.136666. The R.12 blend leaves only .04522 mm tangent-plane patch radius; the *point* contact is geometrically available, adequate Hertz area is not. With 18:32, 20:140, .72 index/.80 output mesh/.75 combined input efficiencies, the magnitude of loaded draw/reverse control at 40/75/113 N is 13.88/26.02/39.20 Nmm at the gearhead. Closing must do positive work; opening may be driven by the load and require controlled braking. All loads/efficiencies are sensitivities, not the validated unit demand. At φ=22.5° the first flank is already gone; do **not** present the larger max-slope numbers as an aligned physical contact case. Ø6×32.8 motor reserves on both axes; source [FAULHABER 0615 S](https://www.faulhaber.com/fileadmin/Import/Media/EN_0615_S_FMM.pdf) .156 mNm rated and [06/1](https://www.faulhaber.com/fileadmin/Import/Media/EN_06-1_FMM.pdf) 256:1, ≤60% efficiency => optimistic ≤23.96 Nmm continuous, despite 25 Nmm gear rating. Speed, thermal duty, allowable tooth forces, side loads, compliance and safe recovery unverified.

**Disk.** Solid 1.45 plate/ribbed back, two shouldered guide dowels, captive screw block at y+4.85, M3×.5 translating piston in fixed rotating nut; opposed thrust races, struts to fixed stop spider and rear hinge frame. Reversing drives the plate home onto three *physical* R1.45 pads on R9.25, not a commanded coordinate. At home service compression bypasses the screw only as far as the stop/guide wrench allows. Another backed 8-slot wheel drives the nut from a separately supported right-angle 18:32 stage. 120 increments =7.50 versus 7.495534 stop travel: need a compliant force-limited endpoint, not .00447 mm commanded overdrive. An indexed endpoint is not a calibrated clamp or an impact stop. Eccentric centre push e=4.85, guide span 2.45, μ=.15, screw efficiency .28: centred 40/75 N push demands 98.5/184.7 N screw thrust and 18.08/33.89 Nmm gearhead. At μ=.23, 40 N requires 447.5 N thrust in the trial two-contact model. Off-centre disk contacts can be worse. No credit for an unproved motor brake, arrival sensor or active tail.

**Registered home is conditional.** The disk front clears the bore at home by just .06525 radial (rear .07925); +.10 transverse centre error exceeds the nominal radius. With one frictionless 45° cone loaded, its line at the rear stop plane lies at r=8.4 and tangential offset 5.36553. Axial reaction fractions on the three pads: [.93874,.36553,−.30427]. Compression-only pads cannot realize this wrench. A guide couple or captive hold-down is needed. A rearward screw preload at +4.85y contributes [.33333,.63605,.03061] by site; for the worst single cone it needs 397.5 N just to keep all stops nonnegative at 40 N axial under the no-guide-couple model. Not a claim this isolated force is the real service wrench. Six cone spreads/preload or sufficient guide moments need their own load proof. A centred axial bias on a jaw is not a disk hold-down.
