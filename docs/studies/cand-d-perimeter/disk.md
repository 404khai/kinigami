# Take 60 — what a full ejector disk can and cannot release

**Exploratory.** A solid disk can exclude a rounded flange from a finite bore; it does not remove a hole in the wall *during* the stroke. Take 58 exhibits a backward-facing lip. Take 59 finds a drive/frame crossing and a disk hold-down problem. These invalidate an installed release inference even though the nominal disk clears the parked jaws.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 PYTHONHASHSEED=0 python3 -B disk.py` here. Dimensions in mm, forces in N; the mass, friction, tolerance and lever budgets are trial inputs, not a new declaration.

**Finite section.** X=0 at bore lip; bore radius 14.50+.28x. R10.75 flange core swept by an R2.50 ball. The three R2.50 index buttons have centres at x=−3.61, R8.4. For a 45° mating cone of height 1.48 at tangential offset 2.18, its flat base is at x=−6.44553 at touch. Each pole is .41223 from the sharp apex in transverse projection, not at the apex. Button-to-flat gap .33553, flange belly-to-apex gap .21553. R12.63 disk, flat to x=+1.05: .06525 nominal outer edge clearance at home. A square full-radius rear edge binds after .23304 axial thickness: the 1.45-thick installed disk tapers .42 on the back. Assembly errors still need a fitted stack.

**Terminal exclusion.** Write A for plate radius, g for maximum radial slit between its edge and a *continuous* lip, ρ for the smallest sphere backing any exposed tail point (a union of balls, not just a fillet called out at one spot). A generator centre at radial coordinate ≤A must be at least p+ρ ahead of the front disk plane at x=p, if approached from the tail side. From A to A+g the lower bound is p+√(ρ²−g²). Thus at p≥1, ρ≥2.45, g≤1.92, even its rearmost point clears x=0 by .07187. For centres outside the bore, the fixed front annulus rather than the disk blocks entry; this assumes actual material out to the contact projection. No such statement about arbitrary necks, forks, electronics, fixed feet or a disconnected patch of wall. A disk does not eject two bodies through one another if their positions are externally fixed.

**Contact work.** On the flat the normal is axial. On the rim the centre-to-contact vector makes an axial fraction at least √(1−(g/ρ)²) for centres in the gap. Further out, where an unbroken lip enforces centre x≥ρ, the lower bound is 1−p/ρ. The worse of these bounds here is .55102 for p≤1.10. For an inclination θ, a Coulomb force has axial component at least N[cos(θ+.6°)−.23 sin(θ+.6°)]. Disk edge, smooth bore wall and solid cone give .34900, .03741 and .53533 per unit N. These signs apply to their smooth surfaces and convex combinations at their outward edges, **not the fixed slot ends or any exposed rear face**. Take 58's example at the parked slit fails this hypothesis before terminal. Rounding a rearward wall edge does not make its normal point forward.

In the ideal smooth section, quasistatic 5W axial resistance for 70 g allows total N on *disk* ≤9.835, lateral ≤9.490. The 73.15 summed guide normal screen assumes **central thrust**, opposed transverse supports 7 apart, max 14 arm to resultant and torsion key couple at R9, no preload or drive feedback. At μg=.23 plus .7 drag this costs 20.96 drive. It is not a result for Take 59: that screw is 4.85 eccentric and has only 2.45 guide span. Its model also allows a negative required reaction on a unilateral home pad. A static contact bound is neither dynamic travel time nor actuator or material rating.

**Closed state.** `preload` uses the 6×6 seat normal wrench matrix, independently bounded jaw magnitudes and tangential forces, no one-third sharing. It debits perturbed normals (.6°) and arms (.19), using axial balance to bound the sum of nonnegative reactions up to their first loss of positivity. Includes 70 g wide-head service, table and joint-fault screens, pitch 38. A positive solution is a sufficient static equilibrium for these prescribed loads, not a proof that the drive can set them. At the real .38 working slope qₓ=.35522, the ±10% fixed-normal, μ=.23 screen gives no finite certificate. The finite frictionless result cannot replace it. Independently at a single 100 N pole, E*=115.4 GPa: cone/sphere Hertz gives 6.47 GPa peak, vs 3.45 GPa on a flat. No proof of hardness, running fit, plastic set, fatigue or assembly stiffness.
