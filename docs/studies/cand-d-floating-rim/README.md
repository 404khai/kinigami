# Take 72 — A transverse floating seat, fixed carrier rim and loaded rocking release

**Exploratory.** The inner running slot and outer disk aperture are small enough for the front-side R2.45 backing. A continuous, finite-stock, loaded path rocks the whole tail 4° on two buttons, not six assumed seats, and pushes its trailing surface past the head. It does **not** leave the tail permanently separated under a maintained compressive load. The 1.60-high center boss also collides with a diagonal button capsule at a permitted lateral offset. Do not freeze this boss for arbitrary offset.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 reach.py > result.json` (Python, NumPy 2.2.6). The imported section module can also be run alone. Lengths in mm, forces in N, moments in Nmm unless specified. Finite geometry and quasistatic equilibrium are not a measured force/displacement curve. No altered gate, candidate, requirements or acceptance data.

## An actual load-bearing rotating branch

Use loaded jaw local y and its tangential axis t. The same plate can stay centered without an imposed XY force: the following *one* admissible four-contact load distribution has zero transverse resultant. This demonstrates existence rather than guaranteed self-centering or equal contact loads.

1. With plate/body centered, three midstations can be seated in the 56° Vs under an adverse axial load. Advancing from p=−6.95 to −6.45 while the jaw angle is locked moves the carried tail from C−.50 to C. The true .60 poles meet at the end. Rigid pressure is not commanded by the last increment. On reverse the tail can follow the backing plate .50 under compression; this is not a presumption that all other cases remain seated.
2. At home let all leaves move to their parked walls. The **entire** rolled neighbor clears the moving leaf stock by ≥.11664 in the cone screen, after angular and phase interpolation debits. The closed-end loaded lobe at forward draw is checked by its eroded-cone polynomial in 1−cosφ, with zero centered pole and positive coefficient for off-pole directions. Inboard and outboard cap/drive stock follow the Take 69 near-cell exclusions; core radius≤10.95 and all nonrounded fork knots ahead of x=2.6979 throughout.
3. Hold θ_leaf=0. Advance the disk .50, then over the next .90 of actual +X advance let the tail rock from 0 to 4° **about the line through the two physical button centers** at (x=BC,y=+5.15,t=±8.92006), carried with the disk. Hold this tilt for the remainder of ejection. In disk coordinates x'=BC+(x−BC)cosα−(y−5.15)sinα, y'=5.15+(x−BC)sinα+(y−5.15)cosα. Four poles remain on the two real R2.50 button ends. The third button moves forward by 15.45 sinα and is not retained in its V.
4. For each retained V contact n=(c,n_y,n_t), c=cos56°, |n_y|=a=sin56°·sqrt(.75), |n_t|=sin56°/2. In positive rock the actual tangent slip at the sphere is proportional to (n_y,−c,0), with magnitude u=hypot(a,c). Use **opposing** Coulomb friction, not helping static friction: f/N=(c−μ n_y/u,n_y+μc/u,n_t). Weight the four pressures by 1−sign(n_y) μc/(au). Under 20 N opposing axial load each retained button then has 6.75021 and 10.05901 N contacts. Direct summation gives exactly +20 N X, zero transverse; resisting moment is 17.59164 about the button line. A pure opposing −20 N at y=5.15+.879582, t=0 supplies this rocker moment. About plate center the support sees **120.59 Nmm** despite zero net XY force. Guide stiffness, pressure, thickness and load transfer must be verified.
5. ## Unhooking is not a lasting gap

At terminal the local non-carrier head ends at x≤.37 in park; trailing tail stock is ≥.70 ahead of it. The rounded tail remains in compressive contact with the four V faces. Pure +X relative translation is a separating motion; the disk cannot supply a tensile static reaction from this branch. Under unchanged adverse compression, the same four tangent reactions are admissible in static friction. A slow reverse can carry the tilted tail *back* along the already verified final part of the stroke; an air gap does not follow from terminal travel, terminal torque or the parking bolt.

For a distinct **sufficient, unqualified dynamic test** starting at rest at terminal, retract the carrier with actual aft acceleration a while the free tail sees bounded external axial compression A, moment arm ≤e about its COM, mass m, least principal inertia I, maximum generator arm L. Every center is at least .99 ahead of the foremost carrier material; because original distance to each solid point is ≥R=2.50, translating forward by s increases material clearance by at least .396s. Initial angular momentum zero gives total angular excursion ≤Ae·dt²/(2I), any axis. Thus a strictly positive material gap follows if a>A/m+LAe/(.396I), provided XY shift does not close it, other external reactions are bounded, and the acceleration is actually achieved. For the example keep plate lateral position fixed in free flight (no lateral forcing/preload; return spring centered), independent of any controller pose. Trial m=.070 kg, I≥1e−5 kg m², e≤.010 m, L≤.035 m, A=.6867 N: threshold 70.50 m/s². With a=100 m/s² for 3 ms the requisite retraction is .450 mm, terminal speed 300 mm/s, lower carrier gap .05256 mm, other local head ≥.54770 mm. At 20 N the same sufficient threshold is **2053 m/s²**. Neither acceleration nor these mass properties have been demonstrated. These are not production recommendations; persistent separation under arbitrary external compression still has no quasistatic guarantee.

The support study separately found a rigid equilibrium on eight poles with one of six V faces recessed .020, at 1 N draw, μ=.15, no XY slide drag; by homogeneity, scaling the loads gives the same rigid configuration. Neither actual disk force nor nominal travel proves all faces have landed. Our four-contact example is a reachable conditional branch, not evidence of mandatory recentering. Here no disk spring directly closes a jaw. Choosing zero *virtual* XY slide relative to the carrier omits no relative guide power on this one ideal virtual motion; measured spring, brake, guide loss and output motion are all needed for actual drive efficiency.
