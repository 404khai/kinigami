# Take 57 — a fast internal retreat after alignment

**Exploratory. Do not install this spring as a release fix.** A fast, captive return is not equivalent to a known neighbour pose. Test the most favourable phase of [the T-bar study](vacuum-fork.md): the bar **already aligned**, with the clocking dog clear. The spring opens, never closes or holds the latch. Nothing is fired across the interface. A separate mechanical stop holds the charged spring and a separate metal load path holds the coupling during service.

## Time and energy after the trigger

The [MISUMI SWM catalogue, 8–15 row](https://cn.c.misumi.com.cn/book/sh2_2019_msm_press_01/pdf/1492.pdf) gives Ø8 × 15, **28.6 N/mm**, nominal 123 N at 4.3 mm and 137 N at 4.8 mm. This is a spring, not a rated assembly. Choose 4.30 ± .15 mm initial compression, scale rate by .90–1.10 as a trial incoming-gauge band, permit .10 mm guide/part lost travel, and allocate 3.60 mm internal motion. Test a **6 g effective moving mass** with a 4 mm loaded guide span. Spring line offset ≤.25 mm, tilt ≤1°, guide μ=.23, .7 N base drag; the extra side load in the table is the sum of loaded guide normals **after** guide leverage, not the external body load. All are study inputs. No shared drivetrain inertia or damper is hidden in the mass. An inertia reflected through a motor or nut could dominate it; a cocking screw would have to be physically disconnected.

The fixed-frame one-axis lower-drive equation is m s″=K(d−s)−D, K=.90k[cos θ−μ(sin θ+2e/L)], D=.7+μN. Integrate from zero speed with the constant lower net-force envelope; if it starts and the work integral stays positive, t≤acos(1−s/(d−D/K))√(m/K). A negative end force need not preclude traversal on momentum. This comparison assumes no latch rub, adhesion, obstruction or unbounded contact, **not** proof that such a run is available.

**Stop and rebound are not small.** With maximum spring rate, 4.45 mm initial compression and no loss, available work at 3.60 mm is **300.1 mJ**. At that corner a .30 mm terminal buffer alone must absorb about 1.0 kN *mean* even before the remaining spring work. With a rigid rebound, the bar can immediately re-enter the aperture; a command to park, a limit switch, or waiting for a pawl to fall is not a one-way stop. A compliant captured brake needs an energy, travel, reaction, engagement and subsequent reset budget, including any earlier spring relaxation. No such stop has been packaged. There is no rated launch, impact or hardware test here.

## What moves besides the fork?

* **Neighbour motion.** Start with .111 mm minimum slot-side margin after the former seated error debit. Conditional free-body calculation: two carriers each ≥54 g, minimum principal inertia ≥5.4×10⁻⁶ kg m², lever ≤12 mm, and on *each* a net force ≤2.942 N, COM moment ≤.060 N·m. These are illustrative lower inertias and external-wrench bounds, not inherited from a 60 g **total** mass allocation. For planar motions from rest the relative side displacement due to those wrenches is at most ½[Σ(F/M+rT/I)]t². The slower 62 N guide case spends **.114 mm** even in this deliberately simple bound. It fails a sufficient inequality, not a proved collision. In the zero-extra-load case .094 mm is spent; remaining possible initial kinetic energy in the worst point-velocity direction is only **.0030 mJ**, from v²≤2EΣ(1/M+r²/I). A mere 32 N preload released linearly over .05 mm stores .80 mJ elastically; it need not all enter this mode, but putting *none* there needs evidence. For free three-axis rotation the angular acceleration also has a gyroscopic term; do not use the planar estimate as a 3D guarantee. Face contacts and floor impulses are not bounded by the external applied loads.
* **Internal recoil.** The independent exact planar zero-external-wrench calculation in [retreat.py](retreat.py) conserves linear and angular momentum. A 6 g slider in a 54 g body uses only **3.24 mm** of a 3.60 mm stroke in world X for a centered free module, before lost travel. Off-axis motion rotates the carrier. At 4 mm COM offset and 3 mm internal travel, the forward pole has retreated only 2.661 mm and moved .113 mm sideways; it has not yet cleared the face 2.90 mm behind its initial position. These are distinct free/fixtured scenarios, **not** superposed solutions: seats can react recoil and the carrier is neither a fixed ground nor necessarily in free flight. Actual contacts could improve or worsen the slot margin. A matched countermass would also need space, matched inertia, timing, its own guides and an arrest.
* **Loss before the trigger.** With the proposed .20 mm pose debit, a 2.55×.55 mm half-section bar fits the short slot dimension only within **4.54°** of its aligned orientation. At 10° error the required half-width is 1.185 mm including that debit, against .95 mm available. The earlier 90° rotation therefore contains a finite interval where axial retreat cannot simply replace vacuum after loss of power or sealing. No pin, passive continuation, interlock or fallback clocking support has been demonstrated there. After full park a **different** outward-moving face still has to displace the neighbour. Even 3 mm against 5W at 60 g costs 8.83 mJ before guide or housing work; energy spent arresting the internal bar cannot be counted a second time.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 PYTHONHASHSEED=0 python3 -B retreat.py`. Do not transfer a millisecond estimate to the transverse plug: there the driven mass can include the neighbour, not just the captive slider. This rejects the proposed timing argument, not finite-time release in general, and changes no gate inputs.
