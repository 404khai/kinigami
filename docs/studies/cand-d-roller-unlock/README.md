# Take 81 — can the coaxial tri-roller sleeve actually release?

**Exploratory; reject the current installation.** Take 82's trial cage already intersects the tail at DRAW. More sleeve thrust cannot drive one rigid solid through another. Removing the colliding rails does not establish release: the sleeve opens radial *space*, not radial roller travel. A real table-load breakaway equilibrium needs 22.06 N at the sleeve on a rail-free interface; the frozen 19.10 N is not a bound. A 360° ring prevents the simple narrow-ram miss on a centered tail but does not keep it upright. There is no demonstrated stroke/force guaranteeing clean, persistent separation for the frozen design.

## Loaded unlock: four interfaces, not a drag multiplier

Tail/roller, sleeve/roller (flat **and** chamfer), cage axial wall/roller, and cage sidewall/roller. `roller.py` uses separate 6-DOF equilibrium for each rigid cylinder and for the tail; 12 inscribed Coulomb rays per sticking site for witnesses, both ends of each generator, inscribed contact polygon on an end face. Normal compression on front or opposed cage faces can generate both radial drag and a cocking moment. Slip on the unlocking sleeve flat is −x relative to a stationary, not freely rolling cylinder. Reaction on the roller is −μx−r per sleeve normal. Static Coulomb cone realizations are admissible load states; a particular realized load requires stiffness, tolerances and history. No assumption that all cylinders carry charge.

**Real guide reaction example** (`bearing.json`). Parallel sleeve offset +.050y actually touches both circular guide bands on +y, so their drag has direction +x on the sleeve. Push/pull through sleeve centerline; supports at −14.30 and −6.85. Tail translation (+.0015,+.050,0); finite V planes adjusted by the reported signed offsets (positive = protruding toward ball); the +.04595 offset would be impossible in the nominal budget, so that face is open. Negative extreme on the opposite face uses the independent extra .020 recess. Roller centers shift radially by .020+.050cos(clock), x=−4.2185; cage midpoint −4.2435; t=+.030 places the +t end on its wall. This is a **different tolerance realization** from the pure .020 recess in the next section. Sum actual tail axial pull ≤45. All 30 force/moment equations are checked (residuals in JSON); the sleeve itself, not just a free cylinder, balances.

Shaft collar, seals, interference, cage tilt and housing compliance remain separate.

**Can the roller self-wedge once the sleeve is out of contact?** On the near-tangential, uncut-cylinder component of this rectangular window, not by the four planar cage walls alone while a 45° shoulder is driving it outward. Projected width normal to i is 3(|uᵢ|+√(1−uᵢ²)) for axis u; play permits axial skew .963° and total tilt about 1.158° (approximate). Opposing side contacts are possible. In the aligned case at most one wall per axis is charged: radial resultant lower bound (1−μ)/√2 − μ[(1+μ)/√2+μ]/(1−μ) = +.216 per unit tail normal. In the skew case, `wedge.py` covers all tilt azimuths by 64 intervals, with two extra exact zero-axial-skew cases. It encloses cylinder support locations in boxes: ±x supports go to opposite t ends, and sidewall support projects to 1.5(cosθ−sinθ)(cosψ,sinψ). It permits both walls of each pair even where that overcounts. The tail site is relaxed to the whole cylinder box, its force cone enlarged by 2° relative error. All friction cones are circumscribed. In every nonzero-tilt cell an independent dual twist has strictly positive dot product with **every** outer wrench ray (smallest re-evaluated margin .0459 with each twist coordinate bounded by 1 in these units). Thus no nonzero combination balances a free roller. This excludes a *particular static Coulomb wedge* under ideal rigid faces; it neither proves a monotone dynamic path nor excludes burrs, rail/cheek corners, end damage, sleeve contact or press-fit forces. `roller.json` also contains the separate finite samples with the sleeve still present at 0/8/20/45°. At 45° the radial pressure assists sleeve retraction even with μ=.23; peak pull need not occur there.

At 8° the exact roller-center offset is 1.75(1−cos8°)=.0170 and the contact lies on the actual starting arc. For E=210 GPa, ν=.30, full L=3: .580 GPa on a flat becomes **1.534 GPa** on the blend (half-width .00570); maintaining that charge requires tail/cage compliance or motion. If effective axial length falls to .75, the screening value doubles. Just .5° line misalignment makes .0262 end-to-end offset versus elastic scale b²/(2R*)=.000076. Rigid sharp-ended tilted cylinders contact at an edge, sometimes on the cap/axial wall too: no finite allowable peak follows without crown/end radii, hardness, elastic pressure solution, fatigue limits and damage tests. ## Are the fixed lands actually retained?

`lands.py` uses the 80 recess/angle budgets (.025 V, .040 roller, .60°/1.00°) plus an **additional** .020 normal recess on one V. Six independent coordinates; nine unilateral *available* inequalities, not nine simultaneous loaded contacts. The cylinder contains an R1.50 insphere regardless of axis skew. Use it to witness nonpenetration on the finite shoulder; the actual end may load sooner. With rotation envelope 1.20° and displacement bootstrap H=.250, the 3 rotating shoulder inequalities include the inverse-frame correction ≤2sin(β/2)H omitted by the frozen screen. The 84 six-row subsets yield 27 vertices. Both trial neighborhoods close strictly inside H and β, bounding the connected LOCK component, not all free space.

## Driven take-up is not a seat sensor (takeup.json)

Sequence for a trial replacement only: clear the ejector, present the pin, command sleeve to the straight lock land while the cage is forward. **With sleeve positively held**, drive the *cage* aft under a series force limit; then hold the cage independently of motor power. The front axial cage wall pushes the finite rollers; the sleeve supplies inward normal, the 45° tail surfaces draw the pin into the fixed six V faces, and their reactions return via the barrel. No spring drives sleeve closure. The nominal load path does not engage at window midpoint −4.20: the .020 radial play requires −.020 axial movement on the 45° shoulder, plus .025 to the front axial wall, so midpoint **−4.245**, .245 aft of reset. A hard stop at nominal DRAW misses it. With a .025/cos65° axial-equivalent V recess plus .040/sin45° at a shoulder and a **trial only** 150 N/mm axial force element, .661 powered travel allows .245 nominal take-up + .116 gap allowance + .300 to reach 45 N; this is a one-axis stack, not acquisition from arbitrary tilt. Force element downstream of the screw, physical end stops outside the useful range, independent cage/sleeve travel and actual transmitted-force observation needed. A screw backdrive calculation does not hold a disconnected cage, and a spring in the draw stage cannot be credited with closing the sleeve.

On a centered all-contact rigid incipient draw branch, the stationary sleeve applies **+μx** to cylinders moving aft; static tail and cage contacts do not use that slip direction. The physical cage pull (before its own guide losses) is tail pull **plus** flat drag. All 64 corners of each box admit equilibrium with ≥.10 N on each available tail contact for 8.14 / 14.07 / 8.61 N cage drive (service/table/fault); minima use dry friction and are not guaranteed loads, initial approach forces or assembly proofs. In contrast even an **actual** 45 N cage pull with zero external wrench and face 0 recessed .020 admits **39.41 N tail draw + 5.59 sleeve drag**, with face 0 uncharged. End-of-drive load alone can falsely declare full seating. It does not defeat the separate local lost-motion bound. Positive powered unload/reverse, individual park confirmation or true geometric interlock, tolerated cage end guidance, full stop-free CAD, strength, wear, guide drag and instrumented forced exit must precede any hardware claim.
