# Take 43 — Can a rolled tail touch the parked button?

**Result: exploratory.** A closed, finite hood can isolate the parked button, root and a short internal bridge from the **entire** specified tail at any attitude. Lowering the interface 7 mm on both units gives the hood 0.753 mm nominal room inside the trial 60 mm sphere. The separate Take 44 drive bridge **does not fit** this hood. Keeping metal off the parked pin is also not the same as making all external contacts assist ejection: the straight slot has a reachable lateral face for the thinner stem.

## Whole-shape proof, including the open front

Use the outer polytope as a mathematical separator; only the pieces specified above are material. Its boundary openings are the lower slot and the central front notch up to L+.40. For each crossing point a=(x,y,z) in either opening, both p±=(x,±3.225,z) are **actual metal**, even at the forward end. This is why a front lintel above the through-slot matters. The closed rear prevents a bypass at x=9.649.

Every tail point q has a straight **material** segment to the centre c of a generator R4.20 ball. For points in a torus or boss ball the segment is radial. For a neck cylinder choose the nearer end; its capsule is convex and both centreline endpoints are ball centres. The maximum length is √((length/2)²+2²), also covering the hemispherical ends. For the stem, use only its torus end. Its far terminal does **not** have R4.20 curvature.

## Insertion, ejection and the distinction between reachable states

**Aligned translation is possible.** For the tail at home translated by any t≥0 along +X, the infinite lower and outer side planes are conservative separators. The ring, lower boss, lower neck and stem are below L; the upper bosses and necks lie outside ±B. Signed gaps increase with t. The smallest are .159 for the ring, .0478 for an upper boss, .0633 for an upper neck. All tail material is at least .730 below the parked tip vertically. Thus the nominal aligned entrance is the reverse of a clear +X exit. This is a continuous collision check, *not* a constraint that the tail follows that motion.

**Actual trial V faces.** At boss b use local u along (0,−b_z,b_y)/hypot(b_y,b_z) and v along (0,b_y,b_z)/hypot(b_y,b_z). Machine x=b_x−4.20√2+|u| over |u|≤3.8, |v|≤1.5, with .40 backing in −x; these three pieces travel with the face. Contact is on two flank interiors at |u|=4.20/√2, v=0, not a fabricated circular envelope. No fixed front land. With μ=.23 and 1° normal error either working face gives ≥.5292 +X work per unit normal. Geometric tangency does not imply equal loads or even six loaded contacts. The script measures exact distance to each V cross-section swept aft (all relative +X tail offsets), then subtracts a centreline arc/segment sampling error using the 1-Lipschitz distance bound. Nonpartner stock clears by ≥1.226; the actual V plates clear the fixed hood at home and for +X plate travel by ≥.266. These numbers omit the V carrier, mounts, tilt, and drive.

**Outer wedge versus inner slot.** The lower plane, two outer sides, upper plane and front plane all have outward normals with positive +X work after friction and 1° error: respectively ≥.2491, .001495, .01113, .9958 N/N. Convex blends whose normals remain in the cone spanned by those normals preserve the minimum. This excludes the aft closing plane, interior walls and nonconvex edges. The straight lower lip for an R4.20 ball bridging intact parallel rails has n_x≥sin(28°)√(1−(3.225/4.20)²); with the stated debits this yields **.063538**, not a strict .0636. At the blind end the slot facing +X is distinct from the external rear of the whole hood. Neither a lip bound nor a V flank bound applies to a rear, side or rim merely because it belongs to the same part.

**Concrete lateral contact.** Use units t=(cos28°,0,sin28°), v=(0,1,0), n=(−sin28°,0,cos28°); b=.25, c=√(1−b²). Map tail axes to (c n+b v, t, c v−b n). Place its terminal centre at x=14.25, y=3.225−2.2, h=.15. Its R2.2 cap is tangent to the **inside vertical wall** y=3.225 in the .40 plate (0<h<.40 cos28°); the rest of its stem stays within the slot and misses both ends. The script checks continuous ring, necks and bosses against supersets of real skin with sample-error debits; the nearest other tail solid clears the hood by ≥.216. The nominal wall force on the stem has n_x=0, so dry friction can oppose +X by .23 N/N. A small translation away in −y opens this contact.

This pose intersects the bottom V with the face at home: **not** a seated jam. With the three example plates relieved 6 in −X, it clears the actual finite plates by .633 and the floor by 2.751 at the 7 mm lowering. That relief is a test configuration, not a specified actuator stroke; rear carrier/housing and the remainder of the mating body may further restrict access. The witness disproves universal positive work for an exposed slotted hood. It does **not** establish access from the mated state before separation. Arbitrary later recontact alone is not an ejection failure.

**Workable next boundary.** Keep the enclosed pocket and two intact endpoints at every opening. For unconditional +X work on this hood after park, one option is a flush load-bearing shutter filling the lower slot and closing the notch, with no shelf facing −X and the aft seam buried. Then only the positive-work exterior is exposed; this requires a collision-checked shutter deployment, measured closure and an insertion/withdrawal interlock, none demonstrated here. Alternatively keep the passive slot and establish, from real seat/fork/carrier topology, that the thin stem cannot reach its vertical walls **before** separation. Neither option is an excuse to impose an external pose bound.
