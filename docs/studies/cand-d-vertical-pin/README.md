## Audit of Take 38 — the continuum exception and finite tips

For a straight capsule generator at s, nearest ring-spine point c=C+Ru is in the direction of the projection of s−C onto the ring plane. At tangency |c−s|=S and t=|projection|≥R−S. Thus **for R>S it is unique even at a hemispherical tip**. If the projected length vanishes, the distance to every spine point is at least R, so there is no contact here. At S=R (centre at C), or S>R (s on the axis at height √(S²−R²)), an appropriately exposed endpoint ball *can* have a circle of equidistant tangencies. Do not extend the proof to those sizes.

Along a straight pin parameter v the squared nearest distance is |s−C|²+R²−2R√(d²+A²(v−v₀)²), A=sinθ with θ the acute angle between pin and eye axes. Its second derivative is 2−2R A²d²/[d²+A²(v−v₀)²]^(3/2). Except for isolated roots there are at most two outer convex intervals and one intervening concave interval. No constant interval: for A=0 the distance squared is quadratic; for d=0 it is piecewise quadratic with a downward cusp, which cannot be a minimum there. Hence at most two equal global minima on the physical generator segment, possibly at an endpoint. Only one end is exposed here. Take 38's spacing identity remains valid: writing n=(c−s)/S=a u+b k, k the ring axis, t=R−Sa gives |Δs|≥min(S,R−S)|Δn|. A planar wall, on the other hand, **can touch a whole ring or arcs of it**. Take 38 bounded pin loads from an external wrench; substituting a wall force that itself depends on those loads would be circular.

**Stronger finite-segment inequality.** Two minima at s₁,s₂ in axis direction e obey e·(n₂−n₁)≥0 by the endpoint derivative signs. With L=|s₂−s₁| this implies L≤R sinθ |Δu|. Projecting their separation into the ring plane gives L sinθ≥√(t₁t₂)|Δu|≥(R−S)|Δu|. Therefore **R cos²θ>S rules out two pin contacts at any axial offset or stroke, including the shank/tip transition.** Threshold 27.2747° for R5/Ø2.50, 34.1659° for R6.50/Ø3.50. Equality is not included. The argument is for rigid, straight spines; the pointwise bounded-offset force test below need not count contacts.

## No-shunt force certificates

Let a contact on the tail have inward unit normal n and unknown normal load N. Its worst projection on a unit translation v is N[cosγ−μ sinγ], γ=angle(v,n), minimized over any angular uncertainty. If this is ≥gN, g>0 at *all* contacts, equilibrium gives **ΣN≤Bᵥ/g** with Bᵥ=max(0,−F_external·v) or its bound. Integrals over contact patches obey the same result if each local normal is covered. Moments may further restrict equilibrium, never invalidate this force inequality. It is independent of the x reaction on the pin. It neither predicts actual loads nor requires moving the eye along v.

**Intact near plane vs a single pin contact.** Near-wall normal (+x−z)/√2. All ideal pin normals on the eye have n_z≤0. The worst angle between pin and face normals is 135°; their bisector yields g≥.15250 after the debit. Multiple face tangencies have identical normal. Consequently external force alone bounds all these reactions when θ<34.1659°. With *no* roof or other contact, the single contact has N≤|F_external| directly. This is not a bound for a rear corner, hole edge or thin stem.

**Open port.** All interior-ball centres at true lip tangency have orthogonal projection in the elliptical opening or on its rim, else the intact plane would be nearer. Projecting back into xy, their centre radius about the pin axis is at most h+r sin45°=3.75919. A full shank at that same height is impossible because S=4.45. The cap centre must sit ≥√(S²−3.75919²)=2.38139 above that particular spine point (or no pin remains). For θ≤5°, the other spine points rise at most 2R sinθ. Any simultaneous pin contact is therefore on the cap and has downward component ≥.2805 (nominal); this is a *different-constituent* bound.

For the circular xy bore, in the wall plane the ellipse has semiaxes A=h sec45°, B=h. At rim parameter u the centre's inward in-plane offset λ is ≤B²√((cos u/A)²+(sin u/B)²), by comparing opposite-y rim points (at the x vertex by curvature). Thus worst tilt away from −z gives a downward latitude ≥45°−asin[(h/r)cos45°]=16.0203°; same minimum latitude toward +x, by reflection. For example, the adverse axial in-plane component is ≤(B²/A)|cos u|; inserting the correlated λ in √(r²−λ²) gives its minimum directional projection at |cos u|=1. A ball bigger than h cannot touch deep concave cylindrical bore wall from inside without local penetration. Intact rim stock, no undercuts/enlarged breaks and pocket-side access are essential. With a lip contact and θ≤5°, **v=−z** gives ≥.03702 N/N at the lip and .04188 at the cap. Simultaneous intact plane contacts also pass. This does not assume a small pin-x shunt. An arbitrary outer edge does not inherit this normal.

## Stock where the result changes

**Aft edge versus ejection.** With cleared pin and aligned eye C=(15,0,2.520067), the torus actually touches both corners (9.75,±3.8,5.22). At each corner ρ=hypot(5.25,3.8)=6.480934, C_z=5.22−√(r²−(R−ρ)²). These are first contacts from below: near x=9.75 the distance to the spine is minimized at maximal |y| because ρ<R, and increases with x up to x=10; beyond x=10 the vertical gap alone exceeds r. The bore is far away. n_x=−.00572, so +x virtual ejection work can be **−.23572 N/N** at μ=.23. In contrast the opening into the interior of the circular port is +x favourable, and a true pure +x front face is favourable. This is not a proof of ejection stall; it vetoes treating *all* of the finite roof as a forward draft. Check actuator force, edges and a positive separation end condition.

**Why deleting the far cheek matters.** For comparison only, a far extended 45° wall with m=(1,0,1)/√2 can take n_pin=−m on one rear tube ball as a hemispherical tip withdraws; with q=s−(S+r)m on the wall both contacts have an arbitrary equal, opposite and collinear compressive reaction, zero net tail wrench. The entire horizontal ring rests clear because that spine point is the most rearward; the same spine point is nearest to the elevated pin endpoint. R5/Ø2.50 about x15 and z=−x+6.03 gives q_x=10.29774. For the R6.50 trial the point would be x=7.89419, **outside** the x≥9.75 package roof. This particular squeeze is removed by a slender terminal that stays in front of that point; it is not a veto on that terminal. Its reaction on the pin points +z, **with** withdrawal; a large normal isn't automatically large adverse draw. A thinner convex far nose gives positive ejection only on faces satisfying m_x>μ/√(1+μ²); its pole, rounded side and accessible backside are not the same domain. It still needs real reach and guide-moment checks. The priority branch physically omits this far assembly; none of these forces are transferred into its budget.

**Excluded parts.** Ring contact ⇒ a constituent radius-r ball exists. This is not generally true of a kinematic boss sharp blend, electrical terminal, locking shoulder, stem root or adjacent housing. Include them as real solids, prove they cannot touch over the monitored domain, or bound their actual full cones in the same test. No claim that the recessed seat or the rest of a neighbouring Kin is unreachable just from the eye calculation. Near and far cheek simultaneous contact cannot be removed by a nominal face-to-face gap without testing the whole tilted solid; the principal branch has no lower cheek at any pose.

## Cantilever and withdrawal load

Illustrative steel E=205 GPa; circular I=πp⁴/4. No yield, life, edge hardness or safety factor established. 121 N is a *specified illustrative transverse load* for the centred case; for multiple contacts the numbers below require an actual **sum of normals ≤121 N**. For central pure transverse F and ideal clamp at z=12: σ=F Lp/I=344.96 MPa, δ=F L³/(3EI)=.04615 mm. With the actual trial near face at 12.55: **360.77 MPa, .05280 mm, .36155°**. The bend at the forward bore rim x=14.80,z=10.27 is only .00246 nominal in that ideal fixed-root model; unlike Take 38 there is no far port. Real bearing compliance and cocking may dominate.

For contact i let L_i=z_root−s_iz. Normal moment at root ≤N_i L_i√(1−n_iz²); friction moment ≤μN_i|s_i+p n_i−root|. For a beam station H≥L_i use the cantilever influence coefficients L_i²(3H−L_i)/(6EI) per transverse unit force and L_i(2H−L_i)/(2EI) per unit couple. Bound the transverse force per normal by √(1−n_iz²)+μ, and the offset friction couple by pμ. No sharing assumption: take the largest coefficient times ΣN. At the mixed witness:

|---|---:|---:|
| shank (L=3.960) | 142.48 | .00707 |
| cap (L=9.253) | 322.47 | .02673 |

Apply the larger per-unit bound to any simultaneous allocation, *not* 121 N to each contact. Across the whole short stroke L≤16.80 to the deepest nose generator, the coarse one-sided bounds rise to **507.12 MPa / .13451 mm** at that generator with ΣN=121; this does not establish the .08 offset domain. A bent/clearance-loaded shank isn't an ideal clamped beam and edge pressure is unresolved.

**Single short journal:** central F=121 acts at z=0 in +x; the fixed near and far ends of the *same* sleeve can react opposite sides. For an ideal dry sliding two-edge bearing of effective separation b=2.55 and root lever L=12.55, N_near=F+N_far. With centred +z drive and eye drag coefficient j, moment balance including rim friction gives N_far=F(L−pμ+pj)/b. If the eye applies **no** axial friction, required guide drag alone in this model is **292.98 N** (697.41 and 576.41 N normal). With j=.23 the guide plus eye requires **329.59 N**. This is a statically admissible limiting case with the corresponding external tail wrench, not a measured start force or a universal upper bound for the installed guides. A clamp as a strength boundary is not a frictionless slider. A guided carrier with larger actual support span changes this calculation. Check the true drive line, sleeve edge/port contacts, friction and preload over travel. Without measuring them there is no specified drive or pass.
