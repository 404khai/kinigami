# Take 18 — central sphere, three positively opened jaws

**Result: exploratory. Build a gripper coupon, not a candidate.** Separating weak radial closure from a metered axial draw makes the closed wrench plausible. A rounded, radially monotone jaw also gives an opening-force bound even if a tilted **neck** hits it. That bound ends at a hard stop. A free 3D translation leaves one jaw retaining the sphere at the nominal 2.45 mm open stop; a different pair of stopped jaws admits self-stress. An actual guard and coordinated positive ejection are necessary. This is the B3.20 **three**-jaw proposal, not the B2.30 two-jaw packaging study. Q85/Q100 unchanged; no Gate 1 or physical result.

## Geometry and sign convention

x is separation, r radially outward along each jaw, t local tangent. Three identical jaws at 120°. Tail: central sphere B=3.200±.015 mm, union with a capsule of radius .98–1.02 whose axis starts at the sphere centre and ends ≤4.60 mm away toward the tail. The neck can be a turned integral part; a flat shoulder, threaded fastener protruding from the sphere or joint between ball and neck is **not** this solid. Exposed union surfaces have interior rolling balls of radius ≥.98; the flange is separate and must remain outside the jaws. Globe orientation does not change the globe's contact point; it changes where the neck goes.

At outward displacement s make a metal jaw from this convex core, offset externally by a ball of radius .800±.020:

    r >= rv+s + max(k*abs(x-xv), ell*(abs(t)-a))
    xv=3.436608, rv=2.065802, k=cot(35°), ell=1.40, a=.480 mm.

Clip the **offset solid** at r≤4.80+s, not the core before offsetting. The two meridional planes meet at a rounded nose; the tangential ends taper back and blend, not straight cutoffs. At lock the nominal front pole on the sphere is B(cos35°, sin35°, 0). The tip radius from the head axis is 1.266. On t=0 the inflated tip for the sphere has radius D=B+.8=4.000 and centre (xv,rv+s). The lower bound on centre r needed to contact, allowing arbitrary t, is rv+s−sqrt(D²−u²) for |u|≤D cos35°, else rv+s+k|u|−D sqrt(1+k²), u=x−xv. It is conservative because finite side facets only **remove** solid from the infinite meridional extrusion. Inflate by neck radius, not globe radius, for each individual rolling neck point. Gauge xv ±.015, rv ±.030, a ±.020, physical front-normal error ≤.5°, total force-direction debit 1°. These are proposed all-in limits, not measured process capabilities.

Let m denote the unit normal **into the jaw**, and N its normal load. Every working facet satisfies m·r≥sin35°; convex combinations and the rounded transitions preserve that circular cone. Including the direction debit and arbitrary 3D Coulomb friction μ≤.23 gives f_jaw·r ≥ N(sin34°−.23cos34°) = **.36851 N per newton of normal** throughout, not just at lock. It holds for contact by sphere, neck, or multiple points on these faces; sum their normals. It does not hold on the outer clipping plane, its rim, a cut side wall, follower, stock, or back of a guide. Removing rear stock is harmless only if a swept inequality proves the new boundary cannot be touched. Cutting a vertical tangent side into a reachable nose can reduce the radial projection to zero.

## Meter and back the jaws; draw the whole cage separately

1. With the draw off, independently close each jaw through a force-capped spring (≤1.20 N carriage-side inward force even on a blocked blade; do not bottom it). Use positive capture/withdrawal dogs. Three gauged metal radial backs engage at the matched closed geometry only; a premature stop or missing independent jaw witness blocks both backing and draw. A spring does **not** hold a jaw closed unpowered.
2. Withdraw/isolate the locating contacts and close-drive reactions. Pull the locked cage axially with active torque-limited advance through a calibrated metal cartridge; park the input mechanically. A hard tensile shoulder catches abnormal travel, not a fourth normal carrier in the rated range. Require series stiffness 30–32 N/mm over **measured loaded** compression 1.310–1.390 mm with ≤.25 N net metering/hysteresis loss: actual jaw draw **39.05–44.73 N**. No inference from turns/current. Every loaded tolerance/deflection state must remain off the hard shoulder and opposite spring solid height; otherwise this load cap is gone. The spring meters draw, not radial latch closure.
3. On release keep deep draw on the three fully backed **flats only**. Positively unwind the axial cartridge first, measure its residual and rear-seat location, then withdraw each outer backing flank. The opening pickup cannot hold an assisted jaw inward: jaws may lead independently. Start positive axial ejection while they are still free to lead, after every backing path has been removed. Do not run the jaws into radially rigid open stops on the strength of an open command.

**Why not just squeeze harder?** On a seated flat with angle β, draw per unit of actual inward radial reaction is between cot(β+atan μ) and cot(β−atan μ). Three independently gauged 9.5–10.5 N radial forces at β=34–36° give 24.82–81.86 N axial, and ≤28.50 N normal at one site. Across friction signs even the best 45° setting has a 2.552:1 window. The clamp also has side force and torque; the full normal passes through the sphere centre, the friction does not. Match to the lead's ~38.20 N correction / 36.96 N fault draw targets **with** their parasitic budget, not to their centered 15.89 N service figure.

**Conditional floating cage.** During draw the only substantial interface between head and cage is a nominally axial, two-joint tension member. Close/open dogs and fixed guides must be disengaged or co-move without reacting load to ground. The example budgets axial pivot spacing ≥7 mm, transverse endpoint mismatch ≤.070 mm, each pivot resisting moment ≤.40 N·mm about **any** axis, axial link force ≤45 N. Fixed pivot within .020 mm laterally and 12 mm axially of Maxwell's triangle centre. All remaining cables/returns together contribute ≤.25 N lateral and ≤.90 N·mm *about that triangle centre*. From moment equilibrium of the link, lateral reaction ≤(.070·45+.80)/7=.565 N; including returns **≤.815 N lateral, ≤8.98 N·mm total moment**. The latter includes 12×the lateral link force, off-axis axial thrust and fixed-joint friction. This is inside the stated 2/10 budgets, without assuming a centered resultant or equal jaw loads. A larger float is not always better: .20 mm endpoint mismatch gives a 19.0 N·mm bound. No compact joint, parked dog or allowance is demonstrated; measure all six wrench components, including torsion.

**Closed front forces and confinement.** On fully backed regular flats f_x≥.67382 N per unit normal. With no rear contact on the floating cage, sum N≤66.39 N. For one jaw, its own radial projection ≥.36851N, the other two each project no less than −.524N along that same axis (120° spacing, 1° error and full Coulomb cone). Using the actual resultant lateral limit gives **≤39.87 N on one jaw**. Even two bearing flats together project ≥1.82 N along their common bisector at minimum draw, exceeding the permitted .815 N; an equilibrated gauged three-front state cannot lose one while those conditions hold. This does not establish self-acquisition.

Require each actual front plane distance from nominal ball centre minus actual B, after backing, indentation and setup, within ±.011 mm. This needs matched gauges/shims; not an assertion that unmatched ±.015 spheres meet it. For an all-bearing state, m_i·O=e_i. The three equiangular radial rows give |O_x|≤E/cos35°, |O_yz|≤4E/(3sin35°), E=.011+2sin(.25°)|O|. Hence .01375 mm axial/.02618 lateral. At every such centre the front contact is ≥.142 mm along the flat from the meridional blend, ≥.231 mm from the side constraint under the stated tolerances. For actual **relative** neck tilt ≤1° the tip-to-neck margin is ≥.089 mm. Verify neck/flange pose from the **closed** mated section, not head form error. Measured positive pull on some unrelated seat is not evidence of three flat contacts.

## Unload, free lead and ejection

**Unloading with all backs home.** A separately supported rear striker can act as a one-sided anti-retreat plane without returning load through the floating cage. Keep its sphere-centre limit O_x ≥−.030 mm in the *moving cage frame* for the entire unloading interval; require actual clearance when fully drawn. A candidate hard setting interval −.030 to −.020 leaves only .006 mm minimum slack at the seated negative corner; do not silently absorb this by interference. From a gauged seated state try the boundary |O_yz|≤.25, |O_x|≤.030. Any violated front-plane inequality in this region is a real penetration: projection onto the core plane has x≤.030+(Bmax+ρmax)cos34.5° < xv_min and |t|≤.25+(Bmax+ρmax)sin.5° < a_min, so it lies on the finite facet. Thus all three nonpenetration inequalities apply even with missing contacts. Summing bounds x≤.01612. Some site projects ≥one half of the lateral shift; that inequality bounds lateral shift ≤.13171. Neither can leave the trial box with the independent rear plane present. Any bearing sphere contact here is on the front flat with ≥.138 mm meridional margin. The **neck** additionally needs .13171+4.60sin(tilt)+1.02 < rv_min−ρmax, i.e. tilt <.798° or a more detailed swept bound. The sphere/striker proof does not constrain this tilt. A striker attached to the draw cage can introduce internal opposition and invalidate the force cap.

Go/no-go for a backed-to-free transfer: first demonstrate ≤5 N *actual* residual pull while the above restrictions hold (not guaranteed by undoing 1.39 mm). Then sum front normals ≤7.43 N, total front outward reaction ≤5.75 N before backing withdrawal, plus any jaw springs, pins and friction. Even at zero spring preload an external 4W pull does not disappear. While some backs remain and some jaws have already led, the three-home confinement no longer applies. For exactly **one** backed jaw and two free jaws the radial balance still gives ≤14.54 N on it irrespective of nose angle **only if** cage lateral reaction remains ≤.815 N, the same exposed normal cones hold and the free-jaw force limit below applies. A fixed follower re-entering the cage can invalidate this; two backed curved/side contacts need a separate certificate or positively guarded sequence. Do not assume that all three backs release at the same instant.

**Free blades.** Per jaw require measured worst guide/breakaway resistance ≤.55+.12 ΣN+.08D, D the magnitude of pickup or closing force, including eccentric contact/follower moments. This is a coupon requirement, not a dry-friction rating. During weak closure ΣN≤[.55+1.08·1.20]/(.36851−.12)=7.43 N; during free leading with closing bias removed, ΣN≤.55/(.36851−.12)=2.214 N at static balance. A positive pickup >.598 N per unloaded blade suffices under that drag model. No equal loading and no dependence on central-ball attitude in this inequality. It fails at an inward-reacting hard stop or reachable cut surface. An obstruction may stall and must be reported, not powered through.

**What the ejector actually needs.** For 50 g, 4W compression plus W table drag is 2.452 N. All Maxwell reactions can be credited with nonnegative outward projection only on the lead's certified outward contours. At k free-leading jaw contacts a *delivered* positive axial striker force >2.452+k·sqrt(1+.23²)·2.214 excludes static balance: **>9.265 N with three, >4.723 N with one**, plus internal drive losses. Monitor actual striker force, travel and local separation; keep a real supporting face under the sphere (a tiny fixed pin can miss it). The force bound is for quasi-static balance, not work guarantee, impact limit or cleared stroke. With one **stopped** jaw and *all other lateral reactions absent*, lateral equilibrium gives a different >4.254 N sufficient service push for ≤sqrt(2)W lateral. Do not use this if the funnel, Maxwell seats, another jaw or a guided striker can supply lateral force.

## Free-pose discriminators and extent of the solids

* **One stopped jaw still retains.** No tilt is needed. Translate the tail centre to (x,y=.5x) towards one jaw while all three open by s=(k+.5)x until x=.16. Continue around the rounded tip with x=xv−Dcosθ, y=.5x, s=y−rv+Dsinθ. At s=2.450: θ=65.287°, x=1.7643, y=.8822. One jaw still transmits **.209–.628 N inward axial per N** at the radial hard stop. The other two move away (their projected y is −y/2). The ideal 45° unilateral V faces strictly separate on this translation because x>|y|; tilt correction or a perfect coaxial tail was never imposed. This is a kinematically accessible contact, not evidence that every external force follows this path.
* **Two stopped jaws can pinch at zero net wrench.** At s=(D+.5a)/sin60°−rv=2.83013 the nearest rounded faces of a pair are exactly a globe diameter apart across their azimuthal bisector. At x=xv, bisector displacement 2.86366 mm, the sphere contacts opposite rounded edges. Each required meridional-zero normal has transverse/radial ratio 1/sqrt(3)<ell and belongs to the actual core-tip normal cone. Arbitrarily scaled equal opposite normals through the centre balance in the rigid static model, even with μ=0; that model alone gives no upper squeeze. Physical force depends on interference/compliance; no assertion of infinite actual force. Axial translation on centre with open jaws then bisector translation reaches this pose in the named unguarded section; x remains greater than the lateral shift. An installed guard could exclude it. A synchronized **bilateral** cam parked here is not an outward-free drive.
* **What more stroke buys.** Distance between fully parked rounded solids is ≥2[(rv_min+s)sin60°−a_max cos60°−ρmax], since their core edges diverge in r. At effective s≥3.05 a single sphere cannot touch two jaws. At ≥4.40 the 9.007 mm bound exceeds even the complete ball-plus-neck diameter 8.835; a tail flange is not included. Neither condition excludes one retaining jaw. After radial clipping at ≤4.83+s the working metal has x≤6.478. Sphere centre x>9.693 clears this **working metal** for the globe; >12.098 clears globe and capsule at any attitude. These are sufficient terminal extents, not necessary travel, and no stripped jaw stem, deck or external housing is included. A covered working profile may allow much shorter actual ejection; prove which positions and probe points are accessible.
* **Guard must clear the actuator too.** The six Ø1.30 rail trial with S=(B+.65+.16)sqrt(1+m²)+mx, ±30°: at x=xv take jaw material with local r=4.50, t=a+(4.50−rv)/ell+.60, within its rounded offset. At s=.221 (m=.35) or 1.100 (m=.55) this point lies only .093/.415 mm from a rail. Reject those placements. A longitudinal slot through r3.50 also needs >3.008 mm tangential width for the unrounded core alone, wider than Ø1.96 neck. The rolling-neck slot argument for a 45° wall debited 2° would require **<.982 mm**. The lead's three interleaved planar guard strips are separate; gauge jaw/stock interference and the neck at both sides and ends, not merely containment of the large sphere. An inaccessible rear cut is permissible; if it exposes a different normal cone, recalculate.

## Strength and next decision

Illustrative elastic Hertz, E*=115.4 GPa. Use load on **one** contact, not one third; round vertex worst-case local radii ρ_min=.780 and B_min=3.185. The front flat at 39.87 N gives 2.16 GPa, .094 mm contact radius. A fully backed vertex at that *same illustrative load* gives 6.40 GPa and is not rated. On the sphere vertex, weak closing 7.43 N gives 3.65 GPa; bare opening 2.214 N gives 2.44 GPa. For smallest rolling neck against that vertex use .98×.78, not B×.78: 4.66/3.12 GPa respectively. This screens crown geometry; it does not predict allowable brinelling, fatigue, wear, burrs or a fillet's material strength. A ground hardened integral neck, or a joined solution with qualified neck and retainer, needs actual material/process data and cycle tests. One 2.3×1.8 mm rectangular jaw root with 2.4 mm effective arm would see ~79 MPa simple bending before root concentration, edge torsion or reduction for a guard cut. A Ø1.96 neck with the demonstrated *net* moment plus 4.6S sees ~32 MPa axial+bending **if** that is the triangle-to-root reference; recompute with actual offset, not a one-sided free-stop load. Neither section nor the 7 mm draw link/inventory fits by assertion.

**Recommend one instrumented three-jaw test, not a freeze.** First demonstrate the parked load path, front contact patch and actual 39.05–44.73 N draw inside the 2 N/10 N·mm budgets. Hold the worst tilted globe/neck under 50 g service, deliberately delay one backing dog, unload and drive eject while the other jaws can lead. Measure all jaw positions, spring forces, striker position, output draw and separation; restart forward/reverse after removing an obstruction at each transfer. Reject if a backed tip/neck can load, the guide exceeds its bound, a leading jaw meets a stop while retaining, or the ejector loses contact. A power or torque trip is a fault, never lock/release evidence. No positive-recovery, all-part swept guard, strength, 60 mm fit or Gate 1 claim.
