# Take 18 continuation — single blade vs moving comb

**Exploratory, design author. Use the comb as a force/stiffness coupon, not as a packaged latch.** The large globe limits its own contact with one thin blade, but a tilted shaft can touch a straight blade side. Sister teeth on the *same rigid jaw* cure that normal-direction failure at interior cuts, without restricting tail attitude. A finite comb still needs an outer boundary, a grille it can actually traverse, controlled tooth deflection, and strength on one tooth, not the sum of their areas.

## Discriminator: one thin blade through a slotted hex

In local coordinates (x separates, r opens the jaw, t is transverse), take the 35° meridional wedge from the gripper study. Offset the wedge in x/r by q=.600±.015 mm, extrude to |t|≤.350±.010, then round in 3D by h=.200±.010. The fixed regular hex has slope .39–.41 and nominal centre intercept .16 mm for the B3.20 globe, ≤.19 worst case. On each adjacent **unslotted** facet the actual sphere centre satisfies a r ± b t ≤ g0+m x, a,b the direction cosines for 60±.5°. A longitudinal slot on the jaw facet has half-width ≤.640; neck rolling radius ≥.980. All allowances provisional; assume continuous facets, no reachable crosswise slot end and no unsupported free rim.

* **Parked:** the normal intrusion into that slot is ≤.238 mm. The longitudinal lip has n_x≥sin(atan .39) cos(asin(.640/.980)) minus .5° direction debit; friction leaves ≥.0447 outward N/N. An effective stroke >4.247 mm hides the entire aligned working blade, with .050 mm normal margin, relative to the tapered face. This is a sufficient stroke for the post-clear geometry, not an available package stroke.
* **Moving, globe only:** write outward normal from blade as (-z cosθ,-z sinθ,±v) with z²+v²=1, θ in [34.5°,145.5°]. Substituting its rolling-ball centre into the neighbouring unslotted plane gives b(B+h)v ≤ g0+m xv−a(rv+s)−b times the core half-width + [q+(B+h)z](a sinθ−m cosθ). Here “−b times the core half-width” is a single subtractand; use the most adverse tolerances and drop s≥0. A meridional facet point away from the apex only tightens this because m/k<a. On a pure side, maximize the same linear functional over the uncut rounded wedge instead. Segment enclosure in slot.py gives c=−n_r≥.3652 after 1° debit, hence ≥.1511 radial N/N with μ≤.23. This allows 17.66/59.25 N free/weak-close per jaw with the guide below; the largest normals need not retain axially. The angle-coupled free resistance is ≤2.280 N axial per jaw. A .19 transverse/.775 meridional radius corner model at those first two normal ceilings gives 7.72/11.56 GPa, not an allowable or necessarily an attained combination.
* **Moving, shaft:** nominal s=1.50, globe centre (3.60,−.35,1.53), shaft axis tilted 80° in the x/r plane, radius .98, length 4.60. The globe and the whole capsule fit even the *uncut* hex by convexity; the globe clears the jaw by .123 mm. The shaft at length 4.45 contacts the straight blade side **inside** its rounded meridional outline, 2.268 mm short of the back trim. The other jaws can be parked clear. Along that side n_r=0 and friction can oppose opening: a radial cap does not cap this side load. A shoulder behind the wall does not change this counterexample. This is a geometry witness, not a reconstructed full-body trajectory or proof of unbounded physical load.

## Comb geometry and signed force proof

For the **separate** comb variant use the broad convex core from grasp.md with k≤cot34.5°, side taper ell=.59–.60 and flat meridional land a=.480±.020. First round it by a common preoffset (nominal .70), giving K. Slice K into parallel transverse strips. Offset each strip by its own small h=.090–.100; the total uncut meridional radius stays within .780–.820. This construction models a real radius on the internal cut lip rather than assigning Hertz area to a mathematical corner. Regular teeth: nominal moving width .60, pitch .90, actual physical gap .24–.32. Nominal central core width .72 (gauge ≥.70 *excluding* the round), outer width .92 with h=.10. Shift the adjacent pitches to preserve physical gaps. Teeth share a positive drive and radial back; no independent equal-loading assumption. Assume μ≤.23, every reachable tail point belongs to a union of interior rolling balls p≥.95, and the flange/other hardware is either included or excluded by a sweep.

**Local geometric requirement.** At any cut with normal pointing into its gap, the next tooth must contain a core point V at transverse displacement G=.42–.52 from U on this cut, same x, with radial displacement at most ell G + E, E=.020 mm. An undeformed common profile permits V=U+G(±t+ell r): translating the original epigraph in that direction stays in it, as does its rounded offset. For real teeth require that this witness still exists at the specified positive radial displacement (not just at an arbitrary lower radius), in the local measured tooth frame. The bounds include stack, profile, tooth twist/deflection and sister phase error **under load**. The core strip normals must still respect the meridional limit in that frame, whose radial axis is within 1° of actual actuation. A missing tooth, clipped tip, burr, bending into the fixed ribs or reachable opposite surface is not a valid sister witness.

1. At a rolling contact the tail ball centre is O=U+(p+h_i)u, with |u|=1, c=−u_r≥0. The convex cut adds a pure ±t normal to the uncut cone, hence |u_x|≤k c. If the transverse normal points away from that cut, |u_t|≤ell c already.
2. On its gap side enforce |O−V|≥p+h_j: **nonpenetration of a single sister**, not a second assumed force. Expansion gives |u_t|≤a_s c + b_s where a_s=ell+E/G_min, b_s=G_max(1+a_s²)/(2(p+h_min))+(h_max−h_min)/G_min. The final term allows independent unequal edge radii. This uses no sphere-centre offset, tail angle, guard reaction or equality of forces.
3. Thus 1≤(1+k²)c²+(a_s c+b_s)². Solve the positive root; subtract 2 sin(.5°) for 1° alignment and debit full Coulomb traction. The resulting radial force floor q applies individually at every valid tooth contact, including multiple teeth on one jaw. Measured guide bound .55+.12 ΣN+.08D per jaw; a free-leading pickup cannot push inward. Therefore (q−.12)ΣN≤.55 with closing bias gone, or ≤.55+1.08×1.20 during force-limited closure.

| Any p≥.95; common h=.10, E=0 | .22537 | 5.22 N | 17.52 N |

The last row fails the .12 guide budget, not proof of a jam. At 50 g include 4W compression + W table drag. In each interval of c bound adverse axial force using |u_x|≤kc before direction debit and divide by the minimum corresponding radial reserve q(c)−.12. Summing these *pointwise* inequalities bounds axial retention by ≤5.586 N per free comb, including arbitrary load concentration. With all other contacts certified outward, sufficient delivered pushes with zero/one/three such jaws are **>2.452 / 8.037 / 19.208 N**, plus drive losses. This does not transfer to a stopped/inward-held jaw. Recovery requires measured unload, release of each radial back, a free inward pocket, positive pickup to a verified hidden stop, separately witnessed ejection, and reverse pickup after real obstruction removal; no such complete mechanism demonstrated.

## Finite teeth, stationary grille, parking

* Regular fixed ribs .16–.21 through .24–.32 moving gaps leave as little as **.015 mm per side** if centered, before relative pose/deflection. The previously budgeted floating link endpoint mismatch is not a no-rub proof. Gauge relative jaw/grille registration throughout draw, before handoff and under load; any touch at lock can bypass the limited draw-link wrench.
* With a widened central stationary slot ≤1.04 mm and all others no wider, p≥.95 gives sag ≤.155 mm. The straight generatrix lip retains ≥.0756 outward axial N/N after a .5° debit. Open working geometry hidden >.155+.050 behind the measured face is inaccessible in any attitude; a sufficient effective aligned parked stroke is >4.152 mm. Both results require complete, separated longitudinal lips, with no exposed ends, merged slots at the hex corners, deflecting ribs or flange not covered by the p bound.
* To preserve the sister test near the rear-stock trim, a physical trim face behind the local slotted plane by ≥sag+2h_max+(ell G_max+E)/sqrt(1+m_min²)+.050 = **.715 mm** suffices for that face: accessible contact → core U → translated sister V → its h-ball. A closer trim can silently remove the comparison point even when the tail cannot touch the trim itself. Stroke/trim estimates refer to the actual local frame; absorb cage rotation, axial travel and deflection or recompute.
* **Counterexample to simply extending the comb through the old hex:** at lock, the fourth regular outer tooth is centered at t=3.76. At x=3.436608 the adjacent unslotted 60° facet crosses it at r=3.449786. This is .5841 mm radially below the original core plane, *inside* the .70 preoffset and 1.350 mm inside the previous stock trim. Adjacent facet interference is real material on this branch, not a benign back-stock cut. If that tooth is omitted, the last flat cut has no neighbour. Prove a covarying natural sloped end with the same radial reserve, or prove all unmatched cut faces inaccessible to the entire rolling probe. More grille slots across a hex vertex change lip geometry; a wider three-face guard is a different package, not a demonstrated fix.

## Single-tooth strength and a bench decision

* **At lock:** keep structural draw on the central backed plane only. For a ≥.70 mm flat land, even the .131706 mm unloading transverse centre bound + B_max sin1° normal error + .020 mm registration + .0938 mm loaded Hertz patch radius leaves ≥.048 mm to its first cut. The original meridional flat-margin and neck/striker conditions still apply; apply matched plane gauging to the new central tooth. With those conditions all 39.87 N on this single tooth give 2.16 GPa on the flat. Were the same load allowed on a straight .09 mm lip the illustrative elastic peak would instead be 9.47 GPa. A rectangular 1.80×.70 mm central root, 4 mm arm, independent conservative component maxima, gives 697 MPa direct+bending **before** section concentration/twist. A wider supporting dog may reduce the arm only if its actual path is shown.
* **One reachable rounded lip:** at zero tail tilt, globe centre (1.25,0,.54), s=1.27777, central .72 mm core with .10 round. The sphere clears even the uncut hex by .192 mm radially and the next tooth by ≥.0105 mm; the axial neck and other open jaws clear. The globe is tangent only to the central tooth's outer toroidal lip. Its local radii are .10 and .70/cos(delta)+.10, sin(delta)=.18/3.30. For an imposed **1 N** normal the illustrative elliptic Hertz peak is **3.79 GPa**; the actual-angle closing ceiling of 3.133 N would give 5.55 GPa, not a demonstrated attained load. Global mixed-pose limits are higher. A p=.95 rolling sphere at the global 22.94 N on a straight .09 round would give 10.53 GPa; actual shaft-cylinder and tip curvatures must be used.
* **Flexure and ribs:** a trial 1.80×.60 mm steel strip (E=200 GPa), 4 mm cantilever and full 23.54 N resultant applied as a transverse sizing case deflects .0775 mm. To limit that case to .015 mm at this section requires an arm ≤2.31 mm, before root compliance; the illustrative elastic estimate alone does not prove the input gap/step. The ejection inequality gives very loose one-stationary-lip normal ceilings: up to 32.43 N parked, 254.02 N with three free jaws. Do not divide by the number of ribs, or assume a sharp stationary edge can survive it.

**Recommendation:** coupon only at ell≤.60. First gauge one loaded central flat, an isolated rounded cut lip, smallest rib gap and a deliberately delayed comb. Measure force, transverse/radial compliance, permanent set and guide stall through both directions. Reject the simple six-facet package as drawn; do not freeze until all terminal teeth, rib edges, backing transitions and obstruction recovery pass a full-body sweep with that measured compliance. No measured allowable, wear life, candidate fit or official result here.
