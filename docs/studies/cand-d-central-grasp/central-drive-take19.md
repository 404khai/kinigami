## Clamp and release sequence
1. **Enter with jaws physically open and piston retracted.** Protected sensors at both shoe tails (three for the large variant), piston home and three tail targets at the periphery. An obstruction at a Vlip or stem is not seated.
2. **Drive the opposed jaw carriers inward.** Candidate radial force element is a guided spring between carrier and shoe, with independent carrier-to-shoe displacement flag at *each* shoe. Positive reverse shoulders pull each shoe out; generous lost motion lets a ball-assisted shoe lead outward instead of wedging against a rigid return flank. A powered radial drive performs capture; do not substitute a spring-closed latch. Need stop/disconnect on actual relative deflection before spring solid or guide hard contact; torque stopping also required, including overrun and jam in midstroke. One obstructed jaw cannot authorize the other.
3. **Pull the captured cage axially** on a separate, centred, series spring. Closed radial shoes must have metallic structural backs against a cage, not rely on motor torque to resist radial service. Travel takes up axial tolerance against the actual spring, not to a prescribed jaw coordinate. No parallel draw shoulder through the spring. A screw/drum output must be positively retained without power (e.g. a disengage-before-motion positive index at measured seating; only a proposal). Limit any locked bias growth under service/compliance. Accept latch only at all three face targets, measured draw force window, all shoe locations, and physical lock. Controller feedback is not a 6D pose oracle.
4. **Unload before opening.** Release the powered-off index under load; drive pressure plate forward to remove spring compression, advance cage ~.20–.25 and verify actual draw reduction. The external 4W load can persist after bias removal. Open using positive pickups which permit outward lead. If a central piston *also* advances during this phase, the jaw path must lead the ball. For a centred sphere before its equator passes the jaw, require open ridge n ≥ sqrt((B+Rc)²−(xj−xb)²), plus offsets. B3.2 initial slope ≈1.664 radial per axial; never ram into the still-closed jaw at a preset motor angle. Simpler normal sequence: verify sufficient open aperture first, keep the jaws open and drive the piston.
5. **Sweep the stud, then verify that the faces have cleared.** Centred piston and/or posts require a real force and positive reverse. Local separation at three identified targets distinguishes a tilted neighbour from rod travel. Hold open on ambiguity; re-arm positively after clearance. Loss of communications, encoder motion, current rise, one missing return or one open jaw is not a release indication. No forced recovery through an unknown obstruction.

### Why axial metering is preferable, but not yet packaged
For the **small** nose, the contact normal lies 45.41–53.42° from radial. With μ=.23, draw/radial force lies between .636 and 2.286, before guide force. No assumption of equal jaws. Upstream spring force is *not* radial contact force: two-opposed-side guide reactions for an axially loaded overhung shoe scale as (1+2e/L) times its axial load. At first bearing n3.50, span2.60, contact n1.371, even the aligned planar self-friction coefficient μ(1+2e/L)·2.286=1.387. A divide-by-(1−coefficient) dry bound is invalid. No claim of infinite physical force; measure/limit actual shoe force downstream of guide friction, or use a flexurally guided shoe/rolling carriage **including a seized-roller case**. Unbalanced lateral jaw reactions cannot be assigned zero merely because the drive is centred. A laterally floating cage with stroke and stiffness bounds and no hard bypass must independently meet the 2 N lateral/10 N·mm total parasitic allowance of the face study. Three contacts are not a guarantee of three-way sharing.

[Springmasters D63203 table](https://www.springmasters.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/disc-springs-din-2093.pdf): OD6, ID3.2, .30 stock, .45 free height, approximately 27 N at .02 deflection and 53 N at .05 (rounded table values). Four discs **in series**, not parallel, give 1.80 nominal free height. An interpolated 37–45 N axial window corresponds to only 1.674..1.637 height. This 37 μm span is not an allowable or a manufacturer load tolerance: calibrate assembled stack with load vs actual displacement in both directions and elastic/thermal/impact debit. A captive series stack is physically different from assuming spring rate at an inferred motor angle. At the jaw a radial-axis D63203 also demands 6 mm axial and tangential width — it cannot be hidden in a thin nose tile. [Smalley CMS08-M1](https://www.smalley.com/wave-spring/cms08-m1) is 30 N at 1.78 with Ø5 shaft clearance, not a 37 N minimum. The Take 18 25/37 N service/fault face figures are conditional targets, not recalculated small-jaw retention. A larger spring upper load raises torque, neck bending and contact pressure.

## Guard and piston are different from the face-release proof
A **continuous**, outward flaring circular guard has favourable axial projection for an inner contact only above atan μ. A simple sufficient spherical-window screen debits asin(g/2Bmin) for an unsupported longitudinal gap and 2° profile/normal error. For g1.50, Bmin2.28 and μ=.23 this takes >34.16°; with .20 transverse clearance the needed guard inner radius at the crest is 5.125. Add .35 wall → 5.475, **inside the proposed n4..8.15 V stock** if it is a separate ring. Cut a common sloping guard/V surface, clock jaw windows and remove only nonworking portions, or show real rod/window normals and remaining facet stock. This is a sufficient screen for this window, not a disproof of a discontinuous rod guard. Neither a square window nor an uncovered cylindrical bore is credited as self-ejecting.

Small piston: radius 2.65±.03, .55 thick (not strength-rated), rim rounded without reducing the stated blocking diameter, centred within .05. With open jaw inner radius 3.00−.06, radial running margin .210. In a closed circular guard of inner radius at most 5.175, widest lateral annular gap 2.605 < ball minimum diameter 4.56. Thus the sphere's **equator cannot cross the piston plane beside the piston while inside that intact guard**. Need the full 3D swept slot width/diagonal and intervening plate/guide constraints, piston rod/skirt coverage as the disk exits, and non-jamming edge normals; this test does not say a ball outside an open lip stays forward forever. For a true stud-blocked condition keep a skirt occluding the jaw region until physical separation is confirmed. For B3.20 try piston R3.45 (not 2.65), recheck chamber/nut and guard. Prefer a hollow pull tube or peripheral pull fingers; a solid central draw rod and a centre ejector cannot occupy each other.

Three independent range targets on the actual tail, at 120° on R6.12, identify the tail plane. At ±.05 error per target a fitted intercept has ≤.05 error and fitted slope vector ≤4·.05/(3·6.12) error. A single range/blank return or pad limit switch cannot measure this plane. Script includes both (a) blanket bounds with whole-body radial reach ≤23 and (b) a proposed, **conditional** per-feature bound. Example: .15 lost post travel, measured 2° tilt and 6.80 stroke: whole-body worst-depth proof misses the full fixed head by 1.38; separating the stud, button bosses and body leaves .425/3.188/5.325 if actual projected stud-centre radius ≤1.10, button footprints ≤8.9, body ≤23, and no upper shaft/boss enters the central stud footprint. Verify limits by finite target windows/guard and the complete two-body mesh; they are not observables from rod position. Target validity, dead zone at small gaps, look angle, drift and installed resolution (including inability to see through a post) are open. Place the three optical or contact-gap channels in separate plateau holes beside posts; use target ID and calibration at their actual baseline. No credit for sustaining a free gap after retracting into external compression.
