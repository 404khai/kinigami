# Take 74 — One transverse key, three solid registry balls, six rigid faces

**Exploratory.** A real seven-coordinate, full-rank lock is possible in the nominal geometry. However, one 20 µm recessed face can remain unloaded at 60 N true key-normal force under a small permitted service wrench. Do not declare this design seated from key force or travel. An independent indicator can correctly refuse an incomplete presentation; it cannot supply the missing seating displacement.

## Tilted 65° trial: three different questions

Three R2.50 balls index against three Vs. The separately powered retaining key loads one integral off-center lug; no pin crosses an eye or ring. Its normal passes through the centroid of the triangle, not an exterior jaw. Unknowns are the six rigid tail coordinates and one transverse key stroke. The six V wrenches span the rigid-body wrench space, and the seventh row contains a nonzero key-motion coefficient sin18°. Unit key normal is balanced frictionlessly by six positive normals of 1/(6 cos65°). This is a compressive self-stress that needs a real positive key restraint; it is not evidence every possible equilibrium has all seven contacts.

## Loaded release: a real edge catch and an improved local path

Let the key withdraw in +Y, with signed travel measured from actual centered touch. An opposing neighbor load may follow the sole key contact while the tail draws along **inclined D**, not global X. On the flat the neighbor travels d=s_key sin18°. The finite flat ends at s_key=.89348 mm. At the rounded distal edge put L=2.50+.35, (kx,ky) its fillet center in the stationary key and θ=18°..90°. Exact contact gives

    d=(kx−Qx−L cosθ)/cos18°,
    s_key=Qy+d sin18°+L sinθ−ky.

The edge ends at s_key=3.74348, d=3.12610. The contact load on the key is N(cosθ+μsinθ,sinθ−μcosθ,0) for positive slip. A quasistatic external wrench opposing that force on the key's counterpart through the actual pole balances the free tail; an arbitrary load line need not drive this same branch. On every exposed flat/toe point in this meridian the least +Y projection is .09027 N/N at μ=.23, but key X holding goes to zero without friction at the last shoulder. Its component along D is then sin18°; a pure X tension is not by itself a balancing wrench. The V faces unload as their plane distances increase by d cos65°. Under sustained compression they may instead stay seated when the key backs away: unlatching is not positive ejection.

**Do not use the horizontal trial's 4.15 mm park.** At draw d=5.15662 mm the actual lug overlaps the parked key's distal/front R.35 corner by .08564 mm. Checking clearance immediately after the first edge would miss the return. At 4.85 park the whole infinite +D continuation clears the key by ≥.58010: project the most adverse point of the entire **rounded key** onto T. During the previous stroke the two nonrounded stem segments clear even the sharp key superset by ≥.98147 (segment and phase debits). The full swept 3D backed wing capsules clear the bore/lip by ≥.45824; the tilted V caps and their backs, distinct radial chambers, guide ears and blind rear wall are kept as stock. The sleeve has a radius-2.50 rolling proof only on the round lobes, not the fork/stem. After 10.65 mm draw the whole tail is ahead of x=0 and has >1.37 mm local clearance through another 3° pitch; this is not a collision certificate for an installed powered neighbor or the table. General out-of-plane, noncentral partial contact, arbitrary compressed recovery and permanent separation remain open.

The contact assistance is not a drive rating. In the centered z=0 branch X reactions at the two real guide bands must react the overhung moment. For known N and no hidden opposing squeeze, bound summed guide normals allowing unequal pad pressure positions, minimum pitch 2.40, the load's full x eccentricity and friction's own couple. With an **unverified example** guide μg=.12 this is ≤11.741 N/N; extra screw pull ≤1.319 N/N before independent baseline drag, inertia or holding friction. Hence ≤79.12 N if actual total key normal is capped at 60 N through handoff; for a single unsupported contacting body the table force vector bound gives ≤4.71 N in that phase. No Coulomb cone bounds an unknown self-stressed installation preload. Guide μ below .00883 would be sufficient for the conservative contact assist alone to cover guide drag, not an assumed coefficient. General off-plane thrust/roll couple and real drag need independent bounds. A powered transverse key withdrawn from its positive holding bolt can backdrive on power loss; no certified handoff by state label.

## Can the tightened angle remove a false equilibrium?

The nominal symmetric tilted 65° geometry first permits a stationary missing face at μ=0.23382..0.23411, compared with .15393–.15468 for the original horizontal-56° trial. With up to .59° normal error, one .020 recessed plane can be absent at μ=.23 and zero external wrench; the actual normals, force coefficients and ≤7e−15 residual are in `compare.json`. No infinitely thin cup or adjustable friction assumption.

For **±.10° head-frame normal error, ±.020 mm independent normal offsets**, μ≤.23, the answer changes at zero external wrench: no key preload and missing *primary* face can balance inside the studied registered cell, within 5° of nominal and 1 mm translation, if no rim/cap/other body touches. The tail's body rotation is free, not confused with the manufacturing angle. The result is weak under a service wrench, as the force table shows. Sphere centers and radii for this bound are the specified rigid ones; extra ball-position/radius errors and Hertz pressure-center wandering need separate debits.

Proof in `tight.py`: if exactly one face is missing, its actual gap can exceed the machining error. Its still-loaded mate must be below the finite 2.12 cap. Adding their two plane equations bounds the open gap G≤.48693. Invert six nominal rigid gap rows about O with the missing residual G; bound the exponential-rotation remainder by BR·θ²/2 and joint translation/rotation by successive enclosing balls. The rotation vector is inside a tube about a line parametrized by G. If at least two faces are missing check all five inequivalent second omissions over the full 5° ball, without assuming the other gap small. Symmetry of the projected contact set reduces the first omission to one; adding true stock can invalidate the contact-set premise, not enlarge the angular certificate.

Each remaining pose cell uses a common, pose-dependent virtual twist. Wrenches include sphere-center lever and spin −R(n×f). A .10° orientation uncertainty rotates the entire local Coulomb cone **and** its spin; use a circumscribed 48-gon for the nominal cone and bound their correlated Taylor remainders. Cellwise support inequalities include changing body centers, twist gradients and their products. The minimum separator work over active poles gives ||(F,M/10.30)||≥.00005277 ΣN if any face is missing. For the *already-engaged* geometry the same certificate omitting any j (G=0) gives a coarse first-loss bound, e.g. a missing face is impossible if the external norm is below .00005277 N_key. Tight on-angle zero-load detection is not a useful kiloNewton preload prescription. The LP threshold brackets in the table use actual service component boxes, not a rotated independent box or this weak norm alone.

**Inspection is a prerequisite, not achieved accuracy.** Locate a common head frame on three measured metal fiducials with ≥16 mm transverse span, fixed to the guide/seat block. Probe the actual V flanks locally, ball-center pattern and key plane **installed under both load polarities**. Proposed maximum error allocation: .030° datum transfer, .035° local face form/pole direction, .020° repeated loaded key/seat pose, .015° expanded inspection uncertainty = .100° worst case. At a .50 mm local land .035° is only .31 µm differential; witness flats alone do not bound the working pole. For the .020 position reserve, trial .006 mm datum, .006 ground height, .004 assembly/load repeatability, .004 expanded inspection uncertainty. Angular and height budgets both require evidence. [ISO 15530-3:2011](https://www.iso.org/standard/53627.html) describes task-specific CMM uncertainty by substitution against calibrated similar geometry; it does **not** certify this part or a machine's nameplate capability. Test with the same tip, access, fixture, preload and thermal cycle; accept only measured deviation plus uncertainty below allocation.

The modeled .080 mm diametral slide clearance is an explicit counterexample to crediting ±.10° from the drawing. A .50° cocked shank needs only .03588 mm clearance across the full 4.10 bearing span. At .10° total differential travel is .00716 mm, and the .020° repeatability allocation is only .00143 mm. Without a measured repeatable loaded lock register/guide, the tightened theorem is **not** an installed performance claim. Independent contact indication may detect incomplete lock, not drive it home.
