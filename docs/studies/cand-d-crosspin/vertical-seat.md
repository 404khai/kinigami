# Fixed Z-pin, moving registry: seated checks

**Exploratory, not a candidate.** The [Y-pin installations](eye-pin-take39.md) have material intersections. Fixing the pin in X removes the swept journal cut. Try holding the pin down in Z while moving an indexed face in +X against the passive eye, limiting take-up and positively reversing the face before lifting the pin. Advance the face farther in +X for ejection **only after** the actual pin is clear. None of those motions is assigned to the tail by a controller.

## Orient the registration pads for a one-sided journal

For comparison [layout.py](layout.py) varies three pads in the transverse YZ plane of the old XZ eye, solving the six-normal problem rather than assuming equal shares. Among the sampled geometries 51 pass its local screens; the lowest sufficient 70 g table draw is 129.29 N. Several have pads that would hit other hardware. A .35 mm separator at a nominal contact *point* does not clear a face or attach the pad.

[flat_face.py](flat_face.py) turns the eye into XY, tries a Ø3.50 pin at x12.95 in an R6.50/r2.70 torus centred at (15,0,0), then places spherical bosses in the eye plane. The upper pin root is now on the +Z side. Two bosses cannot resist a roll around the line between them; use three noncollinear centres. Each has a V, with normal n = cos(58°) h ± sin(58°) Z; the three horizontal h are proportional to (1,1.2,0), (1,−.8,0), (1,−.8,0). Put the centres at (7,−5.04,0), (18,2.56,0), (18,−13.44,0). All six have positive X components. The rear horizontal force line passes through x11.20 on the centreline; so does the combined line from the other two. The actual split is solved from the full inverse matrix.

The sufficient service/table/fault axial draws for this example are **65.5 / 131.6 / 101.1 N** (rounded up) with the earlier wider-body 70 g load inputs. The lowest table value in this sample is 121.12 N; it places a pad at y−17.48 instead. These are *existence* bounds for compressive normal carriers around a fully seated reference with the six lands available. Include .6° normal and .19 mm lever uncertainties, full pin friction disk with µ=.23, .050 mm in-plane side offset and ≤2° relative axis tilt. Pin slope under load counts. At each nominal pole the narrower layout has ≥.739 mm separation from its other pad balls and the torus/pin in the local check. This says nothing about actual side walls, fillets, cheeks, root necks or floor.

## A hard cage is a distinct alternative to always sustaining high preload

Frictionless wrench capacity is not a bound on post-load displacement. Conversely small motion can remain physically caged even if a particular land unloads. [seat_cage.py](seat_cage.py) tests the connected component around an **already verified** reference. Use that reference, six sustained finite lands and an actually held-down vertical shaft, with maximum normal accommodation g at any land (including seat/drive compliance and backdrive), maximum extra axial play s at the pin (including bending, registry errors and support offsets). A mere switch on the screw is not such a measurement.

With centre translation t and exponential rotation vector w, each nominal seat constraint n·t + (p×n)·w ≥ −g has a conservative error debit dn |t| + (dn |p| + dp)|w| + .5(|p|+dp)|w|² about the ring centre. dn is 2 sin(.6°/2), dp=.19 mm. The second-order error follows by integrating the acceleration of a rotated point along the rotation. The seventh inequality is −t_x≥−s: a fully threaded cylindrical shank extending past every core point puts its axis in the eroded projected ellipse; a disk of radius r+b about the axis fits in the outer R-disk. Hence Cx−xpin≤R−r−b, for any attitude **within this fully threaded branch**. The implementation checks straight-shank length, but an actual structure must reserve that length with flexure and tolerance. The pin constraint needs s at the *loaded* pose; it cannot count bearing play twice or ignore tip motion.

These seven affine bounds form a bounded simplex (six independent lands and a strict positive balancing combination with the tensile stop). For trial bounds on |t| and |w| enumerate its seven vertices with the full nonlinear error debit. If all are strictly inside the trial bounds, a continuous path from the witness pose cannot first leave them. The finite lands must contain their entire possible contact excursion and no part may fracture. With g=.010 and s=.060 mm this gives |t|<.283 mm, |w|<1.94° and maximum boss travel .748 mm. With g=.020 and s=.100 the latter rises to 1.858 mm and the angle to 4.86°. At g=.040 and s=.060 this certificate finds no box. Failure to find a box is not proof of escape. A pin reaching a port, large flexure, partial initial engagement or a boss leaving its working face invalidates the interpretation. Use no pose snap to enter this branch.

## Take-up and force evidence still needed

As a scale check, manufacturer [Belleville Springs D83204](https://www.bellevillesprings.com/disc-springs-to-din-2093/) lists OD8/ID3.2, .4 stock, .6 free height; 119 N at .09 mm and 153 N at .12 mm. A stack in series increases measurable travel but not force; friction on a guide, tolerance, set and a seat hitting a hard stop can change both ends of the working interval. A driven compression stack behind a retractable plate, with an open-position mechanical hold, is **not** a spring-closed pin. Neither the catalogue force nor the simplex certifies built stiffness, passive hold or a sensor. The axial motor must move under the worst load, not just survive it at stall. The single-root cantilever, seated eye Hertz stress, mounting, narrow land access and release under simultaneous tail/roof/pin/guide/floor reactions remain separate checks.
