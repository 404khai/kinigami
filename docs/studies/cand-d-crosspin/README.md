# Transverse pin through a tail eye

**Exploratory.** The eye cannot carry torque about its symmetry axis by smooth normal contact. Test a passive circular eye for tension and a separate three-site indexed face for the other loads. The two calculations below concern different phases; neither authorizes a successor to the [run 12 declaration](../../../candidates/cand-d/declaration.json).

## When can the shaft touch both sides?

Coordinates in mm: the untilted eye has centre (15,0,0), major radius A=5 and tube radius r=2.70. The barrel radius b=1.25, along Y at x=13.95 in the drawn position. Set d=r+b. A pair of parallel cylinders is **not** the model: the ring core projects to an ellipse with minor radius B=A cos(tilt) and major radius A in the plane perpendicular to the barrel. The pin centre inside this ellipse must be at least d from its boundary. Translation along the pin is unrestricted in the following long-barrel test.

- One active point: with no other contacts the shaft normal is bounded by the magnitude of the external force, by force balance.
- Two active points: a radius-d disk touches twice on the ellipse's major medial axis. For a parameter q the reflected core points have projected coordinates (±B cos q, A sin q). Orthogonality to their tangents gives the pin offset e=(A²−B²)sin q/A on the major axis. Their common squared distance to the pin is B²[1−(1−B²/A²)sin²q]. This gives **27.275° to 37.814°**, not just the centered, opposite-pole case at the upper endpoint. Distinct contacts coalesce at the lower endpoint.
- In axes with Z along the projected major axis, write the two contact normals (±u,0,v), u=B cos q/d, v=B² sin q/(Ad); reflect axes so both are nonnegative. The pin contacts have x=±bu and y=±s about their midpoint, s=√(A²−B²)cos q. A virtual translation v along Z and angular velocity −u/s about Z gives normal work 1 per unit normal at **both** contacts; the remaining tangential speed is b u²/s. This is a test of equilibrium, not an imposed trajectory. The moment axis also passes through the eye centre. u/s≤1/√[d(A−d)], including the one-contact limit. For an illustrative external 5 N / 50 N·mm major-axis wrench and µ≤.23 this bounds **total normal by 34.41 N and axial Coulomb drag by 7.92 N**. No equal sharing used. Wall, tip, registry, stem, journal, preload and floor reactions are *not* in that illustrative wrench. Do not size a drive from it.

With the pin axis as x=0, trial cheeks satisfy |y|≤H+x tan α. Leaving .15 mm in Y beyond the untilted tube gives H=r sec α+d tan α+.15. At α=45° the wall is at **±7.918 mm** on the pin axis; a journal at |y|=4..7 would be in front of it. A continuous tilted bare-eye path keeps the rear core at (−d,0,0), reaching the opposite-pole pair within those cheeks. The axis remains inside: with B=A cos t and u=cos q, squared distance minus d² equals (1+u)[2B(B−d)+A²sin²t(1−u)]≥0. The mounted stem and the other Kin are not on this path. Conversely, increasing A to 6.50 excludes that *particular* opposite pair by 1.062 mm at this flare; it does not exclude all two-point states.

## Why not simply taper the pin?

A solid cone pulled toward its large end receives assisting axial force everywhere on its flank if tan β>µ. That is attractive for an eye in an unknown attitude. It also makes the hole that must be guarded larger. For a tube-backed surface entering the front of a continuous plane pierced by a circular Ø2p bore along Y, the intersection disk in the sloped plane must fit the aperture's short width 2p. Its rim normal can turn by at most asin(p/r) if p<r; this argument requires a full rim, front-side access and the same local backing radius. For p=1.35 and r=2.70 the 45° wall gives just **+.01875 N in X per normal N** with a 1° normal debit and µ=.23. A thin stem, fillet or interrupted rim need not obey it.

For a cone spanning both symmetric walls the near port needs p≥2H tan β even ignoring far-tip radius, tolerances and oblique wall thickness. An aligned ring clearing the back of the cheek requires H≥r(sec α+tan α) if the rear core is at least r behind the axis. The all-rim positive-work rule needs p/r<sin(α−δ), δ=atan µ_wall plus slope allowance. Even setting δ=0 gives 2tan β<sin α cos α/(1+sin α)≤.300284; the maximum is at sin α=(√5−1)/2. With µ_pin=.23 the full cone cannot satisfy both design rules **at any scale**. Two equal cones reaching the midplane only halve the span; including µ_wall=.23 and 1° gives tan β<.21518 even for those ideal tips. This is an incompatibility between two *sufficient certificates*, not physical impossibility of tapered or rolling pins.

## Put six real force directions on the registry

Separate trial: sphere centres at x=18 on a radius-8.5 circle about X, phases 90°, 210°, 330° from +Y toward +Z. Each sphere seats on a pair of V normals symmetric about +X and inclined tangentially, at ±62° in the example. The shaft bears inside the eye near x=12.70, not at the triangle centre. At a nominal .050 mm lateral offset allowance and ≤2° tilt the projected core ellipse bounds the pin normal error at **3.051°**, allowing lateral/axial traction up to .2868 with µ=.23. That includes the small minor-radius loss of the ellipse, not a fixed-direction axial draw. Assume .025/.015/.010 mm major/tube/barrel radius errors; .19 mm centre and .6° normal errors at each seat; .15 mm pressure-patch extent, .08 registration error and .25 mm axial arm error. These are provisional coupon inputs, not demonstrated fits or stiffnesses.

For each row of the inverse six-normal wrench matrix, subtract the support of the complete external force/moment box, the uncertain pin wrench at the **5.30 mm** lever, and normal/position perturbations. Along a continuous perturbation to first loss of positivity, x balance bounds the sum of face normals by (draw+|Fx|)/cos(62.6°); a singular-value check excludes rank loss. For the [70 g wider-body load boxes](../cand-d-expanding-plug/crown.py) sufficient axial draws are **71.81 N service, 141.93 N table, 106.32 N fault** (rounded up). These prove existence of a frictionless positive normal solution when all specified lands are available, not actual load sharing, seating, minimum possible draw or a manufactured face. Doubling lateral error to .100 mm raises the table figure to 345.66 N. A worse reference registration, pin slope from flexure, or a missing land needs a new calculation.

Taking the 141.923 N axial screen as a single **central transverse shaft load** between simple supports at ±12.9 gives 596.7 MPa nominal bending, .1324 mm elastic deflection (E=200 GPa, Ø2.50). Equilibrium determines the two reactions *for that specific load*. At the aligned inner equator, the signed curvature sum in the ring plane is 1/b−1/(A−r), and the other sum is 1/r. The elastic half-space screen with E*=115.4 GPa gives 3.671 GPa peak pressure and .135/.137 mm semi-axes. It is not a reusable-contact rating. Mixed shear, off-centre tip and port pressure, manufacturing, fork roots, pin/seat motion under this deflection and journal-edge loading are excluded. Specifying a higher torque to obtain the table preload would not by itself settle any of these.

Reproduce from this directory with Python 3 (NumPy/SciPy for the last command):

```sh
python3 -B eye.py
python3 -B barrel.py
OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 -B registry.py
```

The useful discriminator is whether the actual X-direction support can unload **while the pin remains backed**, and what external wrench the loose eye can then receive from its cheeks and indexed pads during withdrawal. A collar switch, actuator current or the bare-eye bound is not that evidence. No gate inputs or verdicts changed.
