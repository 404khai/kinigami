# Take 11 — Six scalar ball constraints and outward-free radial keepers
**Exploratory design-author study, not an independent review of frozen v7 or a gate result.** For the scoped idealization, neither the fully seated flat pairs nor the exposed release edges admit unbounded static self-stress. This is a conditional paper sizing result, not a strength, capture, tolerancing, complete package, or loaded-motion qualification.

## Inputs and face geometry
Head-fixed x points out of the head; three centres R4.65 at 120° in yz. Each metal ball radius 1.045–1.055 on a radial neck from an inboard axial stem presents two unobstructed axial poles and two tangential poles. Exactly one hard normal per pair at most: diameter 2.09–2.11; illustrative opposing hard plane widths 2.14–2.18 mm must retain positive clearance after wear, runout, elastic closure and all local errors. Passive structural retention is metal keeper/back seat plus hard tangential cheek, not a spring. Body and stem must clear the blades throughout the **actual** swept and deflected poses. A symmetric CAD drawing is not this proof.

Use straight three-blade radial withdrawal, tangential-line extrusions at the keeper tips, radial-line extrusions at the cheek outlet, 0.10 mm nose fillets. Force calculations allow 0.5° normal departure, 0.18 mm transverse centre error about each stated local locus, 0.08 mm axial error for the edge study, μ≤0.23 on all dry contacts. The flat model uses one total 0.18 mm three-dimensional centre error about a common translating origin. For the edge study the origin is the nominal head ball ring; no common-mode allowance. These are design/gauge conditions, not inferred pose constraints. All listed loads act at the specified origin; transform any offset wrench there before comparing. The deep face and local corner accessibility are particularly important.

## Locked flats and partial flat sets
Normalize moment by R. With centre c_i=r_i/R in normalized units and direction d_j (x or the local tangent t_i), B has columns (d_j,c_i×d_j). The axial reciprocal rows are (x/3,2(c_i×x)/3); the tangential rows are (2t_i/3,x/3). Thus an arbitrary signed six-wrench has six individual signed normal coordinates; their signs select the bearing faces, not a presumption of six simultaneous contacts. For each active signed normal j, normal force acts through the ball centre. Tangential friction acts at the actual displaced pole, not the centre. For reciprocal row (u,v) and normalized pole p the friction term is μ times the norm of the tangent projection of u+v×p. The code inflates this coefficient for both pole signs, centre and angle error, and adds the incremental normal-wrench error. For any active set I, n ≤ |B⁻¹w|_I + H_II n. Full matrix positive-vector bound ρ≤0.812 (nominal geometry 0.6051); all 63 nonempty subsets inherit contraction. A subset may be unable to react a wrench at all: this bounds only reactions that exist; the body then moves. The inertia wrench must be included for dynamic use.

Unload and positively verify the pads BEFORE reaching an edge, or re-run the edge calculation with their resolved wrench. Adding shims without a cap reintroduces self-stress.

## Keeper edges, other sites already leaving, and real drive forces
The outer closing flank must withdraw while every loaded keeper is still flat at its pole. An inner pickup may push the blade outward but cannot restrain outward motion. Slot has enough outward free lead that an assisted blade reaches full clearance before any outer stop, for every driver position/lag after breakout. Hence blade i has independent inequality −f_i·r_i − kN_i ≤ g₀, using the force **on the tail**, including friction at the tip. Opposing blade drag ≤g₀+kN_i+k_D D_i; outward pickup D_i≥0 and k_D<1, so D_i drops out of that upper-bound inequality. A blocked slot, hard closing flank or shoulder, or hooked keeper corner invalidates it. An external obstruction is a fault, not successful release.

Illustrative nonpreloaded side-wing guides with two radial reaction stations L≥1.60 mm and transverse reaction spacing b≥1.80. For ball/follower contact along r let 2|radial offset from guide midpoint|/L≤1.12, tangential eccentricity ≤.20, axial eccentricity ≤.15. Resolve the transverse x/t load into station reactions and charge radial-axis torsion twice over b. Sum of supporting normals bounded by (1.12+2·.20/b)|Fx|+(1.12+2·.15/b)|Ft|+2(.20+.15)|Fr|/L. Multiplying by μ and |f|≤sqrt(1+μ²)N gives k≤.451 (use .46). This is predicated on the specified nonwedging lands, finite reaction spacing and no unbounded guide preload. Any leaf/friction/preload outside that model is included in a **measured** g₀≤0.8 N **per blade over all travel**. For a centred cam at 35–37°, no tangential follower component, axial-to-radial contact force ≤1.190 including μ, this same geometry gives k_D≤.468. These are explicit fit/force conditions, not existing fabricated bearings or a calibration.

Cheek straight flat must extend to x≥1.48 ahead of nominal ball centre. A keeper-contact ball can advance at most (1.055+.100)(1−cos α)+.08 ≤1.235, so **that** site's own cheek rim cannot simultaneously oppose its keeper at near-axial normals. Other sites may already be on a cheek edge. On that cheek the ball centre moves laterally as well as axially: nominal displacement −s(a+r_edge)(1−cos γ)t + [1.48+(a+r_edge)sin γ]x from its local cheek reference. Both force and moment use this locus. 1.48 nominal with tolerances within .08 and an independently gauged minimum >1.235 is required; a substantially longer cheek changes the moment and stroke. One meridional exit edge, or flat cheek, per site. At a keeper edge n=−cos α x−sin α r; at the cheek exit n=sin γ x±cos γ t. α,γ range continuously from 0 to 90°. Account for tolerances as above, including complete backlash in .08 mm axial/.18 mm transverse. Do not silently extend this result to corner/shoulder/backside/stem contact.

The mixed program checks 5 local states/site: front keeper in either of two angle halves with optional flat cheek, rear seat with flat cheek, isolated cheek flat, or isolated cheek outlet. Both tangential polarities, absent contacts via zero force, arbitrary simultaneous angles between sampled rays. Bounds use the reciprocal **tail wrench + three independent unilateral blade inequalities**. For each of 1,000 configurations, a linear-program dual twist gives verified nonnegative work for every allowed force ray, lower-bounding its chosen reaction objective. Absolute dot products with the full external-load box bound total work. Twelve-ray outer Coulomb polygons, a second-derivative angular interpolation bound, centre/normal/pole uncertainty and positive numerical guard are included. Not an equality-only pose sample. The weaker pure-six-normal analysis cannot simply be extrapolated across the entire tip; a hard reaction against an outward blade would invalidate this additional constraint.

## Final exit and limits
With all keepers clear, rear seats act only in +x and cheek normals flare outward. A purely smooth meridional end does not retain axially at large angle, but a parallel cheek can until slid out. Summing the three tangential reciprocal inequalities and the outward axial equilibrium separately gives a feedback ≤0.829, including cheek-induced transverse centre shift up to 1.155 mm beyond the .18 mm error, normal angle error, friction and pole moment. See T=ΣN cos γ, P=ΣN sin γ+ΣN_rear in the source. At 46/50 g, >9.11/9.90 N **total normal force delivered by the three translating back seats**, not per seat, rules out static balance under the stated 4W+W compression/drag and operating torque. No requirement all seats carry force at once. Budget internal pusher friction and all added drag above this. The rear pads must actually sweep and reach all three balls without running off their own edges, overbalancing the unit, or wedging a stem. 0.3 mm travel is not separation: ≈1.48+.10+1.055+.08+.08=2.795 mm positive travel is a first target for full-ball clearance of the cheeks. Require actual local gap past all hard features.

Radial tips need roughly r4.32 closed → ≥5.95 open (~1.63 mm); .18 error and the .10 tip roll consume the difference from nominal. Check hard backing, side-wing overhang, tail stem, opening lead, and ≥1.6 mm effective support span over this entire sweep within r6.3; it is not a drawn complete stack. 1.63/tan35°=2.33 mm actuator travel. Of Take 10's partial-inventory 3.53 mm coax stroke that leaves 1.20 mm **before** bias unloading, clearance, end stops, independent pusher ratio and overtravel. Reserving .30 mm leaves .90 mm to drive 2.80 mm at ≥3.12 displacement ratio, ≥31 N ideal axial input for 10 N output, plus linkage losses. A combined actuator is not proved.

Flat sphere-on-flat Hertz at provisional steel E*=115.4 GPa, min ball r1.045 and 25.28 N gives ~3.91 GPa peak. The .10 mm convex roll on a keeper or cheek is NOT a plane: it has a smaller principal contact radius (~.091 mm in the tight direction). Need elliptical Hertz/edge yield, actual heat treatment, endurance and proof that a dent/burr cannot give a new catching normal. A knife-edge has no finite nominal contact pressure. No first-contact/impact fatigue bound here. G6 evidence requires swept solids or gauges excluding *all* side corners, wrong-side re-entry and stem/cheek collisions, including unloaded modes where fewer than six normals exist. If a face outside this atlas can bear, report jam/unknown, not capture or lock. End flags for each blade/actual bias and real separation matter after reboot. Servo motion alone is not evidence.
