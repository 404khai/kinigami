# Take 78 — Can the inclined dry key positively eject a retained neighbor?

**Exploratory.** No general release guarantee from the retained neighborhood alone. A narrow powered ram is bypassable even from exact register. A one-piece C-slotted saddle carrying the six rigid Vs eliminates that particular failure at full stroke and admits a loaded one-face exit. A useful input-force bound covers a substantially larger moving neighborhood than the initial fit, but the retained pose does not enforce it throughout withdrawal and ejection. No demonstrated all-pose motor/guide, hard-jam exclusion, passive hold or persistent air gap.

| Claim | Evidence | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Key can physically withdraw | Noncentral 3D free-tail path on flat and rounded edge; bounded cone of favorable key normals | Guide drag, locked preload, and excursions outside the tube |
| R4.10 ram ejects locally | 1.1719 mm minimum end clearance in the tube | Actual 7 mm side bypass leaves 1.0521 mm of lug behind lip |
| C-saddle closes the geometric escape into the bore | Controlled slot's worst empty radius 2.420 versus minimum backing radius 2.45 | Requires actual 11.00 mm stroke, continuous front ring, supported lands and a started-on-front branch |
| C-saddle moves loaded | One finite V face carries the whole noncentral example; 3.6092 mm lug clearance | A reachable branch, not forced motion under every load |
| Motor can be bounded locally | Ideal table axial ≤3.433 N; illustrative guided table bound ≤42.09 N during possible fixed contact | Conditional guide construction, friction, tolerances and departure tube; not a drive rating |

## Wider barrier proposal: a single hard-seated C-saddle

Rigidly attach the same six finite dry V lands to a single axial hard-seated metal floor. No individual saddle motions, no XY compliance. The fixed housing carries the powered transverse key and both guide bands. Preload is reacted against a hard stop at home; for release, park the key first, then positively drive this floor on +X. This is a new package, not a retrofit already present in 74/79. The slot stays one-sided to the OD; it is not a new pin through the tail. Specify:

- Floor radius 13.95, thickness 1.60, x=−8.15 at home and +2.85 fully extended, 11.00 travel. Home rear face x=−9.75. Fixed bore R14.05. Retain the finite upper cap and original dry planes; crop rear tower roots on R13.90 and union them with the solid floor. Actual poles are >3.48 from this trim. Side-tower z clearance to the guide ≥3.4959. For shoe/rail strength the thin floor needs attached metal ribs; these must remain behind its swept profile.
- Through-slot from y=3.73 outboard, |z|≤2.395 through both full guide bands. At y>12.84 neck it to |z|≤1.80 around the shank and two outboard support ties (each |z|≤1.30, respectively x=XB−.52..XB−.19 and XF+.19..XF+.58). ### Terminal proof, independent of where the lug is inside the bore

At any cross-section through sound floor stock the worst distance from a point in the bore circle to metal is ≤2.420 mm, including the actual arc/slot crossing. Debit .025 on each effective floor edge and on bore; widest slot half-width is then 2.420 while it is still wholly inside the OD. In the narrower outboard region use Euclidean distance to the circular land at |z|=1.825. The same analysis on the fixed side portal gives 2.4391. Both <ρ_min=2.45. Never model the open end as a perpetually narrow infinite parallel slit.

Use the moving comparison plane x_g=min(actual floor x−.10, 2.38), inside the 1.60 stock even at maximum end travel +.10. From the registered initial branch, every R2.45..2.55 backing center starts ahead. It cannot first cross x_g rearward within the circle without hitting the floor. It also cannot enter the lagging side through radius 14.075 at x≤x_g: below lip it meets the closed rear/side shell including the bounded blind port, and above lip it is within 2.38 of the uninterrupted annular rim. The rear chassis/rod penetrations must remain closed against these generators. This is why the saddle does **not** need to be a 13 mm deep solid plug. Terminal bore-side trailing material is at least 2.38+sqrt(2.45²−2.420²)−2.55=+.2122 where the center remains projected into the bore circle. A body that went entirely outside the shell can swing back onto the *exterior*; no uniform exterior/return clearance is claimed.

The slender stem is not a fictitious R2.45 ball: its elbow lies .9901 from the chord between the lug center and opposite registry center. The sum of that distance and 1.40 is <2.45; using both thick ends' axial positions puts the full elbow on the forward side, independently of roll. Its other end is inside the broad hub. This implication requires those thick anchors not to have wrapped around the exterior behind the lip. The forks are not rolling balls either; with body pitch ≤30° (any roll), their earliest vertex stays .7201 ahead of the hub generator's trailing bound. Large flips around the extended exterior, contacting neighboring housings, and table reach remain separate. Nothing in the initial 1.85° declaration guarantees that later orientation restriction. Fixed metal/moving-seat contacts can survive terminal extraction.

### Motion and loads on the wider design

**Existence of loaded motion.** Start in the nominal retained pose with one .020 recessed V face. Let the key travel and tail advance axially by t plus .025t in z. Flat ends at .8082 key travel, rounded toe at 2.7775, t=2.9731 and z=.07433. Before toe end one finite actual lug pole can balance a changing service-box wrench: key friction opposes the actual three-dimensional slip, with N≤.45 and wrench bounded by .462 N and 2.078 Nmm. Unload N smoothly at the last contact; no tensile X capacity assumed at its end. Hold the body clear while the key completes park; drive the C-saddle forward. After 2.7618 of pickup the leading V face contacts the ball at relative x=.2113 and z=.07433, well inside its finite .4965 land reserve. All other gaps strictly positive (minimum .01757). The free tail can then translate on that *one* V contact under F=(−.07173,−.39468,.20392) N and M=(4.51935,−.31527,.97942) Nmm about the moving triangle. At actual stroke end the lug trails at x=+3.6092. This force-balanced path uses no infinite plane beyond a land or commanded pose adjustment. It is compatible with compressive following on return; there is no guaranteed persistent air gap.

**Ideal ejection in the declared tube.** Choose body virtual velocity X per unit saddle stroke: every possible force on moving stock, including slot lip/seat and rear surfaces, has zero relative work. Stationary key, barrel and other package are separated; sole possible fixed guide contact is the broad hub's forward-normal edge, f_x>0. Thus the force exerted against the saddle drive is at most adverse F_x independent of unknown self-stress. External moments do **not** disappear from the guide load. If a hub/guide contact is possible then t≤.4533; the finite intact floor under the lug bounds relative axial lag ≥−.8075. Early moving stock contacts are on upper V/caps, deck junctions or front floor: positive normal work along D. At possible deck edges n·D≥.5363; no rounded backing reaches the outside trim (≥.6524), nor does a center pass the floor slot edge (≥2.5658). The broad hub is well ahead of that floor. The D projection also limits the *fixed* contact magnitude, f·D≥.4174 N/N. These pose-specific bounds permit a conservative illustrative bending/torsion load budget:
