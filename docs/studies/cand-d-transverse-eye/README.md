# Take 38 — transverse pin in a solid toroidal eye

**Result: exploratory.** The actual flared cheeks do **not** enforce one-sided pin contact. At 36° tilt the bare eye clears both complete wall planes and touches the cylinder and its hemispherical tip simultaneously. Nevertheless the resulting pin drag is bounded by the *external wrench*, not merely the tension: even opposed contacts transmit a couple. The result is conditional on the fixed-contact, guide, deformation and terminal domains below. There is no qualified release load or demonstrated trajectory. No candidate, Q12/Q85/Q100, registered test or official result changes.

Reproduce with `python3 -B docs/studies/cand-d-transverse-eye/eye.py` (standard library). Lengths mm, forces N, torques N·mm. Positive x is ejection; the pin withdraws in **+y**, its round nose faces −y. All friction bounds use the entire static Coulomb disk, with provisional dry μ=.23, not rolling or equal shares.

## Actual trial stock and stages

Take 39 trial: eye centre C=(15,0,0) nominal, circular spine radius R=5 in xz, solid swept-ball tube r=2.70, pin radius p=1.25, S=r+p=3.95. Cheek planes are |y|=x−6.03, not parallel faces 8 apart. Their half-gap at x=13.95 is **7.92**. Axial seating and the lateral pin drive are distinct. Journal intervals |y|=11.65..14.15 move with the x carrier; their OD is 5.2, not the hole in the working flare. The fixed flare needs the x-swept port from pin axes 13.95..15.00. Calculations below use a 1.35 maximum cut radius; the guide calculation separately needs a *minimum* free radius.

1. **Insert and seat:** aligned radial pin clearance is R−S=1.05. When Cx=15, pin x=13.95 is the position of first metal contact; it does not by itself prove a nonzero series preload. The drive side of the series element needs real extra take-up after contact, tolerance overtravel and a displacement/force limit independent of motor current. Transfer tension from one bore side to the pin, metal journals and axial carrier; compression and the remaining wrench components need actual seats. An axisymmetric eye alone has no normal moment about its y symmetry axis. This is not a six-coordinate retention proof for separate registry pads.
2. **Undo the draw:** moving the axis forward to x15 is not an unload measurement. An aligned ring pulled to Cx=16.05 can still follow it under the full boundary tension. Don't subtract 1.05 of clearance from that load. Measurement or bounded reactions must include any cam bottoming, hard stop and all other paths.
3. **Pull pin, do not eject yet:** nominal nose apex y=−21.55 drawn, −15.55 relieved, +10.50 parked. At x15 full nominal tip setback from the + cheek by .20 normal requires apex ≥8.97+p(sec45°−1)+.20 sec45°=9.77061. The .72939 surplus is local; it does not certify stroke, bending, front/back stock or port closure.
4. **Eject only after actual clearance:** centred +x ejection is compatible with the qualified blind back, flares and ports. A centred pusher still needs real contact, adequate travel/force, floor drag and all opposing terminal/housing reactions checked through physical separation. Do not push on an uncleared pin.

## What the cheeks actually allow

A whole-torus separator for a wall with unit inward normal m is the centre's signed plane distance minus r+R√(1−(m·k)²), k the eye axis. This includes both hemitori. For tilt θ about z, the two support radii are r+R|cosθ∓sinθ|/√2. With Cy=R sinθ the front-ring rise does not cost clearance at the rear of the mouth. At θ=acos(S/R)=37.81449°, Cx=pin x=13.95, Cz=0, the exact centred opposed-shank pinch clears **both infinite** wall planes by .10721. Its contacts are separated 6.13107 along the pin, not in a single washer plane. A blind back, registry or a measured assembly can exclude this pose; a flare-plane calculation cannot. The inner parallel curve of the projected ellipse first loses a unique normal offset at R cos²θ=S (27.2747°); the centred pinch is not the onset of possible multiple contacts.

**Partially withdrawn, spatial counterexample.** Pin x=15,z=0; tilt 36° about z; C=(15.075702,2.938926,−.828370); both plane gaps .835970. Shank generator at y=5.484111, cap centre at y=1.583012 (apex .333012). Unit normals from pin into ring:

| contact | x | y | z |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| shank | .906037 | 0 | .423198 |
| tip | −.717668 | −.192071 | .669373 |

The calculator constructs the shank tangency on the offset ellipse, solves the *other* tip distance root, and checks which side of the forbidden interval is occupied by the metal pin. Neither a midplane section nor an apex-only check represents this pair. A +x-directed radial stem of equal tube radius attached at the forward minor-axis end also clears the pin in this witness (its first spine x exceeds the pin x by 4.121); both wall distances increase along that stem. Not a forced tail pose or proof of reach through other installed stock.

## Wrench bound for all rigid torus / capsule contacts, including partials

Let s run on the pin's finite generator segment, parallel to its actual axis, and c=C+Ru be a torus spine point. At any contact |c−s|=S. The same ball at s must clear the **entire** spine; thus u is the unit projection direction of s−C in the ring plane, of nonzero projected length t. Write n=(c−s)/S=a u+b k. Then t=R−Sa≥R−S>0. The physical contact is at s+p n; the full opposite shaft end is buried, only one cap accessible.

* **Number of contacts.** Squared distance from s to the nearest spine is f=|s−C|²+R²−2R t. With longitudinal coordinate v measured along the shaft, t²=d²+A²(v−v₀)², A the projected unit shaft speed. Then f″=2−2R A²d²/t³: two outer convex intervals and at most one intervening concave interval. At most two distinct global minima on the physical segment, including endpoint minima. For d=0 the intervening cusp is downward; for A=0 the function is quadratic. Projection length zero cannot contact. A zero of f−S² is a global min by nonpenetration. This handles shank/shank, shank/cap and cap alone; with two distinct contacts at least one is on the shank.
* **Spacing vs normal change.** For two contacts,
  |Δs|²=S²[(Δa)²+(Δb)²]+t₁t₂|Δu|², whereas |Δn|²=(Δa)²+(Δb)²+a₁a₂|Δu|². Consequently |Δs|≥min(S,R−S)|Δn|. This does not assume coplanar forces.
* **Nearly parallel pair:** let T=ΣN, F the magnitude of the aggregate eye–pin force, M the magnitude of its moment about C. If |Δn|≤1.60, projection on the mean normal gives F≥(.60−.8μ)T=.416T. One contact has F≥N.
* **Widely separated normals:** otherwise L=|Δs|≥L₀=1.680. Moment about the *other* generator s₂ has magnitude ≥L N₁−pμT, since the shank force has transverse component ≥N₁ and only tangential forces have a moment about their respective generators. N₂≤F+H N₁ with H=√(1+μ²). Also |M at s₂|≤M+(R+S)F. Hence

  T ≤ [(1+H)M+((1+H)(R+S)+L₀)F]/[L₀−pμ(1+H)].

  The denominator is 1.09749 mm. Together **T ≤ 1.847 M + 18.054 F** in the stated units (coefficients rounded outward). This is deliberately loose, not a predicted normal load. At the symmetric pinch alone, T ≤ M_mid/(√(R²−S²)−μp)=.3600 M_mid. The horizontal normal couple cannot be disguised as zero wrench by dry friction at this geometry.
* **Axial drag:** on the withdrawing tip the normal reaction on the pin assists +y; on a straight aligned shank it has zero y component. Thus adverse axial force ≤μT, not H T. If actual shaft tilt relative to y is β, a conservative coefficient is sinβ+μ. Distinct rim strength and patch deformation still matter. This proof is for ideal smooth rigid contacts, not a measured real friction cap, a proof of low force, or a statement that the pin necessarily moves.

**Tail appendages.** An explicit partials domain for a same-r outward radial stem (union of balls C+ℓu, ℓ≥R, including its rounded terminal): measure relative eye/shaft tilt θ and perpendicular distance d of C to the shaft line. If R cosθ−d>S sinθ, an additional stem contact is impossible. At hypothetical contact its transverse separation along projected u is at least R cosθ−d. For unit n this implies n·u>0 even with the worst allowed axial component. Moving a little back along the stem would penetrate the generator ball: contradiction unless ℓ=R, already a torus contact. At θ≤40°, d≤1.05 the margin is .2412. A separate R3 ball at ℓ≥7 on that ray clears the entire shaft by ≥.0623 in the same nominal domain; insufficient for .20 tolerance. Other flanges, fillets, pads and housings require their own rolling-radius and pin-exclusion tests. This bound is **not** inferred from the flares. Full-shank threading implies projected ellipse contains the pin axis with clearance S from its outline, hence d≤R−S and R cosθ≥S; the same inference is invalid once the tip is partway out. A missed stem exclusion invalidates the two-contact count.

## Ejection and the empty port

Let the working wall have α=45° flare and intact metal either side of a straight x-slot: length unrestricted, z half-width h=1.35, ends the intersections of y-axis cylindrical bores with that wall. In the wall plane the half ellipse axes are A=h secα along the generator in xy, B=h in z. A spherical constituent of radius b>h cannot pass the mouth; its centre stays on the pocket side in this local domain. Maximum normal sag into the port is b−√(b²−h²): .36173 for b=2.70. Even at x=16.35 its y reach ends .8184 before the journal's near face. Requires an intact rim, blind backing/edge returns and no alternate access from outside the cheek.

**Exact directional lip bound, not a circular approximation using A.** At an end point (A cos u,B sin u), the ball centre's wall projection lies at inward normal distance λ. The opposite reflected lip point cannot be nearer, so the projection has the same z sign as the point. This gives λ≤B²√((cos u/A)²+(sin u/B)²); at the axial vertex the same limit follows by comparison to adjacent points (radius of curvature). The adverse in-plane x-axis displacement in wall coordinates is ≤(B²/A)|cos u|. For q=h/b, j=|cos u|, the +x component of the ball normal is at least

sinα √(1−q²+q² sin²α j²) − q cos²α j.

At α=45° and q<1 it decreases to j=1. Straight z-parallel-normal slot sides give no adverse in-plane axial component. The effective angle above the perpendicular-to-x plane is therefore ≥45°−asin[(h/b)cos45°]. At b=2.70 that gives **.18422 N/N forward after 1° normal debit and μ=.23**. At b=2.60/2.90 it gives .16948/.21034. The gross bound using only the inradius would give a smaller angle, 15° for b=2.70. A b>1.85091 uniform inside rolling radius suffices on the *specified end geometry*. A bore's deeply recessed convex-facing lip is not assumed unreachable just from nominal radius: contact on the concave cylindrical bore interior is impossible for a larger solid ball by local curvature; on an interior straight slot side it would penetrate the opposite side at the same x,y. The width and intact stock on both sides are essential. For a locally squared-off, broken, enlarged or chamfered mouth recompute the profile bound; the tolerance budget is not permission to delete rim stock.

A union of balls maintains this bound at exposed contacts if **every** contact permits such an interior ball, including a rounded stem end. Convex sums of simultaneous normals preserve positive work. Thin exposed connector tabs or pin-sized wires reaching the port defeat this argument. No result for the ends of the *whole finite cheek*, registry serrations, back of a housing, screw aperture or thin probe. Those need actual +x draft or physical exclusion. A pinch-admissible mouth isn't a pinch-preventing guide.

## Closing the load budgets without equal shares

Let W be the known boundary wrench on the tail (gravity, feet, chain, inertia if bounded), with |force|≤B, adverse x force≤Bx, and moment about C≤K. Suppose every reachable fixed wall/seat has forward axial coefficient ≥g>0 including uncertainty, and its contact lever arm about C≤l. Let P be an independent bound on the adverse **net x** reaction from pin to tail. Then wall normal sum U≤(Bx+P)/g. Feed F≤B+H U and M≤K+l H U into the preceding pin bound. These inequalities include simultaneous wall, throat, tip and shank forces. They neither infer a small normal from small tension nor require a prescribed tail pose. For the pure port use g=.1842, but a shallower registry face can govern. A negative or unclassified fixed contact requires a separate force/moment budget.

P can come from the combined free body of an actually force-limited x carrier, all x shunt loads and its pin. A locked hard stop, cam dead centre, side loading of a guide or the **fixed** port rubbing directly on the pin can bypass the series sensor. Either exclude those under load, independently bound/instrument those bypasses, or measure the complete pin-support wrench, not motor current. The common +x virtual translation of carrier/pin and tail cancels internal pin forces only with those external paths included. Q31's five free feet give a whole-chain static gravity budget, not a local reaction or a bound on motor squeeze. No installed numerical P or W established here.

**Two-sided guide drag.** No diametral contact is counted as a simple one-sided bearing by default. Conditional rigid-shaft test valid for any number of simultaneous journal/rim contacts: all relevant stock stays outside an actual straight cylinder of *minimum* radius h₋ about the nominal relieved pin axis, including both journals, their lead-ins and fixed ports. Let every contacting shaft generator's transverse offset be ≤D and shaft inclination ≤β. Take a virtual rigid centring motion: at generator s of offset d, velocity −d+Y tanβ(d·e), with e the tilt azimuth and angular speed tanβ. Its derivative along the shaft is the corresponding rotation; its axial speed at any point of radial drive eccentricity e₀ is ≤e₀ tanβ. At contact point s−p n (n into pin), the exterior-cylinder inequality gives −n⊥·d≥(h₋²−D²−p²)/(2p). Rotation of the p offset has no normal work. Thus guide work per unit normal is at least

gJ=(h₋²−D²−p²)/(2p)−D tanβ−μ(D secβ+p tanβ).

This holds even on a port rim or partly exited journal; the far race is not assumed present. Let J be total guide normal, W₀ bound adverse virtual work of all non-guide/non-axial-drive forces on the pin/carriage, and Tdrive the axial drive force. For the eye use W₀,eye≤H T(Deye secβ+p tanβ), Deye the loaded offset at its generator(s); add other drive linkage reactions and friction/preload separately. Then gJ J ≤ W₀ + e₀ tanβ Tdrive. With axial parasitics ≤A₀ and k=sinβ+μ, no static balance at positive force above

**[A₀ + k(T + W₀/gJ)] / [1 − k e₀ tanβ/gJ]**, provided gJ>0 and the denominator is positive.

This is a sufficient start-force ceiling, not an assumption that force sharing holds or that a stall limit commands that force. Example only: h₋=1.35, D=.18, β=.5° gives gJ=.04556 mm and e₀<21.87 mm; at 1° e₀<9.60 mm. Here 1.35 is a *hypothetical minimum*, not inferred from the maximum working port. At D=.35, β=4° the certificate fails. One 2.50 long race with .10 radial play alone admits roughly 4.4° cocking (4.57° conservative upper bound from tanβ≤2c/L); it does not establish the small-tilt example. Misaligned bores, bending, preload, excess drag, an uncovered rear cap or an offset actuator couple can invalidate this calculation. Rigid contact certificates do not directly apply to a bent spine. Verify loaded alignment/deformation or re-establish the inequalities including it.

## Stress and the unsupported stroke

With illustrative isotropic steel E=205 GPa, ν=.30 for both parts, the script solves the two Hertz elliptic integrals from the *relative principal curvatures*. N is at a **single** contact.

| 50 N contact | equivalent curvatures, mm⁻¹ | patch semiaxes, mm | peak GPa |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Inner equator / aligned shank | .3652, .3704 | .0972, .0963 | 2.55 |
| Partial 36° witness / cap | .3980, 1.1704 | .1108, .0542 | 3.98 |
| Outer equator / cap geometry | .9299, 1.1704 | .0737, .0632 | 5.12 |

The last row is a local worst-curvature screen, not a reach assertion. Patch and pressure scale as N^(1/3) at fixed curvatures. A sharp port/journal edge is not a Hertz sphere; it needs an actual fillet and strength check. No hardness, yield, wear or life evidence. At first loss of elliptic curvature or a merging patch don't extrapolate a small Hertz ellipse.

Pin root, Ø2.50, illustrative 50 N transverse load: simply supported at race centres ±12.90 with a central point load gives 210 MPa bending (support reactions follow from equilibrium for **that position only**); even perfect fixity at the near face y=11.65 after far-support loss gives 380 MPa at its root. The eye stem's single Ø5.4 section at 50 N has 2.18 MPa average shear, 16.17 MPa pure bending at 5 mm eccentricity; add actual curved-ring stress, throat stress concentration, net section and fatigue. No 50/50 load allocation across the two ring branches.

**Counterexample to a journal-only friction model.** At x=15 with pull on an aligned tail at Cx=16.05, take nose apex −11.60: it has completely left the far race at −11.65 but the forward port edge x=16.35,y=−10.32 still sees pin shank (cap centre −10.35). With perfect near-face cantilever fixity, transverse load F at y=0, L=11.65 and rim overhang Hf=21.97, Euler–Bernoulli δrim=F L²(3Hf−L)/(6EI). Only **32.03 N** consumes the nominal .10 radial stock; 50 N would demand .156 mm without extra support. Thus a port reaction must be allowed in this nominal example, not silently dropped. This is a first-contact screen, not the post-contact force or proof of a jam. Real guide compliance, tip angle, clearance, edge break, off-centre loads and an actual drive change the number.

**Disposition:** a finite wrench-conditional proof replaces the claim that a flared mouth forces a single contact. It is not yet a motor/strength specification. The actual take-up measurement, post-unload tail wrench, fixed port shunts, tip and race deformation, stem/terminal reach and sixth registry carrier are open. The nominal parked tip doesn't demonstrate ejection or Q12. No physical validation or candidate selection.
