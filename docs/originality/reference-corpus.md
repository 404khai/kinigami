# Originality reference corpus

> **Status: fixed reference corpus for the Q61 originality procedure.** It is an input to originality review ([design guidance §4.1](../design-guidance.md)), not a requirement, criterion, or design selection. It changes only by a new decision record.

Corpus access date: **2026-11-04** (all sources below were located by web search on that date).

## Limits of this corpus

- **It is not exhaustive.** It lists prominent, credibly published, or widely known systems found in one search pass. Systems not listed may be closer to a Kin candidate than anything listed.
- **It makes no claim of absolute originality.** Passing a comparison against this corpus means "no resemblance was found to these entries on these five descriptors". It does not mean the design is novel in the world.
- **Descriptors are summaries, not specifications.** They were written from the cited work and general knowledge of each system. Search tools returned bibliographic records and abstracts. Full texts were not read for every entry. A reviewer who relies on a descriptor for a close call must check it against the primary source.
- **Fictional entries have no engineering source.** Their descriptors describe on-screen or on-page appearance and behaviour. Secondary sources are cited and labelled as such. The work itself was not re-viewed or re-read for this corpus.
- **"Verified" means the bibliographic record was found.** The title, authors, venue, and year (and a DOI where given) were returned by search. It does not mean the claims in the work were checked. Entries whose record could not be confirmed are under [Unverified](#unverified).
- **Where a DOI is not listed, none was confirmed.** The URL given is the confirmed record.

## Descriptor scheme

Each entry has exactly five descriptors:

1. **Silhouette/geometry**: body shape and proportions.
2. **Coupler/connection**: how units attach.
3. **Actuation/locomotion**: how units move.
4. **Assembly/reconfiguration**: how structures form or change.
5. **Visual identity**: the look a viewer would remember.

## B. Other fictional swarm and nanite robots

All four cite one secondary source: [Wikipedia, "Nanotechnology in fiction"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology_in_fiction), accessed 2026-11-04. Primary works were not re-checked.

### B1. Replicators (Stargate SG-1)
1. Silhouette/geometry: small insect-like block units of identical shape.
2. Coupler/connection: units interlock into larger bodies; no mechanism shown.
3. Actuation/locomotion: individual units scuttle as swarms.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: swarms self-replicate and assemble into larger machines and humanoid forms.
5. Visual identity: grey-silver blocky "bug" units forming flowing masses.

### B2. Prey swarm (Michael Crichton, 2002 novel)
1. Silhouette/geometry: molecule-scale particles seen only as a cloud.
2. Coupler/connection: none described mechanically.
3. Actuation/locomotion: the cloud flies and drifts as a unit.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: the cloud forms and holds shapes, including human likenesses, and evolves.
5. Visual identity: shimmering, dust-like or smoke-like airborne cloud.

### B3. Borg nanoprobes / Star Trek nanites
1. Silhouette/geometry: microscopic, not individually visible.
2. Coupler/connection: none (injected, bloodstream-borne).
3. Actuation/locomotion: carried in the body; no visible locomotion.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: remodel tissue and technology (assimilation).
5. Visual identity: sub-visible. Effect shows as tissue and surface change.

### B4. T-1000 (Terminator 2)
1. Silhouette/geometry: continuous liquid-metal body, no discrete units.
2. Coupler/connection: none (continuous material).
3. Actuation/locomotion: flows and reshapes.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: mimics people and objects; reforms after damage.
5. Visual identity: polished mercury-like chrome liquid.

## C. Published modular, self-reconfigurable, and swarm robots

All DOIs were returned by web search on 2026-11-04. Access date for every source in this section: **2026-11-04**.

### C1. M-TRAN I
Murata et al., "M-TRAN: Self-reconfigurable modular robotic system", *IEEE/ASME Trans. Mechatronics* 7(4):431–441, 2002. DOI [10.1109/TMECH.2002.806220](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMECH.2002.806220).
1. Silhouette/geometry: two cuboid-and-semicylinder halves joined by a link, giving a box-like module of roughly 65 mm.
2. Coupler/connection: permanent-magnet connection surfaces on each half, with polarity set by the module.
3. Actuation/locomotion: two rotary joints per module; legged or snake gaits from chained modules.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: hybrid chain-lattice reconfiguration; modules re-form between legged and other configurations.
5. Visual identity: blue and red box-halves connected in a sculptural zig-zag.

### C2. M-TRAN II
Kamimura et al., "Automatic locomotion design and experiments for a modular robotic system", *IEEE/ASME Trans. Mechatronics* 10(3):314–325, 2005. Record: [bib-bvb catalogue entry](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_28107551). DOI not confirmed.
1. Silhouette/geometry: same two-half cuboid body as M-TRAN I, with a revised, more compact build.
2. Coupler/connection: permanent-magnet surfaces, as M-TRAN I.
3. Actuation/locomotion: two rotary joints per module; locomotion patterns are generated automatically (the paper's subject).
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: chain and lattice configurations with an automatic gait generator.
5. Visual identity: as M-TRAN I.

### C3. M-TRAN III
Kurokawa et al., "Distributed Self-Reconfiguration of M-TRAN III Modular Robotic System", *Int. J. Robotics Research* 27(3–4):373–386, 2008. DOI [10.1177/0278364907085560](https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364907085560).
1. Silhouette/geometry: two-half cuboid body, refined from M-TRAN II.
2. Coupler/connection: faster, more rigid connection mechanism than the earlier generations (per the paper's abstract). Details not checked here.
3. Actuation/locomotion: two rotary joints per module; distributed controllers.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: distributed self-reconfiguration, single-master synchronous or parallel asynchronous control.
5. Visual identity: as M-TRAN I.

### C4. ATRON
Jorgensen, Ostergaard, Lund, "Modular ATRON: modules for a self-reconfigurable robot", *IROS 2004*. DOI [10.1109/IROS.2004.1389702](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2004.1389702).
1. Silhouette/geometry: sphere made of two hemispheres, one rotating relative to the other.
2. Coupler/connection: eight connectors on the sphere, hook-based, in a lattice arrangement.
3. Actuation/locomotion: a single rotation axis through the sphere's equator; locomotion arises from neighbouring modules.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: lattice-based, with each module self-contained (power, processing, sensing).
5. Visual identity: spherical shells split at the equator with connector pockets.

### C5. SMORES-EP
Daudelin et al., "An integrated system for perception-driven autonomy with modular robots", *Science Robotics* 3(23):eaat4983, 2018. DOI [10.1126/scirobotics.aat4983](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.aat4983). Parallel self-assembly: Liu, Lin, Kim, Yim, *Autonomous Robots*, 2023, DOI [10.1007/s10514-022-10078-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-022-10078-1). The original SMORES-EP hardware paper was not located.
1. Silhouette/geometry: small cubic body with four faces carrying connectors and a wheel-like rotating face.
2. Coupler/connection: electro-permanent magnets on connector faces.
3. Actuation/locomotion: wheel-like faces and a tilt joint; modules drive individually or in reconfigured assemblies.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: lattice-chain hybrid; docking and parallel self-assembly.
5. Visual identity: small cubes with circular faces and wheel discs.

### C6. Roombots
Spröwitz et al., "Roombots: A hardware perspective on 3D self-reconfiguration and locomotion with a homogeneous modular robot", *Robotics and Autonomous Systems* 62(7):1016–1033, 2014. DOI [10.1016/j.robot.2013.08.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.robot.2013.08.011).
1. Silhouette/geometry: two fused spheres, a peanut shape.
2. Coupler/connection: active mechanical connectors on the sphere surfaces, with ten connection points on a body of this type.
3. Actuation/locomotion: three rotational degrees of freedom per module; free in-place and chain motion.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: 3D self-reconfiguration; aimed at adaptive furniture.
5. Visual identity: rounded paired spheres, furniture-oriented.

### C7. M-Blocks
Romanishin, Gilpin, Rus, "M-Blocks: Momentum-driven, magnetic modular robots", *IROS 2013*. DOI [10.1109/IROS.2013.6696971](https://doi.org/10.1109/IROS.2013.6696971).
1. Silhouette/geometry: 50 mm cubes with chamfered edges.
2. Coupler/connection: permanent magnets on faces and edges; no actuated coupler.
3. Actuation/locomotion: an internal flywheel is braked suddenly to pivot the cube; no external moving parts.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: cubes pivot about each other on a lattice and self-assemble.
5. Visual identity: plain cubes with no visible actuators.

### C8. 3D M-Blocks
Romanishin, Gilpin, Claici, Rus, "3D M-Blocks: Self-reconfiguring robots capable of locomotion via pivoting in three dimensions", *ICRA 2015*. DOI [10.1109/ICRA.2015.7139450](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA.2015.7139450).
1. Silhouette/geometry: 50 mm cube, as M-Blocks.
2. Coupler/connection: permanent magnets on faces and edges.
3. Actuation/locomotion: a flywheel with a mechanism that transforms torque into pivoting about several axes.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: independent rolling, plus lattice motion relative to other modules, in three dimensions.
5. Visual identity: as M-Blocks.

### C9. Kilobot
Rubenstein, Ahler, Nagpal, "Kilobot: A low cost scalable robot system for collective behaviors", *ICRA 2012*. DOI [10.1109/ICRA.2012.6224638](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICRA.2012.6224638).
1. Silhouette/geometry: a flat circuit board with a coin-cell-sized profile, standing on three rigid legs.
2. Coupler/connection: none (non-docking swarm robot).
3. Actuation/locomotion: two vibration motors; slip-stick motion on legs.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: no physical assembly. Collectives form patterns by local rules and infrared messaging.
5. Visual identity: a bare circuit board on three legs, with a single RGB LED.

### C10. Claytronics catoms
Goldstein, Campbell, Mowry, "Programmable Matter", *IEEE Computer* 2005. DOI [10.1109/MC.2005.198](https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2005.198).
1. Silhouette/geometry: the envisioned catom is a small sphere or cylinder; hardware prototypes vary.
2. Coupler/connection: planned electrostatic or magnetic adhesion between neighbours.
3. Actuation/locomotion: catoms move by rolling around each other.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: ensembles reproduce shapes ("physical artifacts"); each catom has a CPU, network device, display pixel, and sensors.
5. Visual identity: swarms of small units acting as a 3D display.

### C11. Molecubes
Zykov, Mytilinaios, Adams, Lipson, "Self-reproducing machines", *Nature* 435:163–164, 2005. DOI [10.1038/435163a](https://doi.org/10.1038/435163a).
1. Silhouette/geometry: a cube with one diagonal axis of rotation, producing a bent-cube shape.
2. Coupler/connection: electromagnet-based face connectors.
3. Actuation/locomotion: a single rotation axis about the cube's body diagonal.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: a tower of cubes builds a copy of itself from fed-in cubes (self-reproduction).
5. Visual identity: tilted, half-rotated cubes in stacked towers.

### C12. PolyBot
Yim, Duff, Roufas, "PolyBot: A Modular Reconfigurable Robot", *ICRA 2000*, pp. 514–520. Record: [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/YimDR00/bibtex). DOI not confirmed.
1. Silhouette/geometry: a small cube-like module with one rotation axis and a joint, about 5 cm.
2. Coupler/connection: genderless connector plates, with a latching system on each face.
3. Actuation/locomotion: one rotational degree of freedom per module; snake, loop, spider, and other gaits.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: chain-style, shown switching between two locomotion modes by reconfiguring.
5. Visual identity: cube modules linked into long snake-like chains.

### C13. SuperBot
Salemi, Moll, Shen, "SUPERBOT: A Deployable, Multi-Functional, and Modular Self-Reconfigurable Robotic System", *IROS 2006*. Record: [USC/ISI](https://robots.isi.edu/prl/b2hd-salemi2006superbot.html). DOI not confirmed.
1. Silhouette/geometry: modules built from three joined segments, with cuboid proportions.
2. Coupler/connection: docking connectors on several faces.
3. Actuation/locomotion: three rotational degrees of freedom per module; multimode locomotion.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: hybrid chain and lattice reconfiguration, aimed at deployable use.
5. Visual identity: boxy articulated modules joined into multi-limbed bodies.

### C14. CKBot
Project and publication page: [ModLab, University of Pennsylvania](https://www.modlabupenn.org/ckbot/). DOI not confirmed. A specific paper citation for the module design was not located.
1. Silhouette/geometry: roughly 6 cm cubic module made from laser-cut ABS.
2. Coupler/connection: connectors on faces, with manual reconfiguration in the original design.
3. Actuation/locomotion: a hobby-servo-based single rotational joint per module.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: chain-style and manually reconfigurable.
5. Visual identity: laser-cut plastic cubes in chains.

### C15. Soldercubes
Neubert, Lipson, "Soldercubes: a self-soldering self-reconfiguring modular robot system", *Autonomous Robots* 40(1):139–158, 2016. DOI [10.1007/s10514-015-9441-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10514-015-9441-4).
1. Silhouette/geometry: lightweight cubic module.
2. Coupler/connection: a 2 g soldering connector with no moving parts; melts solder to join.
3. Actuation/locomotion: modules are reconfigured by the system's joints and by melt/solidify cycles.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: self-soldering connection makes bonds strong and electrically continuous.
5. Visual identity: small PCB-like cubes with solder pads.

### C16. FreeBOT
Liang, Luo, Li, Qian, Lam, "FreeBOT: A Freeform Modular Self-reconfigurable Robot with Arbitrary Connection Point – Design and Implementation", *IROS 2020*, pp. 6506–6513. PDF: [freeformrobotics.org](https://freeformrobotics.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/IROS20_0857_FI.pdf). DOI not confirmed.
1. Silhouette/geometry: a sphere with a ferromagnetic shell.
2. Coupler/connection: an internal magnet attaches to any point on a neighbour's ferromagnetic shell.
3. Actuation/locomotion: two motors; single-module motion, connector management, and reconfiguration.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: freeform; connection at arbitrary points.
5. Visual identity: smooth metallic balls in clumps and chains.

### C17. HyMod
Parrott, Dodd, Gross, "HyMod: A 3-DOF hybrid mobile and self-reconfigurable modular robot and its extensions", *DARS 2016*. DOI [10.1007/978-3-319-73008-0_28](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-73008-0_28).
1. Silhouette/geometry: a spherical joint unit in a cubic-lattice envelope.
2. Coupler/connection: HiGen, a high-speed genderless connector; passive HiGen interfaces for extension modules.
3. Actuation/locomotion: three degrees of freedom; independent movement with free in-place rotation.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: chain, lattice, and mobile hybrid; groups into cubic lattice structures.
5. Visual identity: cube-lattice units with a ball joint core.

### C18. Telecube
Suh, Homans, Yim, "Telecubes: Mechanical design of a module for self-reconfigurable robotics", *ICRA 2002*. Record: [researchr](https://researchr.org/publication/SuhHY02). DOI not confirmed.
1. Silhouette/geometry: cube whose six faces extend and retract.
2. Coupler/connection: switching permanent magnets on the faces.
3. Actuation/locomotion: six prismatic degrees of freedom; each side extends to more than twice its length.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: lattice; neighbours attach and detach magnetically.
5. Visual identity: a cube with telescoping arms.

### C19. Crystalline robots
Rus, Vona, "Crystalline Robots: Self-Reconfiguration with Compressible Unit Modules", *Autonomous Robots* 10(1):107–124, 2001. DOI [10.1023/A:1026504804984](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1026504804984).
1. Silhouette/geometry: square (2D) units that expand and contract.
2. Coupler/connection: latches between neighbouring units.
3. Actuation/locomotion: units expand and contract; the lattice shifts by their motion.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: lattice-based shape metamorphosis, in O(n²) time per the paper.
5. Visual identity: a grid of expanding and contracting squares.

### C20. PolyPod
No primary paper was located in this pass. Secondary: [MIT Media Lab page, "Polypod – Modular Reconfigurable Robots"](https://www.mit.edu/people/manoli/polypod/polypod.html); [ETHW oral history, Mark Yim](https://ethw.org/Oral-History:Mark_Yim). Designed at Stanford around 1993 for locomotion research, with two module types.
1. Silhouette/geometry: segment-and-node (two module types) forming a linkage.
2. Coupler/connection: not verified.
3. Actuation/locomotion: statically stable gaits explored from 1994.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: chain-style reconfiguration.
5. Visual identity: a chain of alternating joint and segment modules.

> C20 is retained here because the system is in the task list and the secondary pages confirm it exists. Its primary source is **unverified** (see below).

### C21. Swarm-bots / s-bot
Mondada et al., "SWARM-BOT: A new concept of robust all-terrain mobile robotic system", *Autonomous Robots* 17(2–3):193–221, 2004. DOI [10.1023/B:AURO.0000033972.50769.1c](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:AURO.0000033972.50769.1c).
1. Silhouette/geometry: small round wheeled body with a gripper and a turret.
2. Coupler/connection: a gripper and a ring on the body allow s-bots to grasp each other.
3. Actuation/locomotion: wheels and tracks; the group moves as a connected entity.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: swarm of mobile robots physically connect for all-terrain tasks.
5. Visual identity: cylindrical wheeled robot with a ring and gripper.

### C22. Sambot
Wei, Chen, Tan, Wang, "Sambot: A self-assembly modular robot system", *IEEE/ASME Trans. Mechatronics* 16(4):745–757, 2011. DOI [10.1109/TMECH.2010.2085009](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMECH.2010.2085009).
1. Silhouette/geometry: a cuboid body with one arm-like active docking face and several passive faces.
2. Coupler/connection: docking mechanism with one active and several passive docking surfaces.
3. Actuation/locomotion: each module is mobile on its own (wheels or tracks) and has a joint.
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: self-assembly into structures; shares properties of swarm and self-reconfigurable robots.
5. Visual identity: boxy mobile modules with a hinged docking arm.

### C23. Blinky Blocks
Kirby, Ashley-Rollman, Goldstein, "Blinky Blocks: a physical ensemble programming platform", *CHI 2011 Extended Abstracts*, pp. 1111–1116. DOI [10.1145/1979742.1979712](https://doi.org/10.1145/1979742.1979712).
1. Silhouette/geometry: small cubes with light-diffusing faces.
2. Coupler/connection: magnetic stacking and data links on faces.
3. Actuation/locomotion: none (no self-locomotion).
4. Assembly/reconfiguration: user-assembled, with distributed programs across the ensemble.
5. Visual identity: translucent glowing coloured cubes.

## D. Commercial modular kits
